Quiet breaks at school: ideas for teachers

A short pause can help a class settle. These are our own suggestions, not rules or research, and you know your class best.

When a break might help

  • After a break time, when the class comes in lively.
  • Before a test, for a minute, if the class is nervous.
  • After a hard piece of work.
  • When one child is struggling to settle and you would like to offer something quiet.

How to run one

  1. Say what is going to happen and how long it will take: "We will do one minute of breathing."
  2. Keep the first break short, one to two minutes.
  3. Let a child sit out if they would rather; do not make it a test.
  4. End with a clear signal and go straight on with the lesson.

Using these toys

The breathing circle works well on a projector or a shared screen for everyone. Bubble wrap and sand drawing suit one child at a quiet table or a calm corner. None of them is scored, so there is nothing to win or lose.

Keeping it fair

Offer the same chance to everyone, and keep the toys for calming rather than as a reward or a punishment. If many children cannot settle, think about the lesson, the room and the timetable as well as the break.
