Calm Toys

When a toy is not enough: talking to someone

Calm toys are for ordinary hard moments: waiting, nerves, a long day. They are not a treatment for anything. This page is general and is not medical advice.

Signs it may be time to talk to someone

  • A child is very upset often, or for a long time.
  • Their sleep, eating or school life has changed a lot.
  • They say something that worries you.
  • You find you are worried about it yourself, more than for a day or two.

Who you can talk to

  • A family doctor or health worker.
  • A teacher or school counsellor.
  • A trusted relative or friend, who may help you think it through.

What helps the conversation

  1. Write down what you have seen: when it happens and for how long.
  2. Say what you have tried, including these toys.
  3. Ask what they would suggest.

If someone may be in danger

If you think a person is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services or a local helpline straight away. We cannot give help or advice, and this website is not a service to contact in an emergency.

A kind note

Asking for help is a normal, sensible thing for parents, teachers and children to do.
