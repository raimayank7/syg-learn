Mind maps for revision: how to use them well

Many students like to turn a chapter into a map. Here are our own suggestions for using one without wasting time. People learn differently, so use what suits you.

One chapter, one map

Give each chapter or topic its own map. A map that tries to hold a whole subject becomes too crowded to help.

Try from memory first

  1. Close your book and draw the map from memory, as much as you can.
  2. Open the book and add what you missed in a different colour or by adding new ideas.
  3. Look at the gaps. Those are the parts to study again.
  4. A few days later, draw the map again from memory and see how much more you remember.

Questions on branches

Instead of only writing topic words, try turning a branch into a question: "Why does it happen?" with the answer as the child idea. Your teacher may ask questions in that form.

A simple plan

DayWhat to do
Day 1Read the chapter and draw the map
Day 2Draw it again from memory, add the gaps
Day 5Draw it from memory once more
Before the testLook at the map and say each branch aloud

The days are only an example; change them to fit your timetable.

Print it

The Print button prints just the map, which you can stick on a wall or keep in a folder.
