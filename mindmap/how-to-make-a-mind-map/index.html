How to make a mind map in five steps

A mind map is a picture of how ideas connect. One idea sits in the middle and branches spread out from it. These steps are our own suggestions.

The five steps

  1. Write the topic in the middle. Keep it short: two or three words. If you cannot say it in a few words, the topic is probably too big.
  2. Add the main branches. These are the big parts of the topic. Try for three to seven. Fewer than three and you probably do not need a map; many more and it becomes hard to see.
  3. Add details to each branch. One short phrase per idea, not a sentence. Single words and short phrases are easier to scan later.
  4. Tidy the layout. Move ideas so related ones sit close together. On this site the Auto-arrange button spaces the whole map for you.
  5. Read it aloud. Start at the middle and follow each branch. If a branch does not make sense when you say it, rename it or move it.

A tiny worked example

Topic: "Day trip". Main branches: Where, Who, Pack, Plan. Under Pack: Water, Snacks, Bag. That is one centre, four branches and three details: eight ideas on one page. The starter map called "trip" on the home page has this shape, and you can change it.

Keep ideas short

Each idea box on this site holds up to 40 characters, which is enough for a short phrase. If you need more, add a child idea instead of making one long box.

When to stop

Stop when you can see the whole subject on one page. A map is a tool for thinking, not a work of art, so it does not need to be perfect before you use it.

The advice on this page is our own. It is not a rule from a school, a board or a publisher, and this site is independent and is not connected with any of them. Your map stays in your own browser: nothing you write is sent to us.
