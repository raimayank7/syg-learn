The maths behind magic tricks: why they always work

A number trick is not magic. It is a rule about numbers that is always true, hidden inside a story. Once you see the rule, you can make up tricks of your own. This is our own explanation.

Letters stand for numbers

In the trick Think of a Number, call the secret number n. Doubling gives 2n, adding 10 gives 2n + 10, halving gives n + 5, and taking away n leaves 5. The n disappears, so any number gives the same answer.

Multiples of nine

Many tricks use the number nine. A number and its digits added together leave the same remainder when divided by nine. That is why Always Nine and The Missing Digit work: they take away or compare numbers that differ by a multiple of nine, and the digits of a multiple of nine always add up to a multiple of nine.

A product that hides

The number 1001 is 7 x 11 x 13. A three-digit number written twice is that number times 1001, so dividing by 7, 11 and 13 takes you back. That is the 1001 trick.

Try making your own

  • Choose a rule that always gives one answer, such as add a number then take it away.
  • Wrap it in a story.
  • Test it on twenty numbers before showing anyone.
