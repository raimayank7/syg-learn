How the games work

Each game is a short round of eight questions on one idea. A question is shown with big pictures to tap. A right answer is praised and the answer said back. A wrong tap makes that picture fade, and the child tries again. After two wrong taps the right picture glows, so nobody is stuck. At the end the child earns one to three stars for answers right the first time.

Every game has three levels, so the same game can serve a two-year-old at level 1 and a six-year-old at level 3. Questions are new each time.

Best played together

Small children learn most from people. These games work best when a grown-up sits close, reads the question, and talks about what the child sees. Each game page has tips for grown-ups and a way to play the same idea without a screen.

Made with children in mind

  • No advertisements on the game pages, no login, no chat and no links to other players.
  • No flashing prizes or timers, and each round ends by itself.

Common questions

Are the games free?

Yes. Every game is free, with no login, no purchase and nothing to install.

Are there advertisements in the games?

No. The game pages carry no advertisements. Some guide pages for parents may carry them.

What age are the games for?

They are made for children from about 2 to 7, with a grown-up. Each game lists the age it suits best, and each has three levels.

Do the games work on a phone?

Yes. They are made for touch, with big buttons, and also work with a mouse or a keyboard.

Is my child's data collected?

No. Nothing about your child is sent to us. The best stars for each game are kept in your own browser only.

How long should my child play?

A round takes a few minutes. WHO advises no more than one hour of screen time a day for ages 2 to 4, and none for children under 2. See our page on screen time.
