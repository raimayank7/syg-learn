Big and Small

Pick the bigger or smaller one, the biggest or smallest, the longest or the shortest. Age 2 to 5

Loading the game...

How to play

  1. Read the question: "Tap the bigger one".
  2. Look at the pictures and tap your choice.
  3. The right answer is praised and the next one comes.
  4. A wrong tap fades that picture so the child can pick again.

A round has eight questions and no clock. The best stars for each level are kept on this device only.

Levels

  • Level 1: Two circles: bigger or smaller.
  • Level 2: Three pictures: biggest or smallest.
  • Level 3: Four bars: longest or shortest.

Start at level 1. Move up when most answers are right the first time.

What this game practises

Comparing is the root of measuring. A child who can say "this one is bigger" is ready later to say how much bigger, and then to use a ruler. The game moves from two things to three and then to four, because choosing from several needs the child to hold the comparison in mind. The words bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest, longest and shortest are the point of the game.

Good to know

Words for size are used all day ("a big spoon", "a small bit") yet many children use big and little for everything. Pairs of opposites are learned best side by side, so the game always shows both ends of the range on screen at once.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Use the words in daily life: the biggest apple, the smallest shoe.
  • Line objects up from smallest to biggest and let the child say the order.
  • If the child taps at random, stop and compare two real things first.
  • Level 3 uses bars, so ask "which one would take longest to walk along?"

Play it without a screen

Line up shoes from the whole family and sort them. Compare spoons, cups and bowls. Make a long snake and a short snake from play dough. Ask the child to bring "the longest stick" from the garden and "the smallest stone".

A word of care

The size of the pictures depends on the screen. On a very small phone the difference between level 1 circles is still clear, but in level 2 the smallest picture may look tiny. A bigger screen helps.
