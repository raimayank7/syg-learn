Games for 4-year-olds

Learning games for 4-year-olds.

Four-year-olds love rules and fairness, and are beginning to read signs and their own names. They can count to ten, tell colours and shapes, and often start asking about letters. This is a good age for number and letter games, with a grown-up close by to read.

Games that suit this age

  • Count the ThingsCount apples, stars, fish and more, then tap the right number.Age 3 to 6
  • Number FriendsFind the number named, then say what comes before, after or between.Age 3 to 6
  • Letter HuntFind a letter, big or small, and match a small letter with its big partner.Age 3 to 6
  • First LetterLook at a picture, say its name and find the letter it starts with.Age 4 to 6
  • What Comes NextA row of coloured dots repeats. Which colour comes next?Age 3 to 6
  • Memory MatchTurn over two cards at a time and find the matching pairs.Age 3 to 7
  • Odd One OutThree pictures belong together and one does not. Which one is different?Age 3 to 6

How to play well at this age

  • Learn the letters of the child's own name first.
  • Play Memory Match with a grown-up and take turns.
  • Move to level 2 once most answers are right the first time.
  • Ask the child to teach you the game. Explaining is a good way to learn.

What to expect

A four-year-old can often count up to ten things and knows some letters, usually the ones in their own name. Many four-year-olds still confuse some numbers and letters. Short sessions of two or three rounds, three or four times a week, are plenty.

Screen time at this age

Four-year-olds sometimes confuse mirror-image letters such as b and d, or write backwards. This is common and usually settles with time. If it troubles you after the child has been at school for a while, ask the teacher.

A sample five-minute session

  1. Sit next to your child and say what you will play.
  2. Open Count the Things and choose level 1.
  3. Read each question aloud and let your child tap. Say the right answer out loud together.
  4. When a round ends, praise the trying, not only the stars.
  5. Stop, and do something with your hands or your feet: build, draw, hop, dance.
