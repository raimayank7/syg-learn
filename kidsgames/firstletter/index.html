First Letter

Look at a picture, say its name and find the letter it starts with. Age 4 to 6

Loading the game...

How to play

  1. Look at the picture and read the word under it.
  2. Say the word slowly and listen to the first sound.
  3. Tap the letter the word starts with.
  4. In level 3 a letter is named and the child picks, from four pictures, the one that starts with it.

A round has eight questions and no clock. The best stars for each level are kept on this device only.

Levels

  • Level 1: Picture and word, three letters to pick from.
  • Level 2: Picture and word, four letters to pick from.
  • Level 3: A letter is named; tap the picture that starts with it.

Start at level 1. Move up when most answers are right the first time.

What this game practises

Hearing the first sound in a word is called phonemic awareness, and it is a key step before reading. The game links the sound at the start of a word to the letter written for it. The words are simple and have a clear first sound, such as ball, cat and sun. X is left out because almost no easy word begins with it.

Good to know

Letter names and letter sounds are not the same thing, and they can pull against each other (the letter C sounds like K in cat). Pictures help a child separate the first sound from the whole word. Some words, such as orange and ice cream, start with a sound that is a vowel, which is harder than a consonant for many children.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Say the word slowly together and stretch the first sound: "mmmango".
  • Play it by sound only: "I spy with my little eye something that starts with mmm."
  • Do not worry if a child answers with a different word that starts with the same letter. That is a good sign.
  • Look for the same starting letter on signs and packets.

Play it without a screen

Play "I spy" at home, in the market or on a bus. Sort small toys into piles by their first letter. Find three things in the kitchen that start with the same letter as the child's name.

A word of care

Spoken English in India has many accents, and letter sounds differ a little from one place to another. Say the words in the way you normally say them.
