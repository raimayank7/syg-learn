Preschool Games

Twenty learning games without a screen

You do not need a screen to teach colours, shapes, counting and letters. Small children learn well from real objects, and many of the best games cost nothing. Here are twenty games using only what is at home. Pick a few and see which ones your child loves.

Colours and shapes

  • Colour hunt. Give the child a bag. Name a colour and let them collect things of that colour.
  • Sock sorting. Pair clean socks by colour and pattern. A real job, and good practice.
  • Shape walk. On a walk, look for circles (wheels), rectangles (doors) and triangles (roofs, signs).
  • Dough shapes. Roll play dough or atta dough and make a ball, a snake, a flat circle.
  • Rangoli dots. Make a simple pattern with coloured powder or rice and talk about the shapes.

Counting and numbers

  • Stair counting. Count each step going up and down.
  • Snack counting. Count out five grapes, or four biscuits, then ask how many are left after eating one.
  • Number hop. Draw numbers on the floor in chalk and hop to the one you call out.
  • Cup and coins. Put coins under a cup, show it for a moment and ask how many.
  • Door numbers. On a walk, find number 5 on a house, a bus or a shop.

Letters and words

  • Name puzzle. Write the child's name on paper, cut up the letters and put them back in order.
  • I spy. "I spy something that starts with mmm."
  • Letter trays. Draw a letter with a finger in a tray of rice, sand or flour.
  • Alphabet on the fridge. Magnets and a tin tray do very well.
  • Rhymes. Sing rhymes and stop before the last word so the child can finish.

Thinking and remembering

  • Kim's tray. Five things on a tray, cover them, take one away. Which one is gone?
  • Sort the shopping. Put tins in one place and vegetables in another.
  • Pattern beads. Thread beads in red, blue, red, blue.
  • Opposites game. Say "hot", the child says "cold".
  • Animal sounds. One person acts, others guess.

Keep it light

Play for a few minutes and stop when interest fades. Praise effort, not just answers. If a game is a struggle, leave it for another week. All of these have a matching game on this site (Colour Match, Shape Spotter, Count the Things and others), so you can use a game as a follow-up when a screen is the right choice.

Everyday moments that teach

  • Bath time: floating and sinking, full and empty cups, big and small bottles.
  • Meal time: counting rotis or grapes, naming colours on the plate, hot and cold.
  • Dressing: pairing socks, finding the big button, naming the colour of each piece.
  • Shopping: weighing fruit by hand (heavy and light), counting items into the bag.
  • Bedtime: a story, then the opposites game in the dark: "the opposite of night is...".

None of these needs a plan or a purchase. What counts is that an adult talks with the child about what they are doing, in whichever language the family speaks. Children who hear many words, in any language, usually find school learning easier later.
