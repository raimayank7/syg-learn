Shape Spotter

Tap the circle, square, triangle or rectangle, then count the corners of a shape. Age 3 to 6

Loading the game...

How to play

  1. Look at the four pictures.
  2. Tap the shape that is named in the question.
  3. In level 3 a shape is drawn big and the child picks how many corners it has: three, four, five or six.
  4. Wrong taps fade out so the child can try the others.

A round has eight questions and no clock. The best stars for each level are kept on this device only.

Levels

  • Level 1: Four shapes: circle, square, triangle, rectangle.
  • Level 2: Eight shapes: adds star, heart, oval and diamond.
  • Level 3: How many corners? Triangles up to hexagons.

Start at level 1. Move up when most answers are right the first time.

What this game practises

Naming flat shapes is early geometry. Level 1 is about telling shapes apart by their outline. Level 2 adds shapes that look alike, such as a circle and an oval, or a square and a diamond, so the child must look at edges and not just general roundness. Level 3 asks for counting the corners, which joins shape knowledge to counting.

Good to know

Squares and rectangles cause the most muddle, and a square turned on its corner (a diamond) puzzles many children for a long time because it looks so different. Seeing the same shape in many sizes and positions is the cure, and the game draws shapes at one size. Pair it with real objects: a plate, a window, a biscuit, a road sign.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Run a finger round the edge of the shape on the screen while saying "round and round" or "three straight sides".
  • In level 3, touch each corner as you count. Counting corners by touch is slower and surer than guessing.
  • Look for the same shapes in the room afterwards.
  • Take a break after a round. Shapes are easier when fresh.

Play it without a screen

Cut shapes out of old cards and let the child sort them. Make shapes with sticks, rope or play dough. Draw a big shape on the floor with chalk and walk along its edges. A shape walk outdoors, spotting circles (wheels, plates) and rectangles (doors, books), turns a normal day into a game.

A word of care

Some children draw a circle for every shape. That is fine at age three. Ask for the shape name only; drawing control comes later and is not tested by this game.
