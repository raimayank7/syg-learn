How to choose safe learning games for small children

A good game for a small child is simple, kind and honest. Here is what we look for when making games on this site, and what you can look for in any game or app.

What a good preschool game does

  • Asks one thing at a time, in a few words.
  • Has big buttons that work with a small hand, and no tiny close icons.
  • Is kind about mistakes: a wrong tap is not a loss, and the child can try again.
  • Ends by itself after a short round.
  • Works without reading, or reads the question aloud.
  • Does not show advertisements, offer purchases or ask for a login.

What to avoid

  • Games with timers that make a small child feel hurried.
  • Games that give prizes for playing longer, or send reminders.
  • Games that ask for a name, an age or photos.
  • Games with chat or links to other players.
  • Games with sound or flashing that is loud or sudden.

Our promises

On this site, the game pages have no advertisements, no login, no chat, no leaderboard and no tracking. Your child's best stars are stored in your own browser and are never sent to us. See the privacy policy for the details. We write all the questions and pictures ourselves.

Check your own settings too

  • Turn off auto-play of the next video.
  • Use the "guided" or "kids" mode of the phone if it has one.
  • Keep the phone out of reach of a very young child when you are not there.
  • Close other apps first so no advertisement appears in the middle of a game.

This advice is general guidance from a teaching point of view, not medical or legal advice. For laws on children's data, such as India's data protection rules, you should check the official government sources.

Questions to ask before you let a child play any game

  • Who made it, and can I find a way to contact them?
  • Does it show advertisements, and are they suitable for a small child?
  • Can my child buy anything inside it, by accident or on purpose?
  • Does it ask for personal details, a name, an age, a photo or contacts?
  • Can my child talk to strangers through it?
  • Does it work without a login, and without the internet once loaded?

If the answer to any of the middle questions is yes, think twice. A small child should not be left alone with such a game.

If your child already plays other games

Many children have already met games full of bright rewards and sounds. Our games are plainer, and some children find them dull at first. Give a few sessions and sit with the child; most settle into the calmer pace. Do not use a game as a reward or a punishment, since that makes screen time more important than it is.
