What Comes Next

A row of coloured dots repeats. Which colour comes next? Age 3 to 6

Loading the game...

How to play

  1. Look at the row of dots from the left.
  2. Say the colours aloud in order.
  3. Find the part that repeats.
  4. Tap the colour that comes next, at the question mark.

A round has eight questions and no clock. The best stars for each level are kept on this device only.

Levels

  • Level 1: Two colours taking turns (red, blue, red, blue...).
  • Level 2: Patterns of three: two of one colour, one of another.
  • Level 3: Three colours in a repeating order.

Start at level 1. Move up when most answers are right the first time.

What this game practises

Spotting a repeating pattern is the first step towards algebra and also helps with reading and music. The child must see the part that repeats (the unit) and carry it on. Levels move from two colours to patterns with doubles and then to three colours in a row.

Good to know

Reading the row aloud, "red blue red blue", turns a picture into a rhythm and makes the pattern easy to hear. Children who can clap a pattern often find it easy to see. Patterns are all around: stripes on clothes, a tabla beat, steps on a staircase, the days of the week.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Say the colours aloud together at a steady beat.
  • Clap or stamp the pattern: loud, soft, loud, soft.
  • Let the child make a pattern for you with beads or blocks and ask what comes next.
  • Do not rush level 3. Three colours in order is harder than it looks.

Play it without a screen

Thread beads or buttons in a pattern. Make a pattern with blocks, leaves and stones. Clap, tap and stamp patterns. Find patterns in a sari border, a rangoli, a tiled floor or a fence.

A word of care

Colours are used for the dots. A child who has difficulty telling some colours apart may find this game confusing. Use blocks of different shapes or sizes on paper instead.
