Screen time for young children

Parents often ask how much screen time is right for a small child. The World Health Organization (WHO) has given plain advice, and it is the advice we follow on this site. We read it on the WHO website on 13 November 2026. It comes from the guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under five, announced on 24 April 2019.

What WHO recommends

  • Babies under 1 year: screen time is not recommended.
  • 1-year-olds: sitting in front of a screen (TV, videos or computer games) is not recommended.
  • 2-year-olds: sitting screen time should be no more than one hour a day, and less is better.
  • 3 and 4-year-olds: no more than one hour a day, and less is better.
  • For all ages under five, when a child is sitting, reading and storytelling with a caregiver is encouraged. WHO also says that quality time spent with a caregiver on things without a screen, such as reading, storytelling, singing and puzzles, is very important for development.

WHO also asks that young children are not strapped into a pram or a high chair for more than an hour at a time, that children aged 1 to 4 get at least three hours a day of physical activity of any kind, and that they sleep well (11 to 14 hours for 1 and 2-year-olds, 10 to 13 hours for 3 and 4-year-olds, including naps). These are the numbers on the WHO page, and you should check the page for the latest wording.

What this means for the games on this site

  • The games are for children aged two and above. For a one-year-old, WHO says no screen time. Please do not use our games for babies.
  • For ages 2 to 4, the hour is the total for the whole day, with television and videos too, not an hour for each. A few short rounds of a game may be all you want.
  • Each of our rounds is eight questions and takes a few minutes. It ends on its own, and there is no auto-play or "next game" button.
  • Sit beside your child and talk. WHO says that sitting time with a person is better than sitting time alone.

Signs it is too much

  • The child cries or gets angry when the screen goes off, again and again.
  • The child is not sleeping well, or is tired in the day.
  • The child no longer wants to play outside or with other children.
  • The child asks for the screen at every meal or while waiting.

If any of these happen, cut the time down and give a warm, clear end ("two more, then we put it away"). Children cope with a clear rule better than with a fuzzy one.

Choosing well when you do use a screen

Choose games with no advertisements, no purchases, no chat with strangers and no tricks that keep a child playing. Games on this site have none of these. The game pages carry no advertisements, no login and no tracking, and results stay on the device.

The advice above is general guidance and is not medical advice. If you worry about your child's growth, sleep, speech or behaviour, talk to a doctor.

Source: World Health Organization, news release "To grow up healthy, children need to sit less and play more", 24 April 2019, and the publication "Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age".
