How to make good flashcards that actually help

A flashcard is only as good as its question. This is our own advice.

One fact on each card

If the back of a card is a paragraph you will not know whether you knew it. Split it into small cards with a short answer. "Three causes of the Revolt" is three cards, not one.

Ask a clear question

Weak cardBetter card
Photosynthesis | a long definitionWhat gas do plants take in for photosynthesis? | carbon dioxide
Verbs | list of 20Past tense of "go" | went
Mughals | many factsFirst battle of Panipat: who won? | (one name, from your textbook)

Check every answer in your own textbook or notes; the tool does not know whether your card is right.

Use your own words

Writing the card yourself is half the work of learning. Copying a classmate's deck skips that.

Cards that you keep missing

  • Make the card smaller or split it.
  • Add a clue to the front, such as the first letter.
  • Write a short memory hook on the back, such as a rhyme or picture.

How many cards

Start with 10 to 20 new cards a day at most. A big deck on day one is discouraging and most of it will be due at once.
