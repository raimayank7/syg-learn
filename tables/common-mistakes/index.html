The times table facts children get wrong most

Teachers see the same handful of facts missed again and again. They sit in the middle of the tables of 6, 7, 8 and 9, where there is no easy pattern and the answers sound alike. This page lists them, with a sure route to each. Work on one or two at a time, not the whole list.

Why these are hard

Small facts get asked thousands of times in daily life. Large ones, such as 7 × 8, come up less often, so they get less practice. They also arrive late: by the time a class reaches the table of 8, many children are tired of tables. And several answers sound close when spoken quickly, such as fifty-four and fifty-six, or sixty-three and sixty-four.

Mix-ups to watch for

  • 54 and 56. 54 belongs to 6 × 9. 56 belongs to 7 × 8. A check: 54 has digits adding to 9, so it must be in the table of 9.
  • 63 and 64. 63 is 7 × 9 and its digits add to 9. 64 is 8 × 8, an even number made from doubling.
  • 42 and 48. 42 is 6 × 7. 48 is 6 × 8. Six times an even number ends in that number: 6 × 8 ends in 8.
  • Adding instead of multiplying. A child who writes 6 × 7 = 13 has slipped into addition. Go back to rows of beans for a minute.
  • One row off. Writing 7 × 8 = 49 or 63 means the child is counting along the table and has stopped a row early or late. A direct route, such as 40 + 16, fixes this better than recounting.

How to fix a stubborn fact

  1. Pick one fact. Say it, write it, and say its route aloud.
  2. Ask it again after two other questions, then after five, then at the end of the sitting.
  3. Ask it once the next morning before anything else.
  4. Ask its turn-around (8 × 7 for 7 × 8) and its two division facts (56 ÷ 7 and 56 ÷ 8).

Four small askings spread over two days do more than twenty repeats in one go. The practice page has a "hard facts" set that asks only these.

A quick check for any answer

  • An odd number times an odd number is odd. Anything else is even. So 7 × 7 cannot be 48.
  • A multiple of 5 ends in 5 or 0.
  • A multiple of 9 has digits that add to 9.
  • The answer must lie between the two nearby easy facts: 7 × 8 is more than 7 × 7 = 49 and less than 7 × 10 = 70.
