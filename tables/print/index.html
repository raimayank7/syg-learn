Tables

Print multiplication tables

Choose what you want on the sheet and press Print. You can also save it as a PDF from the print window.

What each kind is for

  • Tables chart: the usual list of tables, to stick on a wall or inside a notebook cover. Ten tables fit one A4 sheet.
  • Multiplication grid: a square chart with every product, good for spotting patterns and for checking answers quickly.
  • Blank grid: the same square with empty boxes. Filling it in against a clock once a week is a simple way to see progress.
  • Tables with blanks: each table with the answers left out, for a child to write in.

How to print or save as PDF

  1. Choose the kind and the tables above. The sheet below changes as you choose.
  2. Press Print. Your browser's print window opens with only the sheet, without the website around it.
  3. To save a PDF, choose "Save as PDF" as the printer (on a phone: Share, then Print).
  4. Choose A4 paper. If the sheet looks cut off, pick "Fit to page" or a smaller scale.

Tips for a wall chart

Print the tables being learnt this month, not all thirty. A chart that shows three tables gets read. A chart that shows thirty becomes wallpaper. Put it where the child sits or stands often: beside the mirror, the dining table or the study desk. Change it when those tables are known.

Black on white prints cleanly on any printer and uses little ink. If you want colour, let the child colour the squares on the diagonal of the grid, or the tables already learnt.

Need questions, not charts?

For practice sheets with fresh questions and an answer page, use the times tables worksheet maker on our Worksheets site. For practice on screen, use the practice round.
