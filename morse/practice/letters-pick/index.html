Morse code practice: letters: pick the signal

Ten questions with four options. The signals are those of Recommendation ITU-R M.1677-1.

What is in this set

The set has 27 characters, drawn from the table in ITU-R M.1677-1: 2 of length 1 (E, T); 4 of length 2 (A, I, M, N); 8 of length 3 (D, G, K, O, R, S, U, W); 12 of length 4 (B, C, F, H, J, L, P, Q, V, X, Y, Z); 1 of length 5 (é). The shortest signals belong to the commonest English letters: E is a single dot and T a single dash, and the letters with two signals (I, A, N, M) follow. The longest signals, of four elements (B, C, F, H, J, L, P, Q, V, X, Y, Z), belong to letters that are used less often in English. The three-element letters (D, G, K, O, R, S, U, W) fall in between.

Picking the signal

Each question shows a character and asks you to pick the right signal from four. This is the direction you need for sending. Wrong options are made to look close to the right one, so the exercise trains you to notice small differences, such as whether the dash comes first or last. When you can score ten out of ten several times in a row, switch to the other direction or to the mixed set.

How it works

Each question shows a character and four options; one is right. After each answer the page shows the character and its signal. Nothing is sent to us, and your best score is kept in your browser.

Tips

  • Say each signal aloud as "di" for a dot and "dah" for a dash.
  • Use the chart after a miss, then try again.
  • Do both directions: reading signals and choosing signals.
