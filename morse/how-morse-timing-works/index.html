How Morse code timing works: dots, dashes and gaps

Morse code is rhythm as well as shape. The Recommendation gives four rules for lengths and spaces, measured in the length of a dot.

RuleLength
A dash3 dots
Space between the signals of one letter1 dot
Space between two letters3 dots
Space between two words7 dots

A worked example

The letter A is ·−. That is a dot (1), a gap (1) and a dash (3): 5 dot lengths. The word SOS is ··· −−− ···: S is three dots with two gaps (3 + 2 = 5), O is three dashes with two gaps (9 + 2 = 11), S again 5, and two letter gaps of 3 each: 5 + 3 + 11 + 3 + 5 = 27 dot lengths.

Speed

The Recommendation does not define a speed. A common convention measures speed in words a minute, counting the word PARIS as one word of 50 dot lengths, so one dot lasts 1.2 divided by the words a minute, in seconds. At 20 words a minute a dot lasts 0.06 seconds. Our player uses this convention.

Using the rules to learn

Learners who count the gaps (one beat inside a letter, three between letters, seven between words) hear letters as units rather than as strings of dots. Practise the gaps aloud before the shapes.

Source: Recommendation ITU-R M.1677-1, "International Morse code" (10/2009), ITU Radiocommunication Sector, saved from the ITU site and read on 14 November 2026. It is the edition we read; we could not check the ITU site for a later edition, so check it before treating this one as current. We write dots as · and dashes as −.
