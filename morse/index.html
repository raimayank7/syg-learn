What is on this site

International Morse code encodes letters, figures and punctuation as short and long signals. The translator turns text into signals and back, the player plays them as sound and as a flashing box, and the trainer asks you to read and pick signals. Every signal comes from the ITU Recommendation that confirms the code.

Where the facts come from

The table, the procedure signals and the spacing rules are from Recommendation ITU-R M.1677-1 (10/2009). We could not check for a later edition. The speed unit is a common convention.

Common questions

Where do the signals come from?

From Recommendation ITU-R M.1677-1, International Morse code (10/2009), which we read ourselves on 14 November 2026.

What is the speed unit?

Words a minute with the word PARIS as 50 dot lengths, so a dot lasts 1.2 divided by the speed. This is a common convention, not part of the ITU text.

Which characters have no signal?

Characters outside the ITU table, such as many symbols, have none and the tool lists them. The table has letters (with the accented e), figures, 13 punctuation marks and some procedure signals.

Does the sound work on every device?

It uses your browser's audio. We have not tested it on every device. If it is silent, check volume and the silent switch.

Are you connected with the ITU?

No. This is an independent site.
