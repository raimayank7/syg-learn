NEET UG pattern and marking scheme

NEET (UG) is the single national entrance test for admission to undergraduate medical, dental and AYUSH courses such as MBBS, BDS, BAMS, BUMS, BSMS and BHMS. This page gives the pattern from the NEET (UG) 2026 information bulletin.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin NEET (UG) 2026, and other NTA documents on neet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the NEET site showed documents for NEET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these figures are the latest we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check neet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the National Medical Commission or any coaching institute.

The paper at a glance

Every question is multiple choice with four options and one correct or best answer. The 2026 paper ran for 3 hours, from 2:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m. (Indian Standard Time).

Mode and languages

The bulletin says NEET (UG) 2026 is a single-day, single-shift test in pen and paper mode, as in 2025. It is conducted in 13 languages: English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu. The question paper medium chosen on the form cannot be changed later.

Marking

What you doMarks
Correct or most appropriate answer+4
Any incorrect option marked−1
Unanswered0

The bulletin also says what happens if a question turns out to have a problem. If more than one option is found correct, +4 goes to those who marked any correct option. If all options are found correct, +4 goes to everyone who attempted the question. If no option is correct, or the question is wrong or dropped, all candidates who appeared get +4.

Extra time

The bulletin gives one hour of compensatory time on a three-hour paper to PwD and PwBD candidates who have a physical limitation to write, whether or not they use a scribe.

Ties in marks

If two or more candidates have equal marks, the bulletin sets this order of tie-breaks:

  1. Higher marks in Biology (Botany and Zoology).
  2. Then higher marks in Chemistry.
  3. Then higher marks in Physics.
  4. Then the lower proportion of incorrect to correct answers across all subjects.
  5. Then the same proportion in Biology, then Chemistry, then Physics.
  6. If a tie is still there, it is settled by a random process under an independent expert committee.

Biology carries half of the marks, and it is the first tie-break, so the biology section deserves half of your preparation time. See our biology study guide and the marks calculator.
