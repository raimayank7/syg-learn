NEET UG fees, age and eligibility

This page gives the fee table and the eligibility rules of the NEET (UG) 2026 bulletin. NEET (UG) 2027 may differ: check the bulletin when it appears.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin NEET (UG) 2026, and other NTA documents on neet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the NEET site showed documents for NEET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these figures are the latest we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check neet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the National Medical Commission or any coaching institute.

Fee for NEET (UG) 2026

CategoryCentres in India
General₹1,700
General-EWS or OBC-NCL₹1,600
SC, ST, PwBD, PwD or Third Gender₹1,000

For centres outside India the bulletin shows one amount, ₹9,500, in its fee table. Payment is online by credit card, debit card, net banking or UPI. Processing charges and GST, where they apply, are paid by the candidate.

Age

  • The bulletin says a candidate must have completed 17 years of age on or before 31 December of the year of the examination, under the Graduate Medical Education Regulations 2023 as amended. For General (UR) and General-EWS candidates in 2026 this means born on or before 31 December 2009. The bulletin states a separate date for SC, ST, OBC-NCL, PwBD and PwD candidates; read it in the bulletin.
  • There is no upper age limit, according to the National Medical Commission letter quoted in the bulletin.

Educational qualification

The bulletin lists qualifying examinations by code. In plain words, a candidate needs Class 12 (or an equivalent examination recognised for the purpose) with Physics, Chemistry, Biology or Biotechnology and English. A candidate whose Class 12 result is awaited may apply and appear, but cannot be admitted to a medical course unless they pass the qualifying examination by the time of the first round of counselling. A candidate who has studied the required subjects, even as additional subjects after Class 12 from a recognised board, may also be permitted, if they give documentary evidence. The bulletin sets out the exact conditions, percentages and categories; read them there.

NRI, OCI and foreign candidates

The bulletin explains that NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreign nationals need to upload a certificate from the Indian diplomatic mission or documentary proof of citizenship, and that admission is subject to the rules of the respective states, institutions and the Government of India. Indian citizens and OCI who want to study medicine or dentistry abroad also need to qualify in NEET (UG).

Things that cause trouble

  • Applying twice. The bulletin warns that more than one application can lead to action even after the result.
  • Choosing the question paper medium wrongly: it cannot be changed later.
  • Wrong category or details. Only some fields can be corrected in the correction window.
  • Uploading unclear photographs or signatures. Read the size and format rules in the bulletin.
