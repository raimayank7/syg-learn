NEET UG score planner

Choose a target and your accuracy. The tool works out how many questions you would need to attempt, under +4 for right and −1 for wrong.

What it assumes

It assumes 180 questions with +4 and −1 marking, as in the 2026 bulletin, and the same accuracy throughout. Real papers are uneven; use the result as a rough guide.

Reading the result

  • At 100% accuracy a target of 500 needs 125 right answers.
  • Each point of accuracy you lose forces you to attempt more questions for the same score.
  • If the tool says the target cannot be reached at your accuracy, either the target is too high or accuracy must rise first.

Accuracy usually improves faster from reading your mistakes than from attempting more. See how to prepare.

How to use the planner in practice

Take your last three full papers and note how many questions you attempted and how many were right. Your accuracy is right divided by attempted. Put that accuracy in the tool with a target, and compare the number it gives with the number you actually attempted. If you attempted far more than the tool says you need, you are probably losing marks to wrong answers that you could have skipped. If you attempted far fewer, you may be leaving marks on the table: the cure is usually faster reading, not riskier guessing.

Questions people ask

Why does accuracy matter so much?

A wrong answer costs the 4 marks you could have earned and 1 more on top, a swing of 5 marks.

Does the tool predict my rank?

No. It turns marks and accuracy into counts only.
