NEET UG negative marking: guess or skip?

NEET (UG) gives +4 for a correct answer and −1 for a wrong one. An unanswered question gets 0. The paper has 180 questions in 180 minutes, so deciding when to answer and when to skip matters.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin NEET (UG) 2026, and other NTA documents on neet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the NEET site showed documents for NEET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these figures are the latest we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check neet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the National Medical Commission or any coaching institute.

The arithmetic of a guess

With four options, a pure guess is right one time in four. Expected marks from a pure guess are (1/4 × 4) + (3/4 × −1) = +0.25. If you can remove one option for a good reason, a guess among three gives (1/3 × 4) + (2/3 × −1) = about +0.67. If you can remove two, it is (1/2 × 4) + (1/2 × −1) = +1.5. These are averages over many questions: in one paper a series of wrong guesses can cost you.

What matters more

  • Accuracy. A wrong answer costs 5 marks compared with a right one.
  • Elimination. Each option you can rule out for a real reason improves the odds a great deal.
  • Time. At one minute a question there is not much time to think, so guessing must be quick and a deliberate choice.

Tools

The score planner shows how many of the 180 questions you must attempt for a target at your accuracy. The marks calculator totals your counts across the three subjects.

A rule you can practise

Many candidates set a rule: "answer if I know it, or can rule out at least two options; otherwise skip." Try it in a full timed paper and count how many guesses were right. After a few papers you will know whether your guesses beat chance. Remember that your own rule must be decided before the exam day, not during.

Questions with a problem

The bulletin explains what happens if a question has more than one correct option, or none. See the pattern page. Do not spend time worrying about this in the hall: answer the question as it stands.
