NEET UG time planner

Set the time for a final check and for Biology. The tool divides the rest between Physics and Chemistry.

The numbers behind it

The 2026 paper had 180 questions in 3 hours: 45 in Physics, 45 in Chemistry and 90 in Biology. The planner takes your final-check minutes off 180, gives Biology the time you choose, and shares the rest between Physics and Chemistry.

How to use it

  • Try a plan in a timed mock paper and adjust.
  • Decide in advance which subject you will begin with.
  • Check the clock after each subject.

More in how to prepare.

How the minutes divide

NEET has 180 questions in 180 minutes, so the average is one minute a question before any time is kept for checking. The table shows the average for different amounts kept back.

Because the average is so low, speed in reading and marking matters, and so does skipping a question early rather than losing three minutes on it.

Many candidates find Biology questions faster to read and answer than Physics calculations, so they give Biology a little less than the pro-rata share and the calculations a little more. Your own papers will tell you the right split for you.

Questions people ask

Should every subject get the same time?

No. Biology has twice as many questions as each of the other two, so it usually gets the largest share, though many students find Physics questions take longer each.

How long should I keep for a final check?

That is for you to find by practice. Try a few values in timed papers.
