NEET UG Chemistry syllabus

The NTA syllabus document lists 20 units for chemistry. The unit titles below follow that document, and the summaries and tips are in our own words. Where a unit has a long list of topics, the summary is shorter; for the exact wording, read the NTA syllabus PDF.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin NEET (UG) 2026, and other NTA documents on neet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the NEET site showed documents for NEET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these figures are the latest we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check neet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the National Medical Commission or any coaching institute.

  1. Unit 1: Some basic concepts in chemistry

    The syllabus lists: The nature of matter, laws of chemical combination, atomic and molecular masses, the mole concept, percentage composition, empirical and molecular formulae, and stoichiometry.

    Study tip: Always write the balanced equation first. Most numerical errors start with an unbalanced one.

  2. Unit 2: Atomic structure

    The syllabus lists: Electromagnetic radiation, the hydrogen spectrum, the Bohr model, de Broglie and Heisenberg, quantum numbers, orbitals and the rules for filling electrons.

    Study tip: Practise writing electron configurations for the first thirty elements until they are automatic.

  3. Unit 3: Chemical bonding and molecular structure

    The syllabus lists: Ionic and covalent bonds, lattice enthalpy, electronegativity, Fajans' rule, dipole moment, VSEPR, hybridisation and molecular orbital theory.

    Study tip: Draw the Lewis structure, count electron pairs, then name the shape. Do this for twenty molecules and the pattern is clear.

  4. Unit 4: Chemical thermodynamics

    The syllabus lists: System and surroundings, state functions, the first law, enthalpy, Hess's law, bond enthalpies, entropy, Gibbs energy and spontaneity.

    Study tip: Keep a single sign convention for heat and work. Check units of kJ against J in every step.

  5. Unit 5: Solutions

    The syllabus lists: Ways of expressing concentration, vapour pressure and Raoult's law, ideal and non-ideal solutions, colligative properties and the van't Hoff factor.

    Study tip: Learn what each colligative property depends on (the number of particles, not their kind). The four formulas then look alike.

  6. Unit 6: Equilibrium

    The syllabus lists: Dynamic equilibrium, the equilibrium constant, Le Chatelier's principle, ionic equilibrium, acids and bases, pH, buffer solutions and solubility product.

    Study tip: Set up an ICE table (initial, change, equilibrium) for every problem, even the easy ones.

  7. Unit 7: Redox reactions and electrochemistry

    The syllabus lists: Oxidation and reduction, oxidation numbers, balancing redox reactions, conductance in solutions, Kohlrausch's law, and electrochemical cells.

    Study tip: Assign oxidation numbers before anything else. Balance by the ion-electron method and check charge as well as atoms.

  8. Unit 8: Chemical kinetics

    The syllabus lists: Rate of reaction, factors affecting it, order and molecularity, rate law, integrated forms for zero and first order, half-life and the Arrhenius equation.

    Study tip: Know the half-life of a first-order reaction does not depend on concentration. It is asked in many forms.

  9. Unit 9: Classification of elements and periodicity in properties

    The syllabus lists: The modern periodic law, block structure, and trends in atomic and ionic radii, ionisation enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, valence and oxidation states.

    Study tip: Learn each trend with its reason (effective nuclear charge, shielding), and note the exceptions the textbook mentions.

  10. Unit 10: p-block elements

    The syllabus lists: Groups 13 to 18: electronic configurations, general trends and the special behaviour of the first element of each group.

    Study tip: Build one comparison table per group. Revise it by covering a column and reciting it.

  11. Unit 11: d- and f-block elements

    The syllabus lists: Transition elements: configurations, trends in properties, oxidation states, colour, catalytic behaviour, magnetic properties, and the lanthanoids and actinoids.

    Study tip: Link colour and magnetism to unpaired d-electrons. It explains many separate facts at once.

  12. Unit 12: Coordination compounds

    The syllabus lists: Werner's theory, ligands, coordination number, IUPAC naming, isomerism, valence bond and crystal field ideas, colour and magnetism.

    Study tip: Practise naming ten complexes both ways, from formula to name and back.

  13. Unit 13: Purification and characterisation of organic compounds

    The syllabus lists: Crystallisation, sublimation, distillation, extraction and chromatography, qualitative detection of elements, and the basics of quantitative analysis.

    Study tip: Match each separation method to the property it uses (boiling point, solubility, adsorption).

  14. Unit 14: Some basic principles of organic chemistry

    The syllabus lists: Tetravalency of carbon, hybridisation, functional groups, homologous series, isomerism, IUPAC naming, bond fission and electronic effects.

    Study tip: This is the foundation of all organic chemistry. Spend extra time on inductive and resonance effects and how they change stability.

  15. Unit 15: Hydrocarbons

    The syllabus lists: Alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and aromatic hydrocarbons: preparation, properties and reaction mechanisms.

    Study tip: Learn mechanisms as stories with arrows, not as pictures to memorise.

  16. Unit 16: Organic compounds containing halogens

    The syllabus lists: Preparation, properties and reactions of haloalkanes and haloarenes, the nature of the C-X bond, substitution mechanisms and some environmental effects.

    Study tip: Compare SN1 and SN2 in a table of substrate, solvent, rate law and stereochemistry.

  17. Unit 17: Organic compounds containing oxygen

    The syllabus lists: Alcohols, phenols, ethers, aldehydes, ketones and carboxylic acids: preparation, properties and reactions.

    Study tip: Make a reaction map of how each group converts into the others, and revise it as a flow chart.

  18. Unit 18: Organic compounds containing nitrogen

    The syllabus lists: Amines (classification, basic character, identification) and diazonium salts, with their uses in synthesis.

    Study tip: Know why aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine; the idea recurs in several questions.

  19. Unit 19: Biomolecules

    The syllabus lists: Carbohydrates, proteins, vitamins, nucleic acids and hormones at an introductory level.

    Study tip: A largely memory-based unit. Short daily revision with flash cards works better than one long sitting.

  20. Unit 20: Principles related to practical chemistry

    The syllabus lists: Detecting extra elements and functional groups in organic compounds, preparing some inorganic compounds, the chemistry of titrimetric exercises, and the principles of qualitative salt analysis.

    Study tip: Know the colour changes and the reagent for each test, and the reason for each step.

How to use this list

Rate each unit as strong, fair or weak, and revisit the rating every two weeks. Practise with our chemistry practice set, and read the preparation guide.
