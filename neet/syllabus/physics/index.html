NEET UG Physics syllabus

The NTA syllabus document lists 20 units for physics. The unit titles below follow that document, and the summaries and tips are in our own words. Where a unit has a long list of topics, the summary is shorter; for the exact wording, read the NTA syllabus PDF.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin NEET (UG) 2026, and other NTA documents on neet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the NEET site showed documents for NEET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these figures are the latest we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check neet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the National Medical Commission or any coaching institute.

  1. Unit 1: Units and measurements

    The syllabus lists: Units and systems, SI units, least count, significant figures, errors in measurement, dimensions and dimensional analysis.

    Study tip: Use dimensions to check every formula you learn, and to catch your own slips in an answer.

  2. Unit 2: Kinematics

    The syllabus lists: Frames of reference, motion in a straight line, graphs, uniformly accelerated motion, relative velocity, projectile motion and uniform circular motion.

    Study tip: Always choose a positive direction and write signs. Practise reading position and velocity graphs, since area and slope questions are common.

  3. Unit 3: Laws of motion

    The syllabus lists: Newton's three laws, momentum and impulse, conservation of linear momentum, friction, and the dynamics of circular motion.

    Study tip: Draw a free-body diagram for every problem, however simple. It is the habit that separates careful solvers from hurried ones.

  4. Unit 4: Work, energy and power

    The syllabus lists: Work by constant and variable forces, kinetic and potential energy, the work-energy theorem, power, conservation of mechanical energy, collisions and motion in a vertical circle.

    Study tip: Decide first whether energy or momentum (or both) is conserved. In collisions, momentum is always conserved; energy only in elastic ones.

  5. Unit 5: Rotational motion

    The syllabus lists: Centre of mass, torque, angular momentum and its conservation, moment of inertia, parallel and perpendicular axes theorems, and rigid-body rotation.

    Study tip: Build a table pairing each linear quantity with its rotational twin (mass and moment of inertia, force and torque).

  6. Unit 6: Gravitation

    The syllabus lists: The universal law, variation of g with height and depth, Kepler's laws, gravitational potential energy, escape velocity and satellite motion.

    Study tip: Learn the derivations of orbital and escape speeds once; then the formulas are easy to rebuild under pressure.

  7. Unit 7: Properties of solids and liquids

    The syllabus lists: Elasticity, stress and strain, moduli, fluid pressure, Pascal's law, viscosity, Stokes' law, Bernoulli's principle, surface tension, heat transfer and calorimetry.

    Study tip: This unit has many small formulas. Group them by topic on one page: elasticity, fluids, surface tension, heat.

  8. Unit 8: Thermodynamics

    The syllabus lists: Thermal equilibrium, the zeroth law, heat, work and internal energy, the first law, isothermal and adiabatic processes, and the second law.

    Study tip: Draw the P-V diagram for each process and shade the work. Signs of work and heat are the usual trap.

  9. Unit 9: Kinetic theory of gases

    The syllabus lists: The equation of state of a perfect gas, kinetic theory, pressure, RMS speed, degrees of freedom, equipartition of energy, specific heats and mean free path.

    Study tip: Learn what the formulas say in words: temperature is a measure of average kinetic energy, for instance.

  10. Unit 10: Oscillations and waves

    The syllabus lists: Periodic motion, simple harmonic motion and its equation, oscillations of a spring, energy in S.H.M., the simple pendulum, wave motion, speed and displacement of a progressive wave, superposition, reflection, standing waves in strings and organ pipes, harmonics and beats.

    Study tip: Write x = A sin(ωt + φ) and differentiate for velocity and acceleration. Everything else in S.H.M. follows.

  11. Unit 11: Electrostatics

    The syllabus lists: Charge and Coulomb's law, electric field and field lines, dipoles, Gauss's law, electric potential, and capacitors with their combinations.

    Study tip: Treat field as a vector and potential as a number. This one idea prevents many mistakes.

  12. Unit 12: Current electricity

    The syllabus lists: Drift velocity, Ohm's law, resistivity, series and parallel combinations, Kirchhoff's laws and their applications, emf and internal resistance of a cell, combinations of cells, the Wheatstone bridge and the metre bridge.

    Study tip: Redraw tricky circuits until they are series and parallel blocks. Use Kirchhoff only when that fails.

  13. Unit 13: Magnetic effects of current and magnetism

    The syllabus lists: Biot-Savart law, Ampere's law, force on a moving charge and on a current-carrying conductor, torque on a loop, the moving-coil galvanometer, the bar magnet as a dipole, and para-, dia- and ferromagnetic substances.

    Study tip: Fix the right-hand rules with a physical pencil and your hand, then check against the sign in each formula.

  14. Unit 14: Electromagnetic induction and alternating currents

    The syllabus lists: Faraday's law, Lenz's law, eddy currents, inductance, AC, RMS values, reactance, impedance, LCR circuits, resonance, power in AC circuits and transformers.

    Study tip: Lenz's law is conservation of energy; use it to decide direction instead of memorising cases.

  15. Unit 15: Electromagnetic waves

    The syllabus lists: Displacement current, the nature of electromagnetic waves and the electromagnetic spectrum with its uses.

    Study tip: A small unit that is easy to score on. Learn the spectrum in order of wavelength with one use for each band.

  16. Unit 16: Optics

    The syllabus lists: Reflection, mirrors, refraction, lenses, total internal reflection, prisms, optical instruments, wave optics, interference, diffraction and polarisation.

    Study tip: Fix a sign convention and keep to it. Draw the ray diagram first and then check with the formula.

  17. Unit 17: Dual nature of matter and radiation

    The syllabus lists: The photoelectric effect, Einstein's equation, the particle nature of light, matter waves and the de Broglie relation.

    Study tip: Practise reading the graphs of stopping potential against frequency; they appear again and again.

  18. Unit 18: Atoms and nuclei

    The syllabus lists: Rutherford's model, the Bohr model, hydrogen spectrum, nuclear size and composition, mass defect, binding energy per nucleon, fission and fusion.

    Study tip: Know the energy-level formula for hydrogen cold, and practise binding energy calculations with the given mass units.

  19. Unit 19: Electronic devices

    The syllabus lists: Semiconductors, the p-n junction diode, rectifiers, LED, photodiode, solar cell, Zener diode and logic gates.

    Study tip: Learn the truth table of each gate and how to build one from another. It is a quick unit to revise.

  20. Unit 20: Experimental skills

    The syllabus lists: Familiarity with the experiments and activities listed in the syllabus, such as the vernier calipers, screw gauge, simple pendulum, metre bridge and others.

    Study tip: Know the least count and the zero error idea of each instrument, and the graph or reading that comes out of each experiment.

How to use this list

Rate each unit as strong, fair or weak, and revisit the rating every two weeks. Practise with our physics practice set, and read the preparation guide.
