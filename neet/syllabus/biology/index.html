NEET UG Biology syllabus

The NTA syllabus document lists 10 units for biology. The unit titles below follow that document, and the summaries and tips are in our own words. Where a unit has a long list of topics, the summary is shorter; for the exact wording, read the NTA syllabus PDF.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin NEET (UG) 2026, and other NTA documents on neet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the NEET site showed documents for NEET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these figures are the latest we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check neet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the National Medical Commission or any coaching institute.

  1. Unit 1: Diversity in the living world

    The syllabus lists: What living means, biodiversity, why we classify, taxonomy and the taxonomic hierarchy, binomial nomenclature, the five-kingdom system (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia), lichens, viruses and viroids, and the main groups of plants and animals with three to five features and at least two examples of each.

    Study tip: Make one small table per kingdom or group: features in one column, two examples in the next. Revise the tables by covering a column. Learn the order of the hierarchy (kingdom to species) as a phrase.

  2. Unit 2: Structural organisation in animals and plants

    The syllabus lists: Morphology and modifications of roots, stems, leaves, inflorescences, flowers, fruits and seeds, plant tissues, the listed plant families, animal tissues, and a brief account of the main body systems of the animal the syllabus names.

    Study tip: Draw the parts of a flower and label them from memory until it is automatic. For families, write the floral formula of each once and keep it with a sketch.

  3. Unit 3: Cell structure and function

    The syllabus lists: Cell theory, prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, the cell envelope, organelles and the endomembrane system, the cytoskeleton, the nucleus, the biomolecules (proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids), enzymes, and the cell cycle with mitosis and meiosis.

    Study tip: Compare mitosis and meiosis in a table, stage by stage. For organelles, link every structure to its job in one sentence.

  4. Unit 4: Plant physiology

    The syllabus lists: Photosynthesis (pigments, light and dark phases, photophosphorylation, C3 and C4 pathways, photorespiration, limiting factors), respiration (glycolysis, fermentation, the TCA cycle, the electron transport system, ATP count, respiratory quotient), and plant growth with the growth regulators.

    Study tip: Draw each pathway as a flow chart with where it happens, inputs and outputs. Keep the numbers (ATP, NADH) on a separate card and revise them often.

  5. Unit 5: Human physiology

    The syllabus lists: Breathing and gas exchange, body fluids and circulation (blood, lymph, the heart, the cardiac cycle, ECG), excretion and the kidney, locomotion and movement, neural control and coordination, and chemical coordination with the endocrine glands, along with the related disorders.

    Study tip: Treat each system as input, process, output and control. Put the disorders next to the organ they affect so that the names stick.

  6. Unit 6: Reproduction

    The syllabus lists: Sexual reproduction in flowering plants (flower structure, gametophytes, pollination, double fertilisation, endosperm, embryo, seed and fruit, apomixis), human reproduction (the male and female systems, gametogenesis, the menstrual cycle, fertilisation, implantation, pregnancy), and reproductive health.

    Study tip: Make a timeline for the menstrual cycle and another for fertilisation to implantation. For plants, draw the embryo sac with its cells and the fate of each after fertilisation.

  7. Unit 7: Genetics and evolution

    The syllabus lists: Mendelian inheritance and its deviations, the chromosome theory, sex determination, linkage, sex-linked inheritance, Mendelian and chromosomal disorders, the molecular basis of inheritance (DNA as genetic material, replication, transcription, the genetic code, translation, the lac operon, DNA fingerprinting), and evolution with natural selection and the Hardy-Weinberg principle.

    Study tip: Solve many small cross problems on paper, writing the gametes and a Punnett square every time. For the molecular part, learn each process as a sequence with its enzymes.

  8. Unit 8: Biology and human welfare

    The syllabus lists: Health and disease, human pathogens and parasites, basic immunology and vaccines, cancer, HIV and AIDS, adolescence, drug and alcohol abuse, and microbes in food processing, industry, sewage treatment, energy generation, biocontrol and biofertilisers.

    Study tip: Keep a table of disease, causative organism, mode of spread and main symptom. Revise it in short daily sessions.

  9. Unit 9: Biotechnology and its applications

    The syllabus lists: The principles and process of genetic engineering (recombinant DNA technology), applications in health and agriculture (human insulin, vaccines, gene therapy, Bt crops, transgenic animals), and biosafety issues such as biopiracy and patents.

    Study tip: Learn the steps of a recombinant DNA experiment in order, with the tool used at each step (enzyme, vector, host).

  10. Unit 10: Ecology and environment

    The syllabus lists: Organisms and their environment, population interactions and attributes, ecosystems (components, productivity, decomposition, energy flow, ecological pyramids), and biodiversity and its conservation (hotspots, the Red Data Book, biosphere reserves, national parks, sanctuaries, sacred groves).

    Study tip: Draw an energy-flow diagram and keep the ecological pyramids with a sketch of each. For conservation, list one example for each term.

How to use this list

Rate each unit as strong, fair or weak, and revisit the rating every two weeks. Practise with our biology practice set, and read the preparation guide.
