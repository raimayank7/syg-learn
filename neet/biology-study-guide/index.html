NEET biology study guide

Biology is half of NEET, and it is a subject of many facts, diagrams, processes and exceptions. Students who do well in it usually do so by organised notes and frequent revision, not by one more reading. This guide is our own advice.

Turn text into tables

Almost every unit can be turned into a table: kingdom against features and examples, disease against organism and spread, hormone against gland and function, phase against input and output. Making the table is half the learning. Keep one page per table and revise them in rotation.

Draw it, then label it

Draw the heart, the nephron, the flower, the embryo sac, the DNA replication fork and the cell cycle until you can do it from memory in two minutes. Check against your book. A labelled diagram you can reproduce is a diagram you understand.

Learn processes as sequences

Photosynthesis, respiration, transcription, translation, the cardiac cycle and the menstrual cycle are sequences. Write each as numbered steps with where it happens and what goes in and out. Then test yourself by saying the steps aloud.

Numbers and names

Some facts are plain numbers: chromosome counts, ATP counts, base pairs, ratios such as 3:1 and 9:3:3:1. Keep them on a card and revise daily. For names (enzymes, hormones, diseases), use flash cards with a question on one side and the answer on the other.

Genetics and ecology as problem solving

Genetics questions are problems: write the gametes, draw the square, count. Do many on paper. Ecology has a few formulas (such as the 10 per cent law of energy transfer) and many definitions; practise both.

Our practice set

The biology practice set has questions in the style of calculations on this page: base percentages, cell divisions, crosses, Hardy-Weinberg, hydrogen bonds, energy flow and glycolysis. They are only a part of biology, and the rest is memory and understanding, so use them as a supplement.

Revision rhythm

  • Read a unit, make a table and a diagram the same day.
  • Revise it after one day, one week and one month.
  • Test yourself with questions at the end of each unit.
  • Keep a list of questions you got wrong and review it each weekend.
