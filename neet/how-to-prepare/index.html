How to prepare for NEET UG

NEET preparation is a long steady effort across three subjects, with Biology carrying half of the marks. This guide is our own writing. It does not promise a score and nothing in it comes from NTA, except where we say so.

1. Start from the NTA syllabus

NTA publishes the syllabus as a document with units for Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Our syllabus pages list every unit with a plain summary and a study tip. Study what is listed, and treat anything outside it as optional.

2. Half your time for Biology

Biology has 90 of the 180 questions, and the bulletin makes it the first tie-break. Read the textbooks of your board line by line and make notes in tables and diagrams. See the biology study guide.

3. Textbooks first

Teachers often advise that Class 11 and 12 school textbooks are the base for all three subjects, and that practice books come after you know the text. The NTA bulletin does not name a textbook, so this is advice and not a rule. Choose one main textbook for each subject and one practice source, and keep to them.

4. Practise the real format

The paper is on paper, 180 questions in three hours, so you have an average of one minute per question. Practise reading fast and marking clearly. Use our practice sets to get used to +4 and −1 marking; they are our own and easier than real questions.

5. Keep a mistake notebook

Write each wrong question, the right idea and one line on why you slipped. Reread it weekly. A short, personal list of errors is more useful than another new book.

6. Revise often, in small pieces

Biology facts fade quickly. Use short, regular revisions: one unit a day on a rotation, with a quick test at the end of the week. Sleep, meals and exercise matter as much as study hours.

7. Look after your mind

Preparing for medicine can feel like the only path. It is not. If the pressure is heavy, talk to a parent, teacher or counsellor. Many careers in health and science are open to those who study these subjects well.
