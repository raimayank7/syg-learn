GPS full form: Global Positioning System

How to say it: letter by letter.

GPS stands for Global Positioning System. It is a system of satellites, owned by the United States and free for anyone to use, that lets a receiver work out where it is on Earth.

How it finds your position

About thirty GPS satellites circle the Earth, each sending out a radio signal that carries its position and the exact time. A receiver, such as the chip in a phone, picks up signals from several satellites and measures how long each took to arrive. From the delays it works out its distance from each satellite, and from at least four such distances it calculates its own latitude, longitude and height. The receiver only listens. It sends nothing to the satellites.

GPS does not need the internet

The satellite signals are free and need no SIM or data connection. Map apps use data to download the maps and traffic, not to find the position. With maps saved in advance, navigation works offline.

GPS is one of several systems

The general name for such systems is GNSS, Global Navigation Satellite System. Others are Russia's GLONASS, the European Union's Galileo and China's BeiDou. India has its own regional system, NavIC, Navigation with Indian Constellation, operated by ISRO. Modern phones use several of these together for a quicker and more accurate fix, though people still call it all "GPS".

Uses

  • Navigation in cars, ships and aircraft.
  • Tracking delivery vehicles, buses and trains.
  • Sharing a live location with family.
  • Surveying land, and setting clocks very precisely.

Why it sometimes fails

The signals are weak and need a view of the sky. Indoors, in tunnels and between tall buildings the position may be slow or wrong.
