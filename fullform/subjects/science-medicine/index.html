Full forms: science and medicine

44 full forms, each with a line on what it means.

Science uses short forms for long chemical names, for instruments and for units; hospitals use them for departments, tests and conditions. This page explains those that appear in school science and on medical papers: the molecules of life, terms of electricity and light, common materials, and the words on a hospital signboard or a laboratory report. It explains what a term means. It is not medical advice, and it does not tell you what a test result means for you; ask your doctor that. Three of the entries, LASER, RADAR and SONAR, began as initials and are now ordinary words usually written in small letters.

Explained in full: DNA

  • DNA

    Deoxyribonucleic Acid

    The molecule in living cells that carries the genetic instructions passed from parents to offspring.

    DNA explained

  • RNA

    Ribonucleic Acid

    A molecule that carries genetic messages within the cell and helps make proteins.

  • ATP

    Adenosine Triphosphate

    The molecule that carries energy for use inside living cells.

  • pH

    Potential of Hydrogen

    A scale from 0 to 14 that shows how acidic or basic a solution is; 7 is neutral.

  • LASER

    Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation

    A device that gives a narrow, intense beam of light of a single colour.

  • RADAR

    Radio Detection and Ranging

    A system that finds distant objects by bouncing radio waves off them.

  • SONAR

    Sound Navigation and Ranging

    A system that finds objects under water using sound waves.

  • AC

    Alternating Current

    Electric current that reverses direction many times a second, as in mains supply. AC is also short for air conditioner.

  • DC

    Direct Current

    Electric current that flows in one direction, as from a battery.

  • EMF

    Electromotive Force

    The voltage produced by a cell or generator.

  • SI

    Système International

    As in SI units: the international system of units of measurement, from its French name. In the police, SI means sub-inspector.

  • STP

    Standard Temperature and Pressure

    Agreed reference conditions for comparing gases.

  • UV

    Ultraviolet

    Light with a wavelength shorter than violet, invisible to the eye; it causes sunburn.

  • IR

    Infrared

    Light with a wavelength longer than red, felt as heat; remote controls use it.

  • CFC

    Chlorofluorocarbon

    A group of gases once used in refrigerators and sprays, which damage the ozone layer.

  • PVC

    Polyvinyl Chloride

    A common plastic used for pipes, wire insulation and flooring.

  • TNT

    Trinitrotoluene

    A well-known explosive.

  • BMI

    Body Mass Index

    A number worked out from weight and height, used as a rough guide to healthy weight.

  • BP

    Blood Pressure

    The pressure of blood in the arteries, given as two numbers such as 120/80.

  • ECG

    Electrocardiogram

    A recording of the electrical activity of the heart.

  • MRI

    Magnetic Resonance Imaging

    A scan that uses strong magnets and radio waves to picture the inside of the body.

  • CT

    Computed Tomography

    As in CT scan: X-ray pictures taken from many angles and combined by a computer.

  • ICU

    Intensive Care Unit

    The hospital ward for patients who need constant monitoring and support.

  • OPD

    Outpatient Department

    The part of a hospital where patients are seen without being admitted.

  • OT

    Operation Theatre

    The room where surgery is done. At work, OT means overtime.

  • ORS

    Oral Rehydration Solution

    A mix of salts and sugar in water, given to replace fluids lost in diarrhoea.

  • HIV

    Human Immunodeficiency Virus

    The virus that attacks the immune system and can lead to AIDS.

  • AIDS

    Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

    The advanced stage of infection with HIV.

  • TB

    Tuberculosis

    An infectious disease, usually of the lungs, caused by bacteria.

  • RBC

    Red Blood Cell

    The blood cell that carries oxygen.

  • WBC

    White Blood Cell

    The blood cell that fights infection.

  • CBC

    Complete Blood Count

    A common blood test that counts the different blood cells.

  • IVF

    In Vitro Fertilisation

    A treatment in which an egg is fertilised outside the body.

  • ENT

    Ear, Nose and Throat

    The medical speciality, and its specialist, for these parts.

  • PCOD

    Polycystic Ovarian Disease

    A common hormonal condition in women, also called PCOS.

  • COVID

    Coronavirus Disease

    As in COVID-19, the disease caused by the coronavirus first reported in 2019.

  • PPE

    Personal Protective Equipment

    Protective clothing and gear such as masks, gloves and gowns.

  • BCG

    Bacillus Calmette-Guérin

    The vaccine against tuberculosis, named after the two scientists who developed it.

  • ASHA

    Accredited Social Health Activist

    A trained woman health worker in an Indian village.

  • AYUSH

    Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy

    The systems of medicine covered by the Ministry of AYUSH.

  • RO

    Reverse Osmosis

    A method of purifying water by forcing it through a fine membrane.

  • TDS

    Total Dissolved Solids

    A measure of the minerals and salts dissolved in water. In tax, TDS is tax deducted at source.

  • EV

    Electric Vehicle

    A vehicle driven by an electric motor and battery.

  • GMO

    Genetically Modified Organism

    A plant or animal whose genes have been changed in a laboratory.

How to read an entry

The letters in bold are the short form as it is normally written. Under it is the full form, and then a line of meaning in plain words. Where the same letters mean something else in another field, the entry mentions it. "Copy" puts the short form, the full form and the meaning on your clipboard, ready to paste into notes or a message.
