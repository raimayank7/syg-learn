Full forms: chat, letters and everyday

49 full forms, each with a line on what it means.

Two very different groups meet on this page. The first is old: Latin short forms that have been used in English writing for centuries, such as e.g., i.e., etc., AM, PM and RSVP. People use them daily without knowing the words behind them, and mix up e.g. with i.e. The second group is new: the short forms of text messages and social media, from ASAP and FYI in office mail to LOL and BRB in chats. The first group is fine in formal writing; the second is not, and should be kept out of applications, examination answers and letters to people you do not know well.

Explained in full: OK AM and PM RSVP

  • OK

    no certain full form

    The most widely accepted origin is "oll korrect", a joke spelling of "all correct" from Boston newspapers of 1839.

    OK explained

  • ASAP

    As Soon As Possible

    Used to ask for something urgently.

  • FYI

    For Your Information

    Used when passing on something the reader should know.

  • RSVP

    Répondez s'il vous plaît

    French for "please reply", printed on invitations to ask guests to say whether they will come.

    RSVP explained

  • PS

    Postscript

    A note added after the end of a letter. From the Latin post scriptum, "written after".

  • NB

    Nota Bene

    Latin for "note well", used to draw attention to something important.

  • e.g.

    Exempli gratia

    Latin for "for example".

  • i.e.

    Id est

    Latin for "that is".

  • etc.

    Et cetera

    Latin for "and the rest".

  • vs

    Versus

    Latin for "against".

  • AM

    Ante meridiem

    Latin for "before midday": the hours from midnight to noon.

  • PM

    Post meridiem

    Latin for "after midday": the hours from noon to midnight.

  • AD

    Anno Domini

    Latin for "in the year of the Lord", used for years counted forward from the traditional date of the birth of Christ.

  • BC

    Before Christ

    Used for years counted backwards from the same date.

  • CE

    Common Era

    A neutral name for the same years as AD.

  • BCE

    Before Common Era

    A neutral name for the same years as BC.

  • RIP

    Rest in Peace

    Said or written of someone who has died. From the Latin requiescat in pace.

  • DOB

    Date of Birth

    The day on which a person was born.

  • ID

    Identity Document

    A card or paper that proves who a person is; in everyday use, short for identity or identification.

  • VIP

    Very Important Person

    A person given special treatment because of rank or fame.

  • DIY

    Do It Yourself

    Making or repairing things oneself.

  • ATM

    At The Moment

    A chat short form. As a machine, ATM is an automated teller machine.

  • BTW

    By The Way

    Used to add a side remark.

  • IDK

    I Don't Know

    A chat short form.

  • IMO

    In My Opinion

    Used before giving a personal view.

  • LOL

    Laughing Out Loud

    Shows that the writer found something funny.

  • BRB

    Be Right Back

    Says the writer is stepping away for a moment.

  • TTYL

    Talk To You Later

    A way of ending a chat.

  • OMG

    Oh My God

    Shows surprise.

  • TBH

    To Be Honest

    Used before a frank remark.

  • DM

    Direct Message

    A private message on a social media app.

  • PFA

    Please Find Attached

    Used in email to point to an attached file.

  • TBD

    To Be Decided

    Used for a detail not yet fixed.

  • NA

    Not Applicable

    Written in a form where a question does not apply. It can also mean not available.

  • PTO

    Please Turn Over

    Written at the foot of a page to show that it continues overleaf.

  • c/o

    Care of

    Used in an address when a letter is sent to someone at another person's address.

  • w.e.f.

    With effect from

    Used in notices to give the starting date of a change.

  • wrt

    With respect to

    Used in letters to mean "about" or "concerning".

  • GM

    Good Morning

    A greeting in chat. In business, GM means general manager.

  • GN

    Good Night

    A parting greeting in chat.

  • HBD

    Happy Birthday

    A short birthday greeting.

  • TY

    Thank You

    A short way to thank someone.

  • SOS

    no full form

    The international distress signal in Morse code. The letters were chosen because they are easy to send, not as initials; "Save Our Souls" was attached later.

  • GIF

    Graphics Interchange Format

    On chat apps, a short looping moving picture.

  • FOMO

    Fear Of Missing Out

    The worry that others are enjoying something without you.

  • AKA

    Also Known As

    Used before another name of a person or thing.

  • TGIF

    Thank God It's Friday

    Said at the end of the working week.

  • PJ

    Poor Joke

    Indian slang for a weak joke.

  • ISO

    In Search Of

    Used in online posts by someone looking for something.

How to read an entry

The letters in bold are the short form as it is normally written. Under it is the full form, and then a line of meaning in plain words. Where the same letters mean something else in another field, the entry mentions it. "Copy" puts the short form, the full form and the meaning on your clipboard, ready to paste into notes or a message.
