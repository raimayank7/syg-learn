Maths games for classes 5 and 6

15 games that suit classes 5 and 6. Tap one to play.

In classes 5 and 6 the subject widens. Whole numbers give way to fractions and decimals, factors and multiples, HCF and LCM, angles and the first negative numbers. Many children who were comfortable until now begin to wobble here, usually over fractions.

The games for this stage are about number properties and about seeing: the size of a fraction, the size of an angle, which numbers are prime. Each one aims at a common weak spot.

Games made for this stage

  • Odd One OutThree numbers share a property and one does not. Find the one that does not belong.Classes 2 to 7
  • Number CatcherA board of twelve numbers. Tap every number that fits the rule, and leave the rest.Classes 3 to 7
  • Fraction PiesA circle is cut into equal slices and some are shaded. Name the fraction.Classes 3 to 6
  • Guess the AngleAn angle is drawn. Judge its size by eye and choose the closest number of degrees.Classes 4 to 7

Also suitable

These games have levels that fit classes 5 and 6 too.

  • Bigger or SmallerTwo numbers side by side. Tap the bigger one, or the smaller one when asked.Classes 1 to 7
  • Sum SprintQuick sums against yourself. Type the answer on the number pad.Classes 1 to 7
  • Missing SignA sum with its sign missing. Which of +, −, × and ÷ makes it true?Classes 2 to 5
  • Place ValueWhich digit is in the hundreds place? What is the 7 really worth?Classes 2 to 5
  • What Comes NextA row of numbers follows a rule. Find the rule and choose the next number.Classes 2 to 7
  • Memory MatchTwelve cards face down. Turn over two at a time and match each sum with its answer.Classes 1 to 5
  • Percent MatchWhich fraction is the same as 25%? What is 20% of 150? Choose the match.Classes 5 to 8
  • Find xA simple equation. Work out the value of x and choose it.Classes 6 to 8
  • Squares and RootsSquares, square roots, cubes and powers. Type the answer.Classes 6 to 8
  • Hit the TargetUse every number once, with any of the signs, to make the target number.Classes 3 to 8
  • Magic SquareA three-by-three square with gaps. Fill it so that every row, column and diagonal has the same total.Classes 4 to 8

What to look for at this age

  • Fractions as numbers. Knowing roughly where 3/4 or 2/5 lies between 0 and 1, and which of two fractions is larger.
  • Factors and multiples without mixing the two words. Our neighbour site Factors explains them with working.
  • Primes up to 50 recognised at sight.
  • Estimation. Judging an angle or an answer before measuring or calculating.

Making practice count

At this age children can reflect on their own mistakes. After a round, look at the list of missed questions and ask what went wrong each time: a slip, a forgotten fact or a misunderstanding. A slip needs care, a fact needs practice and a misunderstanding needs a conversation. Telling them apart is half the cure.
