Maths games for classes 7 and 8

11 games that suit classes 7 and 8. Tap one to play.

Classes 7 and 8 bring algebra, negative numbers used freely, powers and roots, and percentages applied to profit, loss and interest. Mental arithmetic still matters, because algebra is arithmetic with letters and a slow or shaky calculator makes every equation harder.

The games here practise solving equations, converting among fractions, decimals and percentages, recalling squares and cubes, and flexible thinking with numbers. They also make good warm-ups before homework.

Games made for this stage

  • Percent MatchWhich fraction is the same as 25%? What is 20% of 150? Choose the match.Classes 5 to 8
  • Find xA simple equation. Work out the value of x and choose it.Classes 6 to 8
  • Squares and RootsSquares, square roots, cubes and powers. Type the answer.Classes 6 to 8
  • Hit the TargetUse every number once, with any of the signs, to make the target number.Classes 3 to 8
  • Magic SquareA three-by-three square with gaps. Fill it so that every row, column and diagonal has the same total.Classes 4 to 8

Also suitable

These games have levels that fit classes 7 and 8 too.

  • Bigger or SmallerTwo numbers side by side. Tap the bigger one, or the smaller one when asked.Classes 1 to 7
  • Sum SprintQuick sums against yourself. Type the answer on the number pad.Classes 1 to 7
  • What Comes NextA row of numbers follows a rule. Find the rule and choose the next number.Classes 2 to 7
  • Odd One OutThree numbers share a property and one does not. Find the one that does not belong.Classes 2 to 7
  • Number CatcherA board of twelve numbers. Tap every number that fits the rule, and leave the rest.Classes 3 to 7
  • Guess the AngleAn angle is drawn. Judge its size by eye and choose the closest number of degrees.Classes 4 to 7

What to look for at this age

  • Equations as a balance. Doing the same thing to both sides, and checking the answer by putting it back.
  • Signs. Adding, subtracting and multiplying with negative numbers without hesitation.
  • Squares to 20 and cubes to 10 by heart.
  • Percentages as fractions. 25% as a quarter, 12.5% as an eighth. For formulas of area and volume, see our neighbour site Shapes.

Beyond practice

Older children gain most from the open games. In Hit the Target and Magic Square there is more than one way forward, and trying, failing and trying again is the lesson. Ask for a second solution, or ask the child to set a puzzle for you. Explaining a method to someone else is the best test of understanding there is.
