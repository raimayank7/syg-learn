Maths games for classes 3 and 4

15 games that suit classes 3 and 4. Tap one to play.

Classes 3 and 4 are the years of fluency. Children meet multiplication and division, numbers in thousands and lakhs, and longer sums with carrying and borrowing. The aim now is for the basic facts, adding within 20 and the tables to 10, to come without effort, so that attention is free for the method.

The games here give quick practice of those facts and of place value and patterns. A good routine is one fact game, such as Sum Sprint, and one thinking game, such as What Comes Next, each for five minutes.

Games made for this stage

  • Sum SprintQuick sums against yourself. Type the answer on the number pad.Classes 1 to 7
  • Missing SignA sum with its sign missing. Which of +, −, × and ÷ makes it true?Classes 2 to 5
  • Place ValueWhich digit is in the hundreds place? What is the 7 really worth?Classes 2 to 5
  • What Comes NextA row of numbers follows a rule. Find the rule and choose the next number.Classes 2 to 7
  • Memory MatchTwelve cards face down. Turn over two at a time and match each sum with its answer.Classes 1 to 5

Also suitable

These games have levels that fit classes 3 and 4 too.

  • Make TenTwelve number tiles. Tap two that add up to the target. Clear the whole board.Classes 1 to 3
  • Bigger or SmallerTwo numbers side by side. Tap the bigger one, or the smaller one when asked.Classes 1 to 7
  • Clock TimeA clock face shows a time. Choose the time it shows.Classes 1 to 4
  • Coin CountCoins and notes are laid out. Add them up and choose the total.Classes 1 to 4
  • Odd One OutThree numbers share a property and one does not. Find the one that does not belong.Classes 2 to 7
  • Number CatcherA board of twelve numbers. Tap every number that fits the rule, and leave the rest.Classes 3 to 7
  • Fraction PiesA circle is cut into equal slices and some are shaded. Name the fraction.Classes 3 to 6
  • Guess the AngleAn angle is drawn. Judge its size by eye and choose the closest number of degrees.Classes 4 to 7
  • Hit the TargetUse every number once, with any of the signs, to make the target number.Classes 3 to 8
  • Magic SquareA three-by-three square with gaps. Fill it so that every row, column and diagonal has the same total.Classes 4 to 8

What to look for at this age

  • Tables to 10 in any order, in about three seconds each. Our neighbour site Tables has every table with its patterns.
  • Place value to lakhs. The value of each digit in a number such as 3,45,678.
  • Choosing the operation. Knowing whether a story sum needs adding, taking away, multiplying or dividing.
  • Checking. Asking "does my answer make sense?" before moving on.

Speed and accuracy

Use the relaxed mode first. Only when a child gets nine out of ten right should the 60-second race come in, and even then the child should race their own best score, not a brother or sister. Some children enjoy a clock and some are upset by it. Both can become good at maths.
