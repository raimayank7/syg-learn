Answers that change: office holders and your state

A few civics answers are not printed in the study lists, because they change with elections and appointments or depend on where you live. You must answer with the name of the official serving on the day of your interview.

National offices: what the USCIS page lists

USCIS publishes the current accepted answers on its Check for Test Updates page (uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates). When we read it on 12 November 2026, the page was marked last reviewed on September 18, 2025 and listed these answers. Always open the official page yourself shortly before your interview, because an election, a resignation or a new appointment can change an answer after our check.

If you live in Washington, D.C. or a U.S. territory

The official lists give special instructions. For the senator question, residents of the District of Columbia and of U.S. territories should answer that D.C., or the territory where they live, has no U.S. senators. For the representative question, residents of territories with a non-voting Delegate or Resident Commissioner may give that person's name; a statement that the territory has no voting representatives in Congress is also acceptable. For the governor question, D.C. residents should answer that D.C. does not have a governor.

How to prepare these answers

  1. Write the questions above on one card.
  2. One or two weeks before your interview, look up each answer on the official sites and write it on the card.
  3. Check again the day before, especially if there has been an election since you filed. Newly elected officials take office in January after a November election.
  4. Practise saying each name aloud. A family name alone is listed as acceptable for the national offices on the USCIS page.

We do not print the names of senators, representatives or governors on this site. There are hundreds of them and they change; the official sites are always right, and a stale list here could cost you a question.
