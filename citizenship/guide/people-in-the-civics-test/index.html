People in the civics test: who did what

The 2025 list asks about a dozen people by name. The hard part is not learning who they were but keeping them apart under pressure.

Below are the 12 questions of the 2025 list that are about named people, with every accepted answer. Most are worded "… is famous for many things. Name one." So for each person you need exactly one fact. Choose a fact that belongs to that person alone. "President of the United States" is true of several of them and is easy to attach to the wrong name; "wrote the Declaration of Independence" can only be Jefferson.

One fact each

Our suggestion for a single, unmistakable answer for each person. Each is among the accepted answers above.

  • Benjamin Franklin: founded the first free public libraries.
  • George Washington: first president of the United States.
  • Thomas Jefferson: writer of the Declaration of Independence.
  • James Madison: "Father of the Constitution".
  • Alexander Hamilton: first Secretary of the Treasury.
  • Abraham Lincoln: freed the slaves.
  • Franklin Roosevelt: president during the Great Depression and World War II.
  • Dwight Eisenhower: general during World War II.
  • Martin Luther King, Jr.: fought for civil rights.

In order of time

Franklin, Washington, Jefferson, Madison and Hamilton all belong to the founding years, the 1770s to the early 1800s. Lincoln belongs to the Civil War, the 1860s. The leaders of the women's rights movement named in the list worked in the 1800s. Roosevelt and Eisenhower belong to the middle of the 1900s, and King to the 1950s and 1960s.

Presidents by number

The accepted answers mention that Washington was the first president, Jefferson the third, Madison the fourth and Eisenhower the 34th. Lincoln was the 16th.

In the 2008 list

The 2008 list asks about fewer people, and a little differently. It asks who the "Father of Our Country" is and who was the first president (Washington both times), what Susan B. Anthony did, who was president during World War I (Woodrow Wilson), and which war Eisenhower was a general in.
