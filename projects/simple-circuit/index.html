A Simple Electric Circuit: a school project idea

Light a small bulb with one battery and wires, and see what happens when the circuit has a gap.

  • Science
  • Classes 6 to 8
  • About 30 min of work
  • Time to finish: one afternoon
  • Adult needed
An adult is needed. An adult must prepare the wires and the battery, and children must never touch the mains electricity or sockets. Please read all the steps before the child begins.

What this project shows

To show that electricity needs a complete loop to flow and light a bulb.

What you need

You needHow many
AA battery (1.5 volt)1
Small torch bulb in a holder (1.5 volt)1
Plastic-covered wire pieces2
Sticky tape (roll)1
Notebook1

Steps

  1. An adult removes about two centimetres of plastic from both ends of each piece of wire.
  2. Tape one wire to each end of the battery, one at the flat end and one at the end with the small bump.
  3. Touch or tape the other ends of the two wires to the two metal screws or clips of the bulb holder. The bulb should light.
  4. Lift one wire off the battery to make a gap. The bulb goes out. Put it back and it lights again.
  5. Draw the circuit in your notebook, using lines for wires and a circle for the bulb.

What to observe and write down

Draw the circuit with and without the gap. Write what you had to change to make the bulb light and what stopped it.

How it works

A battery pushes electricity round a loop of wire. If the loop is broken anywhere, the electricity cannot flow and the bulb goes out. The bulb lights when the loop is complete.

Presenting it

Show the working circuit and a clear drawing. Explain that you used only a small battery, and never the mains electricity at home, which is dangerous.
