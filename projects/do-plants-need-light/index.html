Do Plants Need Light?: a school project idea

Grow two bean plants, one in light and one in the dark, and compare them.

  • Science
  • Classes 3 to 6
  • About 1 h of work
  • Time to finish: about 10 days
  • No adult needed

What this project shows

To find out whether plants grow differently with light and without it.

What you need

You needHow many
Clear plastic cup2
Soil or damp cotton wool1
Bean seeds4
Small jug of water1
Ruler1
Notebook1

Steps

  1. Fill both cups with soil or damp cotton wool and push two bean seeds into each, against the side of the cup so that you can see them.
  2. Put one cup on a sunny window sill and the other in a dark cupboard. Write on each cup where it lives.
  3. Give both cups the same small amount of water every day or two.
  4. Every day, look at both cups. Measure any shoots with the ruler and write the heights in a table in your notebook.
  5. After ten days, take the dark plant out and compare it with the plant from the window.

What to observe and write down

Write down the height, the colour and the shape of each plant every day. A good table has three columns: the day, the light plant and the dark plant. Draw both plants on the last day.

How it works

Plants use light to make their food. A plant kept in the dark often grows tall, thin and pale, because it is searching for light. Results can differ, so repeat the test with another pair of cups if you can.

Presenting it

Show the two plants side by side with the table and a drawing of each. Say what you expected before you started.
