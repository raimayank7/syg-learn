A Model of a Plant Cell: a school project idea

A clay model of a plant cell with its main parts labelled.

  • Science
  • Classes 8 to 10
  • About 1 h 30 min of work
  • Time to finish: two afternoons
  • No adult needed

What this project shows

To show the main parts of a plant cell and what each one does.

What you need

You needHow many
Modelling clay (several colours)1
Cardboard sheet (large)1
Paper labels8
Marker pen1
Sticky tape (roll)1

Steps

  1. On the cardboard, make a large rectangle from green clay for the cell wall, pressed flat like a thick frame.
  2. Inside it, make a thin line of clay just inside the frame for the cell membrane, and fill the space with a soft layer of clay for the cytoplasm.
  3. Add a round ball for the nucleus and a large clear shape for the vacuole, and small green ovals for the chloroplasts.
  4. Write each part on a label and tape it by the part. Add a title.
  5. Make a small table that says what each part does.

What to observe and write down

Write one sentence for each part. Say which parts an animal cell does not have: the cell wall, the chloroplasts and the large vacuole.

How it works

The cell wall gives the cell its shape, the membrane controls what goes in and out, the nucleus controls the cell, the vacuole stores water and the chloroplasts make food using light. A model like this simplifies a real cell, which is much smaller and more complicated.

Presenting it

Show a drawing of a real cell from a textbook beside your model. Be ready to say what each part does.
