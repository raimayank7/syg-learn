Teaching repeat loops with a turtle

A loop means "do this again". It is one of the big ideas in programming, and drawing shapes makes it easy to see. This is our own way of teaching it.

Step 1: write it the long way

Ask your child to write a square with eight lines:

forward 100
right 90
forward 100
right 90
forward 100
right 90
forward 100
right 90

Step 2: spot the pattern

Ask: "What do you notice?" The same two lines happen four times. Many children see it straight away.

Step 3: use repeat

repeat 4
  forward 100
  right 90
end

Eight lines became four, and a bigger shape does not need more lines. A star only changes the numbers.

Try it in the maker

Step 4: change the numbers

  • Change 4 to 5. What happens? (The shape does not close.)
  • Change 90 to 72 with repeat 5. Now?
  • Try the star: repeat 5, forward 160, right 144.

A loop inside a loop

When your child is ready, try the flower example, which has a loop inside a loop. It draws six sides, turns a little, and does that twelve times.

Open the flower

Why there is a limit

A loop that never stops would freeze a page, so the maker stops any program after 5,000 steps and says so. That is a good moment to talk about how many times a loop really runs.

The advice on this page is our own. It is not a school syllabus or a rule from any organisation, and every child learns at their own pace.
