Trigonometry identities and the angle table

Angles are in degrees on this page. The calculators show both sides of each identity so you can see them match for any angle you try.

sin² + cos² = 1

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for every angle θ

The basic identity; it gives cos from sin and the other way round.

Worked example: With Angle θ in degrees = 37: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

1 + tan² = sec²

1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, where sec θ = 1 / cos θ

Used with the identity above; θ cannot be 90° or 270°.

Worked example: With Angle θ in degrees = 30: 1 + tan²θ = 1.3333; sec²θ = 1.3333.

sin (A + B)

sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B

Finding sines of sums of angles, such as sin 75° = sin(45° + 30°).

Worked example: With Angle A in degrees = 45, Angle B in degrees = 30: sin(A + B) = 0.9659; sin A cos B + cos A sin B = 0.9659.

cos (A + B)

cos(A + B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B

Finding cosines of sums of angles.

Worked example: With Angle A in degrees = 45, Angle B in degrees = 30: cos(A + B) = 0.2588; cos A cos B − sin A sin B = 0.2588.

Sides of a right-angled triangle

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite / adjacent

Finding the other sides when one side and an angle are known. Here the hypotenuse and an angle are given.

Worked example: With Hypotenuse h = 10, Angle θ in degrees = 30: Opposite side = 5; Adjacent side = 8.6603.

Remember by using

  • sin 30° = cos 60° and sin 60° = cos 30°: sin of an angle equals cos of what is left to 90°.
  • The values in the table follow a pattern: sin goes 0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1 and cos goes backwards.
  • Tan is sin divided by cos, so tan 90° is not defined.
