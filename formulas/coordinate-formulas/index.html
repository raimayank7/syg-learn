Coordinate geometry formulas with calculators

Points are written (x, y). The calculators take the numbers you type and show the result at once.

Distance between two points

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

The straight-line length between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).

Worked example: With x₁ = 1, y₁ = 2, x₂ = 4, y₂ = 6: Distance = 5.

Midpoint of a line segment

Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

The point halfway between two points.

Worked example: With x₁ = 1, y₁ = 2, x₂ = 5, y₂ = 8: x of midpoint = 3; y of midpoint = 5.

Slope of a line

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁), with x₂ ≠ x₁

How steep the line through two points is.

If x₁ = x₂ the line is vertical and has no slope.

Worked example: With x₁ = 1, y₁ = 2, x₂ = 4, y₂ = 8: Slope m = 2.

Area of a triangle from its corners

Area = ½ |x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|

The area of the triangle with corners (x₁, y₁), (x₂, y₂) and (x₃, y₃). If it comes to 0 the three points are on one line.

Worked example: With x₁ = 0, y₁ = 0, x₂ = 4, y₂ = 0, x₃ = 0, y₃ = 3: Area = 6.

Remember by using

  • The distance formula is Pythagoras' theorem on the horizontal and vertical gaps.
  • The order of the points does not matter for distance, midpoint or area, but it does for the sign of the slope if you swap only one of the two points' values.
  • If the triangle area is 0, the three points are on one line.

These are standard textbook formulas, checked by program against separate code, but check them against your own textbook before an exam. All advice here is our own writing. This site is independent and is not connected with any board, publisher or exam body.
