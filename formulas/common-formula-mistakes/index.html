Common formula mistakes

These are our own observations of common errors.

MistakeExampleFix
Radius and diameter mixedUsing 10 as r when the diameter is 10Halve the diameter first
(a + b)² taken as a² + b²(1 + 2)² = 9, not 5Remember the middle term 2ab
Units not the sameSpeed in km/h with time in secondsConvert everything first
Percent on the wrong baseProfit worked on the selling priceProfit % is on the cost price
Height and slant height mixedUsing h in the curved surface of a coneUse l = √(r² + h²)
Negative acceleration forgottenBraking entered as +2Slowing down is negative

Why each mistake happens

Radius and diameter. A circle problem may give the diameter, the distance across, while the formulas for area and circumference use the radius, half of it. Always write down which one you were given before you substitute.

Squaring a sum. (a + b)² means (a + b) multiplied by itself, which gives a² + 2ab + b². The middle term 2ab is the one that is forgotten. Test with small numbers: (1 + 2)² is 9, but 1² + 2² is only 5.

Mixed units. A formula works only when its quantities are in matching units. If speed is in kilometres an hour and time is in seconds, the answer will be wrong by a factor of 3,600 or 3.6 and may look quite reasonable. Convert first, then calculate.

Wrong base for percent. Profit and loss percent is worked on the cost price, and discount percent on the marked price. A common error is to work it on whichever number is nearest.

Height and slant height. A cone has two lengths that look alike. The curved surface uses the slant height l, the length along the side, while the volume uses the vertical height h. They are connected by l = √(r² + h²).

Signs in motion. In the equations of motion a quantity pointing against the direction you chose as positive is negative, so braking is a negative acceleration. A calculator will not warn you when you enter it the wrong way round.

How to catch them

Estimate the answer first. If a circle of radius 7 gets an area of 15, something is wrong; the area is about 154. A quick estimate catches most slips.
