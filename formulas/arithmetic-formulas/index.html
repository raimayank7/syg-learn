Percentage, profit and interest formulas

The calculators take numbers you type. Rates are percent a year and time is in years unless stated.

Simple interest

SI = P × R × T / 100, and Amount = P + SI (R is the rate per year in percent, T is in years)

Interest on a loan or deposit that is not added back to the principal.

Worked example: With Principal P = 10000, Rate R % a year = 8, Time T in years = 3: Simple interest = 2400; Amount = 12400.

Compound interest (yearly)

Amount = P × (1 + R/100)ⁿ, and CI = Amount − P (R is the rate per year in percent, n is the number of years)

Interest that is added to the principal each year and then earns interest.

Worked example: With Principal P = 10000, Rate R % a year = 8, Years n = 3: Amount = 12597.12; Compound interest = 2597.12.

Percentage change

Percentage change = (new − old) / old × 100. A negative answer is a decrease

Rises and falls in prices, marks or populations.

The old value cannot be 0.

Worked example: With Old value = 80, New value = 100: Change in percent = 25.

Profit or loss percent

Profit % = (SP − CP) / CP × 100. A negative answer is a loss percent

SP is the selling price and CP is the cost price. The percent is always on the cost price.

Worked example: With Cost price CP = 400, Selling price SP = 460: Profit or loss = 60; Profit % (negative = loss) = 15.

Average (mean)

Average = sum of the values / number of values

Marks, speeds and prices.

Worked example: With Sum of the values = 252, How many values = 6: Average = 42.

Remember by using

  • Profit and loss percent are always worked on the cost price.
  • Percentage change is worked on the old value.
  • Compound interest is bigger than simple interest for more than one year at the same rate.

These are standard textbook formulas, checked by program against separate code, but check them against your own textbook before an exam. All advice here is our own writing. This site is independent and is not connected with any board, publisher or exam body.
