Mensuration formulas: area and volume of shapes

Area is measured in square units and volume in cubic units; keep every length in the same unit before you calculate. The calculators below do not convert units for you.

Rectangle

Area = l × w. Perimeter = 2(l + w)

Floors, fields and sheets.

Worked example: With Length l = 12, Width w = 5: Area = 60; Perimeter = 34; Diagonal = 13.

Triangle

Area = ½ × base × height. Heron: s = (a + b + c)/2, area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]

Triangles of any kind; Heron's formula needs only the three sides.

The three sides must make a triangle: each side shorter than the other two added together.

Worked example: With Side a = 13, Side b = 14, Side c = 15: Perimeter = 42; Area by Heron = 84.

Triangle from base and height

Area = ½ × b × h

When the height (the straight distance from the base to the opposite corner) is known.

Worked example: With Base b = 10, Height h = 6: Area = 30.

Circle

Circumference = 2πr. Area = πr²

Wheels, plates and round fields. π is about 3.14159.

Worked example: With Radius r = 7: Circumference = 43.9823; Area = 153.938.

Trapezium

Area = ½ × (a + b) × h, where a and b are the parallel sides

Shapes with one pair of parallel sides.

Worked example: With Parallel side a = 8, Parallel side b = 12, Height h = 5: Area = 50.

Cuboid

Volume = l × w × h. Total surface area = 2(lw + wh + hl)

Boxes, rooms and tanks.

Worked example: With Length l = 6, Width w = 4, Height h = 3: Volume = 72; Total surface area = 108; Space diagonal = 7.8102.

Cube

Volume = a³. Total surface area = 6a²

Dice, blocks and cubic tanks.

Worked example: With Edge a = 5: Volume = 125; Total surface area = 150.

Cylinder

Volume = πr²h. Curved surface = 2πrh. Total surface = 2πr(r + h)

Pipes, cans and round tanks.

Worked example: With Radius r = 7, Height h = 10: Volume = 1539.3804; Curved surface = 439.823; Total surface = 747.6991.

Cone

Slant height l = √(r² + h²). Volume = ⅓πr²h. Curved surface = πrl. Total surface = πr(r + l)

Funnels, tents and ice-cream cones.

Worked example: With Radius r = 3, Height h = 4: Slant height l = 5; Volume = 37.6991; Curved surface = 47.1239; Total surface = 75.3982.

Sphere

Volume = 4/3 × πr³. Surface area = 4πr²

Balls and globes.

Worked example: With Radius r = 6: Volume = 904.7787; Surface area = 452.3893.

Hemisphere

Volume = 2/3 × πr³. Curved surface = 2πr². Total surface = 3πr²

Bowls and domes (half of a sphere).

Worked example: With Radius r = 6: Volume = 452.3893; Curved surface = 226.1947; Total surface = 339.292.

Remember by using

  • Radius is half the diameter. Check which one a problem gives.
  • For a cone, the slant height is the sloping side, not the height. The calculator works it out from r and h.
  • Volume of a cone is a third of the cylinder with the same base and height.

These are standard textbook formulas, checked by program against separate code, but check them against your own textbook before an exam. All advice here is our own writing. This site is independent and is not connected with any board, publisher or exam body.
