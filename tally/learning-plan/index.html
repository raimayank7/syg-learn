Learn Tally in 30 days: a daily plan

An hour a day for a month, with your hands on the keyboard for most of it, is enough to do day to day accounting entries with confidence.

Before you begin

  • You need a computer with TallyPrime installed from the official site, and a notebook.
  • You need the basics of accounting: debit and credit, journal entries, what a ledger and a trial balance are. If these are new, spend a week first on learn.shareyourgroup.com/accountancy.
  • Create a practice company (Shanti Traders) and never practise in a real company's data.
  • Each day: read the lesson (15 minutes), do it in Tally (35 minutes), write three lines in your notebook on what you learned (10 minutes).

Week 1: the ground

DayStudyPractise
1What TallyPrime isOpen Tally. Move around with Enter, Esc and Alt+G. Find five reports.
2Create a companyCreate Shanti Traders. Open F11. Shut and reopen the company.
3Groups and ledgersWrite the 28 groups from memory. Do the group quiz twice.
4Create ledgersCreate the practice ledgers. Alter one. View Chart of Accounts.
5Voucher typesOpen each voucher with its key. For twenty transactions on paper, write which voucher you would use.
6ShortcutsLearn the keys marked essential. Work for half an hour without the mouse.
7ReviewCreate a second practice company from memory, with ledgers. Group quiz: aim for 18 of 20.

Week 2: accounting vouchers

DayStudyPractise
8Receipt and ContraEnter capital, a bank deposit, a withdrawal and a bank transfer.
9PaymentEnter ten payments: rent, salary, electricity, a supplier, drawings.
10JournalEnter the eight examples in the lesson.
11Reports: Day Book and ledgerCheck everything entered so far in the Day Book. Alter two vouchers. Delete one.
12Reports: Trial Balance, P&L, Balance SheetDrill down from Balance Sheet to a voucher and back. Add a comparison column.
13Common errorsMake three deliberate mistakes, find them from the reports, and fix them.
14ReviewEnter thirty mixed vouchers from a list you write yourself. Check the Trial Balance by hand.

Week 3: stock and GST

DayStudyPractise
15Stock items and unitsCreate units, groups and the three practice items.
16GST set-upEnable GST. Create tax ledgers. Add state and GSTIN to parties, and rates to items.
17PurchaseEnter a local and an interstate purchase. Check Stock Summary.
18SalesEnter a credit sale, a cash sale and an interstate sale. Print one invoice to PDF.
19Debit and credit notesEnter one sales return and one purchase return against the earlier bills.
20GST entriesWork out the month's tax on paper, pass the set-off journal, and compare with the GSTR-3B report.
21ReviewOutstandings reports: tag every receipt and payment against its bill.

Week 4: putting it together

DayStudyPractise
22Bank reconciliationWrite a made-up bank statement for your entries and reconcile against it.
23Backup and restoreBack up to a pen drive. Restore into a new folder and open it.
24 to 27Practice exerciseIn a fresh company, enter the whole month of transactions. Compare your Trial Balance with ours.
28Reports againFor the exercise company: P&L, Balance Sheet, Stock Summary, Outstandings. Explain each figure aloud.
29GlossarySpeed: enter twenty vouchers against the clock using keys only.
30ReviewDo the month-end routine from the reports lesson. List what you still find hard.

How to know you are ready for work

  • You can create a company, ledgers and items without looking anything up.
  • Given a bill or a slip, you know at once which voucher it is.
  • You can enter a GST sales invoice and a purchase correctly and explain every line.
  • You can find and correct your own mistakes from the reports.
  • You can reconcile a bank account.
  • You take a backup without being reminded.

After the thirty days

Real skill comes from real documents. If you can, work alongside an accountant for a few weeks, even unpaid, entering actual bills under supervision. Learn how that office names ledgers and treats tax, and ask why. Further features (payroll, cost centres, orders, batches, multiple godowns, TDS) are best learned when a job calls for them, from Tally's own help. And keep your accounting theory moving: software changes with every release, while debit and credit do not.

For job seekers

On a resume, say what you can do, not only "Tally": for example "TallyPrime: ledgers, GST sales and purchase invoices, bank reconciliation, Day Book and Trial Balance". Our neighbours career.shareyourgroup.com/resume and career.shareyourgroup.com/interview have a sample resume and interview questions for accounts jobs.

Questions learners ask

How long does it take to learn Tally?

For day to day entries (ledgers, vouchers, GST invoices, basic reports), about a month of an hour's practice a day, if you already know basic accounting.

Can I learn Tally without knowing accounting?

You can learn the screens, but not to use them correctly. Learn debit and credit and journal entries first.

Do I need a paid course to learn Tally?

Not necessarily. With the software, a practice company and regular practice you can learn the basics yourself. A course or working with an accountant helps with real documents and office practice.
