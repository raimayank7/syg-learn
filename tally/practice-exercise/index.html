Tally practice exercise: one month of accounts

22 transactions of Shanti Traders for April, to be entered in a fresh practice company. Then compare your trial balance with ours. The answers for each voucher are below, closed until you open them.

Rates here are assumed. The examples use GST at 18% (CGST 9% + SGST 9%, or IGST 18%) only to show the entries. Actual rates depend on the goods or service and change by notification. For rates and return rules see the official sites gst.gov.in and cbic-gst.gov.in, or ask your accountant.

Set-up

  1. Create a company called Shanti Traders in your own state, with the financial year from 1 April, and inventory and GST switched on.
  2. Create the ledgers listed in the trial balance below, each under the group shown. Mohan Paper Mart and City Book Depot are in your state; Delhi Pen House and Jaipur Stationers are in other states.
  3. Create two stock items: A4 Paper (unit Ream) and Ball Pen (unit Box), with GST at the assumed 18%.
  4. Set up GST with separate input and output tax ledgers.

Key figures

  • Cash in hand: ₹1,17,900. Bank balance: ₹3,08,254.
  • Receivable: City Book Depot ₹8,940; Jaipur Stationers ₹35,400.
  • Payable: Mohan Paper Mart ₹13,100; Delhi Pen House ₹47,200.
  • Input tax credit carried forward: CGST ₹360, SGST ₹360, IGST ₹1,800.
  • Closing stock: 70 reams of paper at ₹250 and 20 boxes of pens at ₹400 = ₹25,500.
  • Net sales: ₹73,000. Net purchases: ₹85,000.
  • Gross profit: sales 73,000 + closing stock 25,500 − purchases 85,000 − carriage inward 1,500 = ₹12,000.
  • Net result: gross profit 12,000 + interest 850 − indirect expenses 24,636 = a net loss of ₹11,786 for the month.

In Tally, check these in the Trial Balance, Stock Summary, Profit & Loss A/c and Balance Sheet. Stock Summary should show the closing quantities above; Tally values them at average cost, which here equals the purchase rates.

If your figures differ

  1. Find the first ledger whose balance differs from the table.
  2. Open it in Tally (Alt+G, Ledger Vouchers).
  3. Compare each entry with the answers that involve that ledger.
  4. Correct the voucher from the ledger by pressing Enter on it.
  5. Return to the Trial Balance and move to the next difference.

The usual causes are a wrong date, a reversed contra, a tax line left out of a return, a supplier payment entered as a purchase, or an interstate party set up in your own state. The lesson on common errors lists the signs of each.

Going further with the same data

  • Do the bank reconciliation: assume the cheque of 24 April for ₹40,000 has not been presented by 30 April. The bank statement balance should then be ₹3,48,254.
  • Open the Outstandings reports and check that the receipt and the payment are tagged against the right bills.
  • Take a backup, restore it to a new folder, and confirm the trial balance is the same.
  • Enter May: invent ten transactions of your own, work out the trial balance on paper first, and see whether Tally agrees with you.

Questions learners ask

My trial balance does not match. What should I do?

Compare ledger by ledger with the table. For any ledger that differs, open it in Tally and tick its entries against the answers for the transactions that use it. The unticked entry is the mistake.

Why is there a loss in the first month?

The business paid a month of rent and salaries while it was still building up sales, and much of what it bought is still in stock. That is common for a new shop.

Do I have to use GST for this exercise?

It is written with GST at an assumed 18% so that you practise tax entries. You could first do it without tax to practise the vouchers alone; your figures will then differ.
