Reports in TallyPrime and how to read them

Vouchers go in; reports come out. Knowing which report answers which question is what turns a data entry operator into an accountant.

Which report answers which question

What was entered today?Day BookGateway of Tally > Day Book
What does this party owe? What happened in this account?Ledger VouchersDisplay More Reports > Account Books > Ledger
How much cash and bank do we have?Cash/Bank BooksDisplay More Reports > Account Books > Cash/Bank Book(s)

The quickest way to any of them is Alt+G (Go To): type a few letters of the report's name and press Enter.

Keys that work in every report

  • F2 changes the date; Alt+F2 changes the period.
  • Enter drills down: from a group to its ledgers, from a ledger to its months, from a month to its vouchers, from a voucher to its alteration screen. Esc comes back up.
  • Alt+F1 or Alt+F5 shows the report in detailed or condensed form.
  • Alt+C adds a column, for another period, another company or a different valuation, for comparison.
  • Ctrl+B (Basis of Values) and Ctrl+H (Change View) change what is shown and how.
  • F12 opens the configuration of the report: what to show and in what format.
  • Ctrl+P prints, Ctrl+E exports, Ctrl+M emails the report.

Day Book

The list of all vouchers for a day or period, in order, with type, number and amounts. Use it at the end of each day to check your own work: every bill and slip on your desk should appear once. From here you open a voucher with Enter to alter it, delete with Alt+D, or duplicate with Alt+2.

Ledger

All entries in one account with a running balance. For a party it is the statement of account you send to the customer. For an expense it shows what makes up the total. If a balance looks wrong, this is where you look first.

Trial Balance

All groups, or with Alt+F1 all ledgers, with debit and credit closing balances. In Tally the two totals always agree, because the software will not save an unbalanced voucher. So here the Trial Balance is not a test of arithmetic. It is a test of sense. Read down it and ask of every line: should this ledger have a balance of this size, on this side?

  • Cash with a credit balance: impossible in reality. A receipt is missing.
  • A customer with a credit balance, or a supplier with a debit balance: an advance, or an entry on the wrong side.
  • Suspense with any balance: something is unresolved.
  • An expense with a credit balance: a wrong entry.
  • Two ledgers that look like the same thing.

Profit and Loss Account

Tally shows it in two parts. The upper part works out gross profit from opening stock, purchases, direct expenses, sales, direct incomes and closing stock. The lower part adds indirect incomes and deducts indirect expenses to reach net profit. Drill into any figure to see its ledgers and vouchers. Alt+C with last year's period gives a comparison. A vertical layout is available from the report's configuration.

Balance Sheet

Liabilities (capital, loans, current liabilities) on one side and assets (fixed assets, investments, current assets) on the other, on the date chosen. The profit for the period appears with the capital side. Check three things: that closing stock is sensible, that "Difference in opening balances" does not appear, and that no asset shows a negative figure.

Outstandings

Receivables lists each unpaid sales bill by party with its due date and days overdue; Payables does the same for suppliers. These depend on bill-wise references having been tagged correctly in sales, purchases, receipts and payments. An ageing view groups the dues into ranges of days.

A month-end checking routine

  1. Day Book for the month: nothing missing, nothing doubled.
  2. Bank reconciliation for each bank.
  3. Cash book: balance agrees with cash actually counted.
  4. Trial Balance: read every line for sense.
  5. Receivables and Payables: follow up overdue bills.
  6. Stock Summary: no negative stock; spot-check a few items physically.
  7. GST reports: no vouchers listed with errors.
  8. Take a backup.

Questions learners ask

How do I see the Day Book in TallyPrime?

Choose Day Book from Gateway of Tally, or press Alt+G and type "day book". Press Alt+F2 to change the period.

Why does the Trial Balance always tally in Tally?

Because Tally does not save a voucher unless its debits equal its credits. Use the Trial Balance to check whether balances make sense, not whether the totals agree.

How do I compare this year with last year?

In the report, press Alt+C to add a column and choose the other period.
