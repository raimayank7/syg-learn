Lesson 3 of 20

Groups and ledgers: which ledger goes under which group

A ledger is an account. A group is the shelf it sits on. Put a ledger on the wrong shelf and it turns up in the wrong place in the balance sheet.

Ledger and group

In Tally every account is called a ledger: Cash, State Bank of India, Rent, Sales, Ramesh & Sons. Every ledger must be placed under a group, and the group tells Tally what kind of account it is: asset, liability, income or expense, and where it appears in the final statements. This is how Tally knows, without being told each time, that rent belongs in the Profit and Loss Account and furniture in the Balance Sheet.

The 28 predefined groups

Tally comes with 28 ready groups: 15 primary groups and 13 sub-groups that sit under some of them. You can create more of your own under these, but you rarely need to at first.

No ledger in our list matches. Think about its nature: is it something owned, something owed, an income or an expense? Then pick the group of that nature.

How to decide for a ledger not in the list

  1. Is it a person or firm you trade with? A customer goes under Sundry Debtors; a supplier under Sundry Creditors.
  2. Is it something the business owns? For long use: Fixed Assets. Money, stock or amounts receivable soon: a sub-group of Current Assets.
  3. Is it something the business owes? To the owner: Capital Account. A loan: Loans (Liability). Bills and dues payable soon: Current Liabilities.
  4. Is it an expense? If it is spent on buying the goods or making them ready for sale (freight inward, wages of production, power for the factory): Direct Expenses. Everything else (office, selling, finance): Indirect Expenses.
  5. Is it an income? From selling goods: Sales Accounts. Other income connected with trading: Direct Incomes. Interest, commission, discount received: Indirect Incomes.

Why direct and indirect matter

Ledgers under Purchase Accounts, Sales Accounts, Direct Expenses and Direct Incomes are used to work out gross profit. Ledgers under Indirect Expenses and Indirect Incomes come after that, to give net profit. Putting carriage inward under Indirect Expenses does not change the net profit, but it gives a wrong gross profit, and a careful owner or auditor will notice.

Creating your own groups

When a business wants finer reporting, groups can be created under the predefined ones: for example "Debtors - North" and "Debtors - South" under Sundry Debtors, or "Administrative Expenses" and "Selling Expenses" under Indirect Expenses. The path is Gateway of Tally > Create > Group. Do this only when there is a clear need; too many groups make the reports harder to read.

Test yourself

The group quiz shows a ledger name and asks you to pick its group. Ten minutes on it fixes this lesson better than re-reading. Then go on to creating ledgers.

Questions learners ask

How many groups are there in Tally?

TallyPrime has 28 predefined groups: 15 primary groups and 13 sub-groups. You can create more under them.

Under which group does a bank account go?

Under Bank Accounts, a sub-group of Current Assets. A bank overdraft or cash credit account goes under Bank OD A/c.

What is the difference between direct and indirect expenses in Tally?

Direct expenses relate to buying or producing goods and affect gross profit. Indirect expenses are office, selling and finance costs and affect net profit.
