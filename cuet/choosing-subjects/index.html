How to choose your CUET UG subjects

The most important decision in CUET is which papers to take. The bulletin leaves the choice to you, but it also says the choice should follow the eligibility rules of the programme and university you want. This guide is our own advice on how to decide.

Start from the programme, not the paper

Write down three to five programmes and universities you may apply to. Open each university's admission page and find which CUET papers it accepts for each programme. Some ask for a specific domain subject, some ask for a language, and some accept the General Aptitude Test. The bulletin says each university sets its own requirements.

Make a table

ProgrammeUniversityPapers it asks forPapers I already know
B.Com (example)Your choiceAccountancy or Business Studies, Economics or Mathematics, a language, GATFill in your own
B.A. (example)Your choiceA language, two domain subjects such as History or Political Science, GATFill in your own
B.Sc. (example)Your choicePhysics, Chemistry, and Mathematics or Biology, plus a language or GATFill in your own

These example rows only show how to lay out the table; they are not rules. Each university's page has the real list.

Count the papers and the fee

You can take up to five papers. The fee is lowest for up to three, and each extra paper costs more (see fees). Take every paper you need for your top choices, and think twice about extras you do not need.

Match papers to your strengths

  • Choose domain subjects you studied well in Class 12, even though the bulletin allows others.
  • The General Aptitude Test is open to everyone and rewards practice in arithmetic and reasoning; our practice sets are for that.
  • Choose a language paper in a language you read comfortably; the test is on reading comprehension and vocabulary.

Keep the schedule in mind

The bulletin says papers are held in multiple shifts. Your five papers may fall on different days. Plan revision accordingly, and keep the timetable that the admit card gives.

Check, then check again

Papers cannot be changed freely after the correction window. Before you submit, compare your choice list with the university pages one more time, and show it to a teacher or parent.
