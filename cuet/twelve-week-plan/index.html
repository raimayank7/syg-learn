CUET UG twelve-week study plan

This is a twelve-week plan you can adjust. It assumes you have read the books once and have a few hours on school days. It does not promise a score. Change it to fit your boards and your own strengths.

Weeks 1 to 4: cover the ground

  • List your papers and the topics of each from the NTA syllabus.
  • Give more time to the papers you like least, but keep a little time for every paper.
  • After each topic, do 20 questions and note the wrong ones.

Weeks 5 to 8: build speed

  • Take one timed 50-question paper a week for each of your papers in turn.
  • Spend a day reviewing mistakes.
  • Do 15 to 20 General Aptitude Test questions every day (see our practice sets).

Weeks 9 to 11: sharpen

  • Two timed papers a week, with a full review next day.
  • Revise from your own notes and mistake notebook.
  • Fix your rule for guessing (see negative marking).

Week 12: calm and ready

  • One light paper early in the week. No new topics in the last three days.
  • Check the admit card, the shift timings for each paper, the route and the ID.
  • Sleep on schedule.

Working backwards

Once NTA announces the dates, count the weeks. If you have fewer than twelve, shorten the first block. Do not neglect your board examinations.

Tracking

Keep a table of week, paper, score, accuracy and the two most frequent mistakes. If the trend is flat after four weeks, change one thing.

If you take five papers

Five papers is a lot of ground. Divide your weekly time in proportion to how far you are from ready in each paper, but give every paper at least one session a week so that nothing fades. Put the papers with the earliest dates first in the last weeks. Keep a one-page chart on the wall with each paper, its date and shift, and the topics still weak.

If you take fewer papers

With two or three papers you have room to go deeper. Use the extra time for full-length timed practice and for a second reading of the textbooks, and for the General Aptitude Test if you have chosen it.
