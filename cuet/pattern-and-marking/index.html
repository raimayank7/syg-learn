CUET UG Guide

CUET UG pattern and marking scheme

CUET (UG), the Common University Entrance Test for undergraduate programmes, is run by the National Testing Agency (NTA). Unlike a single-paper entrance test, it is a set of separate subject papers, and you choose which ones to take. This page gives the pattern from the CUET (UG) 2026 bulletin.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin CUET (UG) 2026, from cuet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the CUET site showed documents for CUET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these are the latest figures we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check cuet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any university.

Broad features

FeatureWhat the bulletin says
Subjects offered37: 13 languages, 23 domain-specific subjects and 1 General Aptitude Test
How many you can takeUp to 5 subjects, including languages and the General Aptitude Test
Questions per paper50, all compulsory
Type of questionObjective multiple choice
Duration of each paper60 minutes (PwBD candidates get 20 extra minutes for each hour)
ModeComputer Based Test (CBT)
ShiftsMultiple shifts, depending on the number of candidates and subject choices
Medium13 Indian languages: Assamese, Bengali, English, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Punjabi, Odia, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu

Marking

What you doMarks
Correct answer+5
Incorrect answer−1
Unanswered0

A full paper is therefore out of 250 marks (50 × 5). The bulletin states negative marking of one mark for each incorrect answer.

What each kind of paper tests

  • Language papers test reading comprehension on different types of passage (factual, literary and narrative), literary aptitude and vocabulary.
  • Domain-specific subjects follow the NCERT syllabus, as the bulletin says. The detailed syllabus is published separately by NTA.
  • General Aptitude Test covers general knowledge, current affairs, general mental ability, numerical ability, quantitative reasoning (simple application of basic arithmetic, algebra, geometry, mensuration and statistics) and logical and analytical reasoning.

Choosing the papers

The bulletin says you may choose subjects irrespective of what you studied in Class 12, but should choose according to the eligibility criteria of the programme and university you want. Fees depend on the number of subjects. See our pages on choosing subjects and fees.

For your own practice, the marks calculator totals up to five papers.
