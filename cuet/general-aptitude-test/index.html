CUET General Aptitude Test: what it covers

The General Aptitude Test (code 501) is the one CUET paper that is not tied to a school subject. The bulletin says it covers general knowledge, current affairs, general mental ability, numerical ability, quantitative reasoning (simple application of basic mathematical concepts: arithmetic, algebra, geometry, mensuration and statistics) and logical and analytical reasoning. This page explains how to prepare each part.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin CUET (UG) 2026, from cuet.nta.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. On that day the CUET site showed documents for CUET (UG) 2026 and none for 2027, so these are the latest figures we could read. Rules can change by public notice: check cuet.nta.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any university.

Numerical ability and quantitative reasoning

These are school mathematics up to about Class 10, used in short, practical questions. The usual topics are percentages, ratio and proportion, averages, simple interest, speed and distance, profit and loss, area and perimeter, and HCF and LCM. Speed comes from knowing the formulas by heart and from practice. Our quantitative practice set is a start.

Logical and analytical reasoning

Typical topics are number series, direction sense, clocks, calendars, ages and odd-one-out. The way to improve is to do many small questions and to write the working down. Our reasoning practice set covers several of these.

General knowledge and current affairs

There is no fixed syllabus for these parts. Read a good newspaper daily, note a few facts in a notebook and revise them weekly. Be careful with facts: use official sources and recent news. We do not offer current-affairs practice on this site, because we could not tie it to a source that stays correct.

Mental ability and verbal reasoning

The bulletin lists general mental ability. In practice this means puzzles, pattern recognition and short reasoning questions. Regular practice with small puzzles helps.

How to work through the 60 minutes

  • The paper has 50 questions in 60 minutes, so about 72 seconds each. Our time planner shows the arithmetic.
  • Do the questions you can do at once first; mark the others and come back.
  • A wrong answer costs 1 mark and a right answer earns 5, so a guess among four options has a positive expected value, but accuracy still matters most. See negative marking.

Our practice sets are original and easier than real questions. Use them for speed and habit, and do full-length practice from NTA material for a true measure.
