How to prepare for CUET UG

CUET preparation is different from preparing for a single-paper entrance test, because you take several papers and each is only 60 minutes. This guide is our own writing. It does not promise a score.

1. Fix your list of papers early

Everything depends on which papers you take. Use our guide to choosing subjects and your target universities' pages to fix the list as early as you can.

2. Treat each paper as a 60-minute sprint

Fifty questions in sixty minutes is a little over a minute each. Practise short, timed sets for each paper rather than long study sessions only.

3. Domain subjects: textbooks first

The bulletin says domain papers follow the NCERT syllabus. Read the NCERT textbook for each subject line by line, make short notes, then practise questions. The NTA syllabus document lists the topics.

4. The language paper: read widely

Reading comprehension improves with daily reading. Read short passages of different types, factual, literary and narrative, and practise answering questions on them. Build vocabulary through reading, not just through word lists.

5. General Aptitude Test: little and often

Arithmetic and reasoning questions get faster with practice. Do 15 to 20 questions a day. See the General Aptitude Test guide and our practice sets.

6. Keep a mistake notebook

Write each wrong question, the right idea and why you slipped. Reread it weekly.

7. Look after yourself

Several papers over several days is tiring. Sleep, eat and rest. If the pressure is heavy, talk to someone you trust.

Read next: negative marking, the twelve-week plan and common mistakes.

A word on coaching and books

You do not need many books. For domain subjects, the school textbook named in the NTA syllabus is the main resource. For the General Aptitude Test, one book of arithmetic and reasoning practice, or our free sets, is enough to start. Extra material is useful only if you finish what you have and still want more.

Keep a weekly review

Once a week, sit down with your mistake notebook and your timetable. Ask three questions: which paper am I weakest in, which kind of mistake do I repeat, and what will I do differently next week? Write the answers in one line each.
