CUET UG time planner

Set the minutes for a final check. The tool shows the seconds a question gets in the 60-minute paper.

The numbers behind it

Each paper has 50 questions in 60 minutes. The planner takes your final-check and passage-reading minutes off 60 and divides the rest by 50.

How the minutes divide

Each CUET paper has 50 questions in 60 minutes. The table shows the time per question for different amounts kept for a final check.

Even with nothing kept back, you have 72 seconds a question. Reading-comprehension papers often have longer questions, so you may spend more on those and less on short ones.

Three sample plans

Here are three ways to spend the 60 minutes. They are examples, not rules. Try each in a timed paper and see which suits you.

  • Straight through: 55 minutes for the 50 questions in order, 5 minutes to go back to marked ones.
  • Two passes: 30 minutes to answer every question you can do at once, 25 minutes to return to the rest, 5 minutes to check marking.
  • Hard first (not usually advised): start with the hardest questions. This can cost time if the hardest questions take longer than expected.

Whichever you pick, glance at the clock every ten questions. If you are behind, speed up by skipping the next hard question, not by reading faster.

Questions people ask

Is every paper 60 minutes?

The bulletin says each test paper is 60 minutes, with extra time for PwBD candidates (20 minutes for each hour).

How long should I keep for a final check?

Find out by practice. Try a few values in timed papers.
