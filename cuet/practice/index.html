CUET General Aptitude Test practice

Original four-option questions with +5 and −1 marking. Three sets: quantitative, reasoning and mixed.

  • QuantitativeTen questions, +5 and −1 marking
  • ReasoningTen questions, +5 and −1 marking
  • Mixed: both kindsTen questions, +5 and −1 marking

Why only the General Aptitude Test?

The language papers test reading comprehension of real passages, and the domain papers test school subjects. Questions for those need real passages and subject text, and we do not copy text from anyone. The General Aptitude Test is different: its numerical and reasoning questions can be made honestly by a program and checked by a second program, so those are the ones we offer.

What a good practice habit looks like

Ten minutes a day with a set, done honestly, is worth more than a long session once a week. Keep the same time and place, and write your score and your two worst slips in a notebook. After two weeks look back: you will see which slips repeat.

What the sets are for

Practice builds habits that the CUET marking rewards: working carefully, checking the unit, and deciding when to skip. Each round has ten questions with four options. The topics follow what the bulletin lists for the General Aptitude Test: numerical ability, quantitative reasoning and logical reasoning.

Our questions are original and easier than the real thing. They are made by a small program, so there is no question bank to memorise, and each answer is checked by a second program when the site is built. We do not offer practice for the language papers (reading comprehension) or the domain subjects (NCERT syllabus), because their questions need real passages and subject text that we cannot make honestly by program.

How to use them

  1. Pick a set. Start with the one you like least.
  2. Do a round of ten without help. Skip when you are unsure.
  3. Read the working for every wrong answer and note the reason.
  4. Repeat the next day. Aim for fewer wrong answers.
