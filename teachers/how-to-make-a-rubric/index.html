How to make a good rubric: criteria, levels, descriptions

This is our own advice.

Choose few criteria

Three to five criteria are usually enough. A rubric with twelve rows is hard to mark and hard for pupils to remember.

Describe each level in plain words

LevelWeak descriptionBetter description
GoodQuite good contentIncludes three or more relevant facts, each explained in a sentence
BeginningPoor contentIncludes one or two facts with no explanation

Share it first

Give pupils the rubric before they start so that they know what a good piece looks like. Ask them to mark their own work against it before handing it in.

Use it for feedback

Circle the level for each criterion and add one comment saying what would move the work up a level.

Do not over-trust the total

A total score can hide which criterion is weak. Look at the pattern across the rows, not only at the sum.

The comments and advice here are our own wording, not official guidance, and they are starting points: change every comment so that it is true of the child in front of you.
