How to write report comments that parents understand

This is our own advice.

Start with something true and specific

"Works hard" says little. "Has taken on a longer story this term and finished it" says something. One specific example makes a comment feel written for the child.

Give one next step

Parents can act on one thing: "Read for ten minutes each evening" is better than a list of five.

A simple shape for each comment

PartExample
What the child can doreads aloud with growing confidence
Evidenceespecially in the group reading on Fridays
Next stepa short book each week at home will help

Keep the words plain

  • Avoid jargon such as "working towards expectations" without saying what it means in practice.
  • Say "is learning to" rather than "struggles with" when you can do so honestly.
  • Check the name, the pronoun and the spelling before you send.

Be honest and kind

Do not hide a real concern. Say it gently, say what you will do, and invite the parent to talk.

The comments and advice here are our own wording, not official guidance, and they are starting points: change every comment so that it is true of the child in front of you.
