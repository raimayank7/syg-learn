Maths Olympiad syllabus: algebra, geometry, number theory

HBCSE says anyone aiming at the International Mathematical Olympiads (IMO, EGMO, APMO) and the domestic selection rounds (RMO and INMO) has to be familiar with all the topics in the NCERT Mathematics books of Classes VIII, IX and X, and also with the areas below. It also says the problems come mainly from algebra, combinatorics, geometry and number theory, and that the difficulty rises from RMO to INMO to IMO.

Areas listed by HBCSE

AreaTopics HBCSE lists
AlgebraInequalities, progressions (A.P., G.P., H.P.), theory of indices, systems of linear equations, theory of equations, binomial theorem and properties of binomial coefficients, complex numbers, polynomials in one and two variables, functional equations, sequences
Plane geometryTriangles, quadrilaterals, circles and their properties, standard Euclidean constructions, concurrency and collinearity (theorems of Ceva and Menelaus), basic trigonometric identities, compound, multiple and sub-multiple angles, general solutions, sine rule, cosine rule, properties of triangles and polygons, coordinate geometry (straight line, circle, conics, 3-D geometry), vectors
CombinatoricsBasic enumeration, pigeonhole principle and its applications, recursion, elementary graph theory
Number theoryDivisibility in the integers (division algorithm, greatest common divisor, Euclidean algorithm, the Diophantine equation ax + by = c), the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, basic properties of congruence, linear congruences, Chinese Remainder Theorem, Fermat’s Little Theorem, Wilson’s Theorem, Euler’s phi function and its generalisation of Fermat’s theorem, Pythagorean triples, Diophantine equations

HBCSE adds an important warning: the topics listed do not make an exhaustive list, and problems in RMO and INMO may include topics not stated. It also lists recommended books for each area and points to the question papers of earlier years on its site.

How the areas link

Many problems cross areas: a counting problem may need number theory, and a geometry problem may need algebra. Do not study the four areas in separate boxes. After finishing a topic, look for a problem from another area that uses it.

Where to start

Start with the NCERT books of Classes VIII to X, as HBCSE asks, then number theory and algebra, which need the fewest prerequisites, and then geometry and combinatorics.
