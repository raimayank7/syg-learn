Maths Olympiad in India: IOQM, RMO, INMO to the IMO

The Mathematical Olympiad programme in India, which leads to Indian students taking part in the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO), is organised by the Homi Bhabha Centre for Science Education (HBCSE) for the National Board for Higher Mathematics (NBHM) of the Department of Atomic Energy. HBCSE says its main purpose is to spot mathematical talent among pre-university students.

The five stages

StageWhat it isFacts from HBCSE
1IOQM, Indian Olympiad Qualifier in Mathematics (earlier called PRMO)A three-hour examination with 30 questions. Each answer is a one-digit or two-digit number marked on an OMR sheet. Question paper in English and Hindi.
2RMO, Regional Mathematical OlympiadA three-hour examination with six problems, each needing a written proof. Offered in English, Hindi and regional languages.
3INMO, Indian National Mathematical OlympiadHeld on the third Sunday of January. For the 2026 cycle HBCSE gives 17 January 2027, 12:00 to 16:30 (4.5 hours), six problems with proofs.
4IMOTC, International Mathematical Olympiad Training CampThe top students from INMO (about 65) are invited to a camp from April to May; selection tests choose six students.
5IMOThe team of six goes to the International Mathematical Olympiad, with four teachers or mentors.

Between RMO and INMO, regional training camps (INMOTC) train students for INMO. A pre-departure camp of 8 to 10 days is held for the IMO team.

What the stages ask

  • Stage 1 asks for numerical answers, so a correct method with a slip in arithmetic earns nothing: accuracy matters.
  • Stages 2 and 3 ask for full written proofs. The notice for RMO 2026 says each question requires writing detailed proof.
  • HBCSE says the difficulty rises from RMO to INMO to IMO.

Where our practice fits

Our practice sets are in the style of Stage 1: a question with a numerical answer from 0 to 99. They are much easier than real IOQM questions and they cannot practise proof writing, which is the heart of Stages 2 and 3.
