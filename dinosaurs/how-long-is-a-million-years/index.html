How long is a million years?

The dinosaur pages say things like "83.6 million years ago". A million is a very big number, so here are ways to picture it.

Counting up to a million

  • One million seconds is about 11.6 days, a bit over eleven days.
  • One million minutes is about 1.9 years.
  • One million hours is about 114 years.
  • One million days is about 2,738 years.

Lifetimes

If a person lives 80 years, one million years is 12,500 lifetimes laid end to end.

Squeeze time into seconds

Pretend that one million years takes just one second. Then the whole Mesozoic Era, which the database dates from 251.902 to 66 million years ago, would last about 186 seconds (about 3 minutes 6 seconds), and the time from the end of the Cretaceous until today would last 66 seconds.

Why it matters for the pages

When a page says that a genus could have lasted "at most 17.6 million years", that is a window larger than any person could count to in a lifetime if they said one number every second without sleeping. It does not mean that every animal lived that long. A dinosaur lived for some years, a genus for a long time, and the fossil dates only show the window.

Numbers on this page are plain arithmetic: they use 365.25 days in a year, and a 24-hour day.
