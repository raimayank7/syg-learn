Centrosaurus: dinosaur facts from the fossil records

Centrosaurus is a dinosaur genus in the family Ceratopsidae. A genus is a group of very closely related kinds of animal. This page shows what the Paleobiology Database says about it, and no more.

When is it found?

The database puts the first appearance of Centrosaurus between 83.6 and 72.2 million years ago, in the Campanian interval, which lasted from 83.6 to 72.2 million years ago. It puts the last appearance between 83.6 and 66 million years ago, in the Maastrichtian interval (72.2 to 66 million years ago). Its earliest and its latest interval both belong to the Cretaceous Period.

If we take the oldest possible start and the youngest possible end, Centrosaurus could have lasted at most 17.6 million years. That is the widest window the fossil dates allow. Fossils are rare, so the fossils that were found do not show every year in which the animal lived. For a feel of the size of the number, one million years is 12,500 lifetimes of 80 years.

How many fossils?

The database holds 57 fossil records filed under Centrosaurus. A record is one report that fossils of this genus were found. More records usually mean a genus has been found more often or studied more, and they do not tell us how many animals lived. 11 of the 56 genera on this site have more records than Centrosaurus.

What did it eat?

The database lists the diet of Centrosaurus as "herbivore". Herbivore means an animal that eats plants. Nobody ever watched this animal eat. Scientists work diet out from clues such as teeth, jaws and the shape of the body, so a diet in a database is an interpretation, not something anyone saw.

Its family and neighbours in time

Centrosaurus is in the family Ceratopsidae. A family is a group of genera that scientists think are closely related. The other genera on this site in the same family are Pachyrhinosaurus, Triceratops.

The possible time windows of 37 other genera on this site overlap with the window of Centrosaurus: Alamosaurus, Albertosaurus, Ankylosaurus, Bagaceratops, Carcharodontosaurus, Chirostenotes, Daspletosaurus, Edmontonia, Edmontosaurus, Euoplocephalus, Gallimimus, Gorgosaurus, Gryposaurus, Hypacrosaurus, Kritosaurus, Megalosaurus, Neuquensaurus, Ornithomimus, Pachyrhinosaurus, Panoplosaurus, Pinacosaurus, Prosaurolophus, Protoceratops, Rhabdodon, Richardoestesia, Saurolophus, Saurornitholestes, Spinosaurus, Struthiomimus, Struthiosaurus, Tarbosaurus, Telmatosaurus, Thescelosaurus, Triceratops, Troodon, Tyrannosaurus, Velociraptor. Overlap in the data does not show that two animals met. The windows are long, and animals can live on different continents in the same period.

What the data does not say

The database gives nothing about the size, colour, sound or behaviour of Centrosaurus, so this page does not either. Scientists usually cannot learn a dinosaur's sound or its exact skin colour from bones, and we will not make them up.
