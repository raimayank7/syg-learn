UPSC Prelims negative marking: the one-third rule

The notice says that for each wrong answer in the objective papers one-third (0.33) of the marks assigned to that question is deducted. If you give more than one answer it counts as wrong, even if one of them is right. A blank has no penalty. This changes how you should choose which questions to attempt.

What a question is worth

If a question carries 2 marks, a wrong answer costs two-thirds of a mark; if it carries 1 mark, it costs one-third. Whatever the marks, the ratio is the same: one right answer cancels three wrong ones. We call a question 1 mark in the examples so the numbers are simple.

The arithmetic of a guess

What you can rule outChance of a right answerAverage result of guessing
Nothing (blind guess among 4)1 in 40.25 × 1 − 0.75 × 1/3 = 0.00
One option1 in 30.333 × 1 − 0.667 × 1/3 = +0.11
Two options1 in 20.5 × 1 − 0.5 × 1/3 = +0.33

A blind guess has an average value of exactly zero: it neither helps nor hurts on average. Ruling out even one option makes guessing worth a little on average, but only on average. A paper has a limited number of questions, so luck matters less than reading each question carefully.

A worked example

CandidateAttemptedRightWrongMarks (1 each, 1/3 off)
A60481248 − 12/3 = 44.00
B80562456 − 24/3 = 48.00
C90543654 − 36/3 = 42.00

Candidate B attempted more than A and did better. Candidate C attempted still more at lower accuracy and did worse than both. So the right number to attempt depends on your accuracy, and you find your own number by practising under the real rule.

A rule of thumb (our advice, not the Commission's)

  • Answer every question you can justify.
  • Guess only when you can rule out at least one option.
  • Do not leave a question blank just because you are afraid: a reasoned guess has a positive average.
  • Do not guess blindly out of panic at the end: it adds nothing on average and adds variation.
