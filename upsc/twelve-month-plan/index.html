UPSC twelve-month plan: a study schedule for 2027

This is a twelve-month plan of our own. It is an example to adjust, not an official schedule. It counts back from the Preliminary Examination of 23 May 2027 in the 2027 calendar, which is liable to alteration, so check the notice of 13 January 2027 when it comes.

MonthsFocusWhat to do
1 to 2Orientation and basicsRead the notice. Get the basic textbooks. Start a daily newspaper habit. Take one CSAT paper to see where you stand.
3 to 5Core subjects, first readingHistory, geography, polity, economy, environment and science. Make short notes. Begin one answer a day.
6 to 7Optional subject and ethicsFirst reading of both optional papers. Case studies for the ethics paper.
8 to 9Revision and testsSecond reading of notes. Preliminary mock papers with the one-third penalty. CSAT practice three times a week.
10Preliminary pushMocks every three days. Review every wrong answer. Light revision only of your own notes.
11 to 12Preliminary and afterAfter the examination, rest two days, then begin answer writing and optional revision for the Main Examination.

Each week

  • One full mock or sectional test
  • One review of the mistake notebook
  • Five answers written with a timer
  • One day off

If you have less time

Drop the second reading, not the practice. Practice shows you what you do not know, while a second reading shows you what you already do.

If you are working

Plan fewer hours but keep them fixed, for example one hour before work and two hours after, and use the weekend for mocks. Regular hours beat bursts.

Source: Union Public Service Commission, Examination Notice No. 05/2026-CSE dated 4 February 2026 for the Civil Services Examination 2026, and the UPSC programme of examinations for 2027 (as on 20 May 2026), from upsc.gov.in, read on 14 November 2026. The notice for 2027 was not out on that day, so figures for 2027 are 2026 figures unless the page says otherwise. Rules can change: check upsc.gov.in and the Rules published by the Government. A sample weekday

  1. Early morning, 90 minutes: one core subject, reading and short notes.
  2. Midday or evening, 60 minutes: newspaper and three lines of notes on the main item.
  3. Evening, 60 minutes: numeracy and reasoning practice, or one answer written with a timer.
  4. Night, 20 minutes: review the day and list the next three tasks.

When you fall behind

Do not try to catch up by doubling the next day. Drop the least valuable task of the week and keep the rest. Practice and revision beat a new chapter at this stage.

The last two weeks before Prelims

Stop starting new books. Revise your own notes, take a mock every three days under the real marking rule, and keep sleep regular.
