UPSC Civil Services exam pattern: Prelims and Mains

The Civil Services Examination (CSE) is held by the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) to fill posts in the services listed on the services page. The notice says it has two successive stages: a Preliminary Examination (objective type), which only screens candidates, and a Main Examination, which has a written part and an Interview or Personality Test. Your final rank comes only from the Main Examination.

Stage 1: Preliminary Examination

  • Two compulsory papers of 200 marks each, both objective type (multiple choice), each of two hours.
  • The question papers are set in Hindi and English.
  • General Studies Paper II is qualifying only, with minimum qualifying marks of 33% (66 of 200).
  • The marks of the Preliminary Examination are not counted for the final order of merit.
  • The list for the Main Examination is drawn from the 33% mark in Paper II and a cut-off in Paper I that the Commission decides.
  • The notice says about twelve to thirteen times the number of vacancies are admitted to the Main Examination.
  • A candidate has to appear in both papers, or is disqualified.

Negative marking

For each wrong answer, one-third (0.33) of the marks assigned to that question is deducted. If more than one answer is given, it counts as wrong even if one of them is right. A blank costs nothing. See our page on negative marking.

Stage 2: Main Examination

The written examination has nine papers of conventional (essay) type, each of three hours. Two are qualifying; the other seven count for rank.

Written total for rank: 7 × 250 = 1,750 marks. The Interview or Personality Test is 275 marks, so the grand total for the Main Examination is 2,025 marks.

The qualifying papers

Paper A and Paper B are of matriculation (Class X) or equivalent standard. The notice says the papers on Essay, General Studies and the Optional subject of a candidate are taken into account only if the candidate scores at least 25% in the Indian language paper and 25% in the English paper. Paper A is not compulsory for candidates from Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Sikkim, nor, with the conditions in the notice, for certain candidates with a hearing impairment.

Language of answers

Apart from Papers A and B, a candidate can write the papers in any language of the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution or in English. The optional papers may also be written in English.

The Interview or Personality Test

About twice the number of vacancies are called. It carries 275 marks with no minimum qualifying mark. Marks from the written part and the interview together decide the final ranking, and candidates are allotted to services by rank and by the preferences they give.

If marks tie

The notice sets an order: first, more marks in the common papers (Essay and the four General Studies papers) together with the Personality Test; then more marks in those five common papers; then the candidate who is senior in age; and after that the Commission decides.
