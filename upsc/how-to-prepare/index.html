How to prepare for UPSC Civil Services

This is our own advice, built on the facts the notice gives: a two-paper screening test, a nine-paper written Main Examination of 3 hours each, and an interview of 275 marks. We cannot promise results, and we do not claim an official source for the advice.

1. Read the notice first

The notice is long (it has 159 pages in the version we read) but most of it is syllabus. Read the first 30 pages for the rules and the scheme, then skim the General Studies syllabus once. Print the General Studies syllabus and tick each head as you cover it.

2. Build one base for both stages

The Preliminary General Studies paper and the Main General Studies papers share ground: history, geography, polity, economy, environment and science. A first reading that is deep enough for the Main Examination also serves the Preliminary, so do not prepare them as separate subjects.

3. Make the CSAT paper safe

Paper II is qualifying at 33%, but it can still stop a good General Studies score, because the notice makes a candidate with less than 33% in Paper II ineligible for the Main Examination. Spend a steady half hour a day on numeracy, data reading and reasoning, and on comprehension passages, until your accuracy is dependable.

4. Write, not just read

The Main Examination is conventional (essay) type, with three hours a paper. Start writing answers early, with a time limit. Reading alone does not train you to write a clear answer in the time available. The notice itself says answers must be relevant, meaningful and succinct.

5. Read the newspaper with a purpose

Current events appear in Paper I and sit behind many Main questions. Read one newspaper for an hour a day, and at the end of each day write three lines in your own words on the most important item and the area of the syllabus it touches.

6. Choose the optional early

Two papers of 250 marks each is a large share of the 1,750 written marks. See the optional subjects page for how to choose.

7. Practise the rule of the paper

Practise Preliminary papers with the one-third penalty, and Main papers with a stopwatch. Use the marks calculator after each mock.

8. Look after yourself

The exam is a long road. A regular sleep time, a daily walk and one weekly day with no study keep you steady, and steadier candidates make fewer slips.

Source: Union Public Service Commission, Examination Notice No. 05/2026-CSE dated 4 February 2026 for the Civil Services Examination 2026, and the UPSC programme of examinations for 2027 (as on 20 May 2026), from upsc.gov.in, read on 14 November 2026. The notice for 2027 was not out on that day, so figures for 2027 are 2026 figures unless the page says otherwise. Rules can change: check upsc.gov.in and the Rules published by the Government.
