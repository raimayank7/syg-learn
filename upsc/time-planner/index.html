UPSC Prelims time planner

Set the number of questions and the minutes for a final check. The tool shows the seconds a question gets in the two-hour paper.

The numbers behind it

Each Preliminary paper lasts two hours. The planner takes your final-check minutes off 120 and divides the rest by the number of questions you type.

How the minutes divide

Each Preliminary paper lasts two hours (120 minutes). The notice does not give the number of questions, so the table shows two common mock sizes.

With nothing kept back, a 100-question paper gives 72 seconds a question and an 80-question paper gives 90. Reading passages (the comprehension questions) take longer than a short numeracy question, so you will spend less than the average on some and more on others.

Three sample plans

  • Two passes: answer every question you can do at once in the first 80 minutes, then spend 30 minutes on the marked ones, and 10 minutes on a final check of marking.
  • Sections first: do the questions you are best at first, to bank marks early.
  • Passages first: read the comprehension passages first while your mind is fresh.

Whichever plan you pick, check the clock every 20 minutes. If you are behind, skip the next hard question instead of reading faster.

Why seconds matter

In a two-hour paper a slip of ten seconds a question adds up to many minutes over a whole paper. Practise with a clock, and notice which kinds of question you answer fastest and slowest. Then plan to spend your extra seconds on the slow kind and to bank marks quickly on the fast kind.

Questions people ask

How long is each paper?

Two hours each, per the notice. Candidates with certain benchmark disabilities may get extra time as the Commission decides.

How long should I keep for a final check?

Find out by practice. Try a few values in timed mocks.
