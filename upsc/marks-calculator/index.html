UPSC Prelims marks calculator

Enter the number of questions your mock has and your right and wrong counts. Each wrong answer takes off one-third of a question's marks.

How it works

The notice sets each paper at 200 marks and takes one-third of the marks of a question off for a wrong answer (read on 14 November 2026). The tool divides 200 by the number of questions in your mock to get the marks of one question, then gives it for each right answer and takes one-third of it for each wrong one.

Using it well

  • Use it on mock papers, not to predict your result.
  • Paper II is qualifying at 33% (66 of 200). Paper I marks decide the cut-off, which the Commission fixes each year.
  • Look at how many marks you lose to wrong answers, and whether skipping them would have been better.

An example

PaperQuestionsRightWrongMarks
Paper I mock1006030100.00
Paper I mock100500100.00
Paper II mock80404066.67

In the first row 60 right and 30 wrong gives 2 × (60 − 10) = 100 marks for 2 marks a question. In the second, 50 right and none wrong also gives 100 marks, so attempting 30 more questions and getting 30 wrong gained 10 more right answers but lost the same amount to the penalty. The third paper has 80 questions, each worth 2.5 marks, and 40 wrong answers wipe out a third of the 40 right ones.

Questions people ask

How many questions does the real paper have?

The notice gives marks (200 for each paper) and time (two hours), not the number of questions. That is why you type the number your mock has.

What is the 66 for?

The notice says Paper II is qualifying with minimum marks of 33%, and 33% of 200 is 66.

Does Paper I have a cut-off?

Yes, the Commission decides a cut-off for Paper I each year. This tool cannot know it.
