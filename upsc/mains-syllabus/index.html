UPSC Mains syllabus: Essay, GS I to IV and optional

The Main Examination has seven papers that count for rank and two qualifying language papers. The 2026 notice sets out the syllabus of every paper. This page summarises the headings it gives and points you to the notice for the full text.

The notice says the Main Examination is meant to assess overall intellectual traits and depth of understanding, rather than the range of information and memory. It also says the General Studies papers are such that a well-educated person can answer without specialised study, and that answers should be relevant, meaningful and succinct.

Qualifying papers A and B

Indian language and English, of matriculation or equivalent standard: comprehension of passages, precis writing, usage and vocabulary, and short essays; the Indian language paper also has translation between English and the language.

Paper I: Essay

Candidates may be asked to write essays on multiple topics. They are expected to keep to the subject, arrange their ideas in order and write concisely, and credit goes to effective and exact expression.

Paper II: General Studies I

Indian heritage and culture, history and geography of the world and society. Heads in the notice: Indian culture (art forms, literature and architecture from ancient to modern times); modern Indian history from about the middle of the eighteenth century; the freedom struggle; post-independence consolidation and reorganisation; world history from the eighteenth century; salient features of Indian society and diversity; women, population, poverty and urbanisation; globalisation; social empowerment, communalism, regionalism and secularism; world physical geography; distribution of key natural resources; geophysical phenomena and changes in geographical features.

Paper III: General Studies II

Governance, Constitution, polity, social justice and international relations. Heads include the Constitution (underpinnings, evolution, features, amendments, basic structure); Union and State functions and federal issues; separation of powers and dispute redressal; comparison with other countries; Parliament and State legislatures; the Executive and the Judiciary; the Representation of People's Act; constitutional, statutory and regulatory bodies; government policies and welfare schemes; social sector issues in health, education and human resources; poverty and hunger; governance, transparency and e-governance; the role of civil services; India and its neighbourhood; international groupings and institutions.

Paper IV: General Studies III

Technology, economic development, bio-diversity, environment, security and disaster management. Heads include the Indian economy, planning, resources, growth, employment and inclusive growth; government budgeting; agriculture (crops, irrigation, subsidies, food processing, land reforms); effects of liberalisation; infrastructure; investment models; science and technology and its applications; awareness in IT, space, computers, robotics, nano-technology and bio-technology and intellectual property; conservation, pollution and environmental impact assessment; disaster management; internal security, including cyber security and money laundering, and border management.

Paper V: General Studies IV (ethics, integrity and aptitude)

Questions test attitude and approach to integrity and probity, and may use case studies. Areas: ethics and human interface; attitude; aptitude and foundational values for civil service; emotional intelligence; contributions of moral thinkers; public and civil service values and ethics in administration; probity in governance; and case studies.

Papers VI and VII: the optional subject

You choose one subject from the list of optional subjects. The notice says the level is broadly honours degree level, a level above a bachelor's and below a master's; for Engineering, Medical Science and Law the level matches a bachelor's degree. The notice prints the full syllabus of each subject.

Using this page
