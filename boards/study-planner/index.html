CBSE boards study-time planner

Choose a subject and your hours. The tool shares them across the units in proportion to marks.

What it uses

The marks of each unit come from the course structure in the CBSE curriculum 2026-27 (read on 14 November 2026).

Subjects included

  • Class X: Mathematics, Science, Social Science, English.
  • Class XII: Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, Biology (units grouped as in the curriculum where the curriculum groups them).

A table to see the idea

For 60 hours, the planner gives each unit of Mathematics a share in proportion to its marks. Here are the shares for Class X and Class XII Mathematics.

In Class X Mathematics, Algebra (20 marks) gets 15 hours of 60 and Number Systems (6 marks) gets 4.5. In Class XII Calculus (35 of 80) gets 26.3 hours.

How to use it

Treat the split as a starting point. If a unit is one you find hard, move a few hours from a unit you are strong in. Do not drop any unit: every unit has marks in the paper.

A worked example

Suppose you have 37 hours for Class X Science. Unit I (25 of 80 marks) gets about 11.6 hours, Unit II (25) the same, Unit III (12) about 5.6, Unit IV (13) about 6.1 and Unit V (5) about 2.3. The tool shows these figures for you, rounded to one decimal place.

When the plan needs changing

If your tests show one unit is much weaker than the rest, move some hours there. If a unit is described in the curriculum as assessed only formatively, as some Class X Science topics are, still learn it, but give it less of your revision time than a unit that carries board marks.

Questions people ask

Why split by marks?

The curriculum gives each unit marks, so time in proportion to marks is a fair first plan.

Does it include practicals?

No. It splits the theory paper only. Practicals and internal assessment need their own time.
