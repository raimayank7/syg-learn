CBSE Class 10 marks calculator: the 33% rule

Enter your board paper marks out of 80 and your internal assessment marks out of 20. The tool adds them and shows the 33% line for each part.

How it works

The curriculum (read on 14 November 2026) combines an 80-mark board paper with 20 marks of internal assessment and asks for at least 33% separately in the external and internal parts. The tool shows whether each part reaches 33% and gives the total of 100.

Using it well

  • Use it to see where you stand, not to predict a result.
  • If one part is below its line, work on that part first.

An example

A student scores 56 in the board paper and 17 in internal assessment: 73 of 100. Both parts are above the line (27 of 80 and 7 of 20). A student with 25 of 80 in the paper and 20 of 20 internal has 45 of 100, but the board paper is below 27, so under the curriculum wording that subject needs attention even though the combined total looks comfortable.

Where the 27 and the 7 come from

33% of 80 is 26.4 and marks are whole numbers, so 27 is the first whole mark at or above that. In the same way 33% of 20 is 6.6, so 7. The calculator uses these figures. They are our arithmetic from the 33% rule in the curriculum.

Questions people ask

Is this the official result rule?

It is a reading of the curriculum wording. CBSE and your school apply the rule to your result, so follow the result CBSE declares.

Does it work for Class XII?

No. We did not find the Class XII pass rule in the curriculum text we read, so the tool is for the Class X scheme only.

What about subjects of 100 internal marks or other splits?

Some subjects use different marks; the syllabus of the subject says so. The tool handles the common 80 + 20 split only.
