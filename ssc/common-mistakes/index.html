Common SSC exam mistakes and how to avoid them

Before the exam

  • Not reading the notice. The age, qualification and fee are in it, not in the scheme PDF.
  • Preparing only one part. All four parts count equally in Tier-I (50 marks each).
  • Leaving Tier-II to the end. It has different sections and 3-mark questions.

In the exam hall

  • Blind guessing. It gains almost nothing and may cost marks.
  • Spending too long on one question. The whole of Tier-I is 60 minutes for 100 questions.
  • Not marking an answer you can justify. A reasoned answer is worth more than a blank.

For skill tests

  • Starting dictation practice late. It is a daily skill.
  • Practising at a lower speed. Use the speed the post asks for.

How to keep a mistake from coming back

Write each mistake with its reason in a notebook and review it every week. A mistake you name is easier to stop.

Source: Staff Selection Commission, the scheme-of-examination PDF for this examination from ssc.gov.in/for-candidates/scheme-of-examination, read on 14 November 2026. Those PDFs give the scheme only. They do not give age limits, fees, vacancies or the syllabus topics, so this site does not state them: read the notice of each examination on ssc.gov.in. Rules can change by notice. A word on comparing yourself with others

Candidates take different shifts and different posts, so a friend mock score tells you little about yours. Compare your own mocks over time and look at where each mark was won or lost.

Keep a weekly review

Once a week, sit with your mistake notebook and ask three questions: which part is weakest, which kind of slip repeats, and what will I do differently next week. Write each answer in one line.
