SSC negative marking: 0.50 off for a wrong answer

The scheme PDFs for CGL Tier-I and CHSL Tier-I both say negative marking of 0.50 for each wrong answer. In both, a question carries 2 marks (50 marks for 25 questions), so a wrong answer costs one quarter of the marks of a question. A blank costs nothing. The MTS and Steno PDFs we read do not state a penalty, and the CGL Tier-II page does not either, so check the notice for those.

The arithmetic of a guess

What you can rule outChance of a right answerAverage result of a guess (2-mark question)
Nothing (blind guess among 4)1 in 40.25 × 2 − 0.75 × 0.5 = +0.125
One option1 in 30.333 × 2 − 0.667 × 0.5 = +0.33
Two options1 in 20.5 × 2 − 0.5 × 0.5 = +0.75

A blind guess is worth +0.125 on average, which is almost nothing, and it may cost marks in any one test. Ruling out an option raises it. These are averages: a few guesses can go the wrong way.

A worked example

CandidateAttemptedRightWrongMarks (2 each, 0.5 off)
A80701070 × 2 − 10 × 0.5 = 135.0
B90751575 × 2 − 15 × 0.5 = 142.5
C100762476 × 2 − 24 × 0.5 = 140.0

C attempted everything and scored less than B, because the 24 wrong answers cost 12 marks. B attempted fewer questions and did best. The number to attempt is the one your accuracy supports.

A rule of thumb (our own advice)

  • Answer everything you can justify.
  • Guess only when you can rule out at least one option.
  • In a 60-minute paper of 100 questions, do not let one hard question eat the time of three easy ones.

See the score planner for how many questions you need to attempt at your accuracy.
