SSC score planner

Choose what to plan, a target and your accuracy. The tool works out how many questions you would need to attempt.

What it assumes

It assumes 2 marks a question, 0.50 off for a wrong answer, and the same accuracy throughout.

Reading the result

  • At 100% accuracy the number to attempt is the least that reaches the target.
  • Each point of accuracy you lose forces you to attempt more questions for the same score.

A table to compare

This table uses the same rule as the tool for a whole Tier-I of 100 questions and 200 marks: the number of questions you must attempt, out of 100, to reach each target at each accuracy, with 0.50 off for a wrong answer.

Look along a row: higher accuracy cuts the questions you need to attempt. Look down a column: each rise in target needs more right answers. "Not reachable" means even attempting all 100 at that accuracy falls short.

How to use the planner in practice

After a timed mock, note how many questions you attempted and how many were right. Divide right by attempted for your accuracy, put it in the tool with your target, and compare with what you attempted. If you attempted far more than the tool says, you may be losing marks to wrong answers.

Questions people ask

Why does accuracy matter so much?

A wrong answer costs a quarter of the marks you could have earned, on top of losing the chance of a right one.

Does it predict the cut-off?

No. SSC decides cut-offs.
