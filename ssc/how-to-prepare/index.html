How to prepare for SSC exams

This is our own advice, built on the structure the scheme PDFs give: first-stage tests of four parts in one timed block, a penalty of a quarter of a question's marks, and a second stage with its own modules. We cannot promise results.

1. Read the scheme and the notice

The scheme PDFs are two pages each. Read the one for your examination, then the latest notice for age, qualification and fee.

2. Build the four-part base

Reasoning, quantitative or numerical ability, English and general awareness appear in every examination, so the same base serves CGL, CHSL, MTS and the Steno examination.

3. Make arithmetic fast

With 36 seconds a question you need tables, squares, fractions and percentages at your fingertips. Do a short drill every day.

4. Read one newspaper section a day for awareness

General Awareness has 25 questions in Tier-I. Keep a notebook of facts with dates.

5. Practise the penalty

Practise with the penalty so that your habits of skipping and guessing form before the exam. Our practice sets do.

6. Plan for Tier-II early

CGL and CHSL Tier-II have 3-mark questions, separate sections and computer or typing tests, so start some of that practice before Tier-I results.

7. If you are going for Steno

Dictation and transcription need regular daily practice. Start early and use real speeds: 80 words per minute for Grade D and 100 for Grade C.

8. Keep a mistake notebook

After each mock, write the question type, the reason for the error and the fix. Review it every week.

Source: Staff Selection Commission, the scheme-of-examination PDF for this examination from ssc.gov.in/for-candidates/scheme-of-examination, read on 14 November 2026. Those PDFs give the scheme only. They do not give age limits, fees, vacancies or the syllabus topics, so this site does not state them: read the notice of each examination on ssc.gov.in. Rules can change by notice.
