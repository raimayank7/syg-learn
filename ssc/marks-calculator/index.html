SSC Tier-I marks calculator

Choose the examination and enter your right and wrong counts for each part. Each right answer is 2 marks and each wrong one loses 0.50.

How it works

The scheme PDFs (read on 14 November 2026) give each part 25 questions for 50 marks, so a question is 2 marks, and say 0.50 is deducted for each wrong answer. The tool adds 2 for each right answer and takes 0.50 off for each wrong one.

Using it well

  • Use it on mock papers, not to predict your result.
  • Look at each part, not only the total.
  • Check how many marks you lose to wrong answers, and whether skipping them would have been better.

An example

PartQuestionsRightWrongMarks
Quantitative Aptitude2520438.00 of 50
General Intelligence and Reasoning2525050.00 of 50
General Awareness25151025.00 of 50

In Quantitative Aptitude, 20 right and 4 wrong give 20 × 2 − 4 × 0.50 = 38 marks. In General Awareness, 15 right and 10 wrong give 30 − 5 = 25: the ten wrong answers cost 5 marks, the value of two and a half right answers.

A way to use the calculator

After a mock, enter your right answers part by part. Look first at the total, then at the part where you lost most. If one part is far behind the others, give it the next week. If all parts are close, work on speed with more timed mocks.

What the result means

The tool cannot tell you your real score because mocks differ from the real test, and SSC fixes its own cut-offs. Use it to compare yourself with yourself over several mocks.

Questions people ask

Is this the final score?

No. SSC decides cut-offs and may normalise; the tool only adds your counts under the Tier-I rule.

Does it cover Tier-II?

No. Our scheme pages show Tier-II sections and 3-mark questions, but the scheme PDF does not state a penalty, so we do not calculate it.
