What is on this site

The Staff Selection Commission holds computer based examinations for posts in central government offices. This site gathers the scheme of four of them (CGL, CHSL, MTS and Stenographer), the tentative calendar, and plain guides to negative marking, the Tier-I test and how to choose an exam.

Alongside the facts are practice sets for the reasoning and arithmetic parts, and tools that turn your counts into marks, a target into attempts, and 60 minutes into seconds per question.

Where the facts come from

Every figure about an exam comes from the scheme-of-examination PDFs on ssc.gov.in and the 2026-27 tentative calendar, read on 14 November 2026. The scheme PDFs do not give ages, fees, vacancies or syllabus topics, so we state none of them. Schemes and dates change by notice, so check the official site before you act. Our study advice is our own and makes no promise about results.

Common questions

What is the SSC CGL Tier-I pattern?

Four parts of 25 questions and 50 marks each (General Intelligence and Reasoning, General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude, English Comprehension), 100 questions and 200 marks in 60 minutes, with 0.50 off for each wrong answer. Source: the CGL scheme PDF, read on 14 November 2026.

Is there negative marking in SSC exams?

CGL and CHSL Tier-I take 0.50 off for each wrong answer on a 2-mark question. The MTS and Stenographer scheme PDFs do not state a penalty; check the notice.

When is the next SSC exam?

SSC's tentative calendar 2026-27, updated 13 November 2026, lists the Constable (GD) exam for January to March 2027, advertised in September 2026 with a closing date in October 2026. The dates in the notice rule.

What are the age limit and fee?

They are not in the scheme PDFs, so this site does not state them. Read the notice of the examination on ssc.gov.in.

What is the MTS pattern?

A computer based exam in two 45-minute sessions: numerical and mathematical ability (20 questions, 60 marks), reasoning (20, 60), general awareness (25, 75) and English (25, 75). Havaldar has a walking or height test too.

Are you connected with SSC?

No. This is an independent site, not connected with the Staff Selection Commission.
