Revision method: planning the last weeks before an exam

Revision is the time you use to make what you learnt stick. This is our own advice; people differ and no number here is a rule.

How much time to keep

The planner lets you keep a share of your study days for revision. A common starting point is a fifth to a third of the days. If you are learning a lot of new material, keep a larger share; if you already know most of it, a smaller one. Keep it as a block at the end so that nothing new arrives in the last days.

Revise by testing, not only reading

  • Close the book and write what you remember about a chapter. Then open it and fix the gaps.
  • Solve past questions of the kind your exam asks, in the time it gives.
  • Explain a topic aloud, as if to a friend.

Revise in rounds

Go through all subjects once quickly, then a second time on the topics you marked as weak. A second round on weak topics gives you more than a third round on strong ones.

The last two days

Do not start new topics. Read your own short notes, check your admit card, pens and route, and sleep well.

What to drop when time is short

Drop the topics that carry the fewest marks and that you find hardest, not the ones you are close to mastering. Say this plainly to yourself and make the choice early.

All advice on this page is our own. It is not research and not from any exam body. Adjust it to your own life. This site is independent and is not connected with any examination board.
