How many hours should you study each day? A practical guide

There is no right number of hours for everyone. This page is our own advice for finding a number you can keep up.

Start from what is possible

List the hours of your day that are really free: not school, travel, meals, chores or sleep. Study hours must come from that list. A plan that needs more hours than exist will fail in the first week.

Think in blocks

Break your hours into blocks of 45 to 90 minutes with a break of 5 to 15 minutes between. Put the hardest subject in your best block. Phones go out of reach during a block.

Protect sleep and a rest day

Sleep is not time lost: studying tired gives little. Keep a regular bedtime. Keep one weekday free; the planner has a rest-day setting for this.

Try, then adjust

Use the number for a week. If you could not keep it, lower it and extend your start date instead of skipping days. If you kept it easily, raise it a little. A number you keep is worth more than a bigger one you drop.

Hours of what kind

One hour of solving questions and checking mistakes teaches more than an hour of rereading. Plan the hours as kinds of work: learning, practice and revision, and let the planner split them by days.

All advice on this page is our own. It is not research and not from any exam body. Adjust it to your own life. This site is independent and is not connected with any examination board.
