How the study planner works: the arithmetic explained

The planner is simple arithmetic. Knowing the sums lets you trust it, check it and change the inputs sensibly.

Step 1: count the study days

The days from the start date up to the day before the exam, leaving out your rest weekday. From 14 November to 13 January there are 60 days; if you rest every Sunday, about 8 or 9 of them go, which leaves about 51 or 52 study days.

Step 2: split learning and revision

The revision share (say 25 per cent) of the study days, rounded up, becomes the revision days at the end. With 52 study days, 25 per cent is 13 days, so 39 days are for learning.

Step 3: share the hours by weight

If you study 4 hours a day and your weights are 3, 3 and 2, the subjects get 4 × 3/8 = 1.5, 1.5 and 4 × 2/8 = 1 hour a day. Over 52 days that is 78, 78 and 52 hours.

Step 4: spread the chapters

A subject with 14 chapters over 39 learning days covers 14/39 chapters a day, about 0.36, which is a chapter in about three days. The table shows the chapter range for each day.

A check you can do by hand

Add up the hours of one subject over all the days: it should equal the days times that subject's hours a day. Our build runs this check on test cases every time the site is built.

Changing the plan

If you fall behind, set the start date to today and the plan is recalculated for the days that remain. If a subject worries you, raise its weight by one or two points.
