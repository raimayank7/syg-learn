How a chart of accounts feeds your statements

The chart of accounts is the menu from which the final statements are built. After the books are written up, each account's closing balance is carried to one of two statements, and the class of the account decides which.

The rule

AssetPosition statement13

EquityPosition statement3
IncomeProfit statement5
ExpenseProfit statement19

The profit statement collects income and expense accounts for a period and ends with the profit or loss. The position statement lists assets, liabilities and equity at a date. Profit is added to the owner's equity, which is why the two statements agree.

Why the chart matters for the statements

  • If an account is put in the wrong class, the profit and the position are both wrong. A deposit you hold, entered as income, makes the profit too high.
  • Accounts in one class that belong together should have neighbouring codes: then each group is easy to find and add up.
  • The starter chart has 5 income accounts and 19 expense accounts. Each would appear as its own line in the profit statement unless you group several lines together.

For exams and filings

Your board or authority will set the layout and headings for the statements you must present. The chart tells you which balances to use; the required layout tells you how to arrange them.

These lists and the numbering are our own examples. They are not an official, statutory or board-approved chart, and they claim to follow no accounting standard or tax rule. Rules differ between places and organisations, so check what your own accountant, course, lender or governing body requires.
