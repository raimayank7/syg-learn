Chart of accounts for a service business

This chart of accounts suits a consultant, freelancer, agency or any business that sells time and skill. With no stock, the main questions are who owes you, what work is not yet billed, and what each client project costs you. The chart has fee income by kind, subcontractor costs and a work-in-progress account. If you are a sole freelancer, you may drop the subcontractor and project accounts.

The list has 55 accounts: 16 asset, 8 liability, 3 equity, 7 income, 21 expense. 5 of them are contra accounts.

Every account in the list

The codes follow the pattern in how to number accounts: 1000s assets, 2000s liabilities, 3000s equity, 4000s income, 5000s and 6000s expenses. You can change any code. A contra account (marked) has its balance on the opposite side from the others of its class; see contra accounts.

Making it yours

  • Delete accounts you will never use, and add accounts only when you need to see a total on its own.
  • Keep the miscellaneous account small. If it grows, give the big items their own account.
  • Ask your accountant or course which names and groupings you must use before you start recording.

These lists and the numbering are our own examples. They are not an official, statutory or board-approved chart, and they claim to follow no accounting standard or tax rule. Rules differ between places and organisations, so check what your own accountant, course, lender or governing body requires.
