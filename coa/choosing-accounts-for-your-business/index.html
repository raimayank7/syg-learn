How to choose accounts for your own business

The right number of accounts is the number that lets you answer your real questions: how much did we sell, what did it cost, who owes us, whom do we owe, and what is left. More accounts give more detail but also more work and more chances of posting to the wrong one.

A checklist

  1. Start from the nearest chart and delete what you will never use.
  2. Give an account of its own to anything you want a total for: each kind of income, and each cost you want to watch or control.
  3. Group the rest. Several small costs can share one account until one of them grows.
  4. Keep customers and suppliers in a separate list (a subsidiary ledger) if you have many. The chart then needs only one receivables account and one payables account.
  5. Ask your accountant or course which accounts you must have, and add them.

Signs your chart needs changing

  • The miscellaneous account is one of your biggest expenses.
  • You often hesitate over which of two accounts to use. Merge them, or write a short rule for each.
  • You cannot answer a question your lender or owner asks without going back through the receipts.

These lists and the numbering are our own examples. They are not an official, statutory or board-approved chart, and they claim to follow no accounting standard or tax rule. Rules differ between places and organisations, so check what your own accountant, course, lender or governing body requires.
