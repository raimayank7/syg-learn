JEE Main Mathematics syllabus

The NTA syllabus document for JEE (Main) 2027 lists 14 units for mathematics in Paper 1. The unit titles below follow that document, and the summaries and tips are in our own words. Where a unit has long lists of topics, the summary is shorter; for the exact wording, always read the NTA syllabus PDF.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin, JEE (Main) 2027, read on 13 November 2026. Rules and dates can change, so check jeemain.nta.nic.in before you act. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any coaching institute.

  1. Unit 1: Sets, relations and functions

    The syllabus lists: Sets and their operations, power set, types of relations, equivalence relations, one-one, onto and into functions, and composition of functions.

    Study tip: Draw Venn diagrams for every set identity once. For functions, test one-one and onto with a small table of values before trying algebra.

  2. Unit 2: Complex numbers and quadratic equations

    The syllabus lists: The form a + ib, the Argand plane, modulus and argument, algebra of complex numbers, and quadratic equations with the relations between roots and coefficients.

    Study tip: Learn sum and product of roots until they are automatic. Many questions ask for a symmetric expression of the roots, and these never need the roots themselves.

  3. Unit 3: Matrices and determinants

    The syllabus lists: Types of matrices, algebra of matrices, determinants of order two and three, area of a triangle, adjoint and inverse, and solving simultaneous linear equations by matrices.

    Study tip: Practise evaluating three by three determinants by row expansion with signs written out. Know when a system has no, one or many solutions.

  4. Unit 4: Permutations and combinations

    The syllabus lists: The fundamental principle of counting, permutations and combinations, the meaning of P(n, r) and C(n, r), and simple applications.

    Study tip: Before using a formula ask: does order matter? Most errors come from that one question.

  5. Unit 5: Binomial theorem and its simple applications

    The syllabus lists: The binomial theorem for a positive integral index, general term, middle term and simple applications.

    Study tip: Write the general term T(r+1) every time and set the power you want. It is slower than guessing and nearly always right.

  6. Unit 6: Sequence and series

    The syllabus lists: Arithmetic and geometric progressions, inserting arithmetic and geometric means, and the relation between A.M. and G.M.

    Study tip: Keep the sum formulas and the nth-term formulas on one card. Many problems reduce to forming two equations in a and d (or a and r).

  7. Unit 7: Limit, continuity and differentiability

    The syllabus lists: Real-valued functions, limits, continuity, differentiability, derivatives of standard functions, derivatives up to order two, rate of change, monotonic functions, and maxima and minima of functions of one variable.

    Study tip: Master the standard limits and the chain rule first. For maxima and minima, always check the sign of the second derivative or the sign change of the first.

  8. Unit 8: Integral calculus

    The syllabus lists: Integration as the reverse of differentiation, standard integrals, substitution, parts, partial fractions, definite integrals and their properties, and area bounded by curves.

    Study tip: Do definite integrals with properties (symmetry, a + b − x) separately from technique drills; they save the most time in the paper.

  9. Unit 9: Differential equations

    The syllabus lists: Order and degree, solving by separation of variables, and homogeneous and linear first-order equations.

    Study tip: Learn to recognise the type before solving: separable, homogeneous or linear. Check by differentiating your answer.

  10. Unit 10: Co-ordinate geometry

    The syllabus lists: Distance and section formulae, locus, slope, forms of a straight line, angles between lines, circles, and the conic sections: parabola, ellipse and hyperbola with their standard equations.

    Study tip: Draw a quick sketch every time. For conics, memorise the standard forms and the meaning of each constant (a, b, c, e).

  11. Unit 11: Three dimensional geometry

    The syllabus lists: Co-ordinates in space, distance and section formulae, direction ratios and cosines, the angle between lines, equations of lines and skew lines with the shortest distance between them.

    Study tip: Link it to vectors: a line is a point plus a direction, and most formulas follow from that.

  12. Unit 12: Vector algebra

    The syllabus lists: Vectors and scalars, addition, components in two and three dimensions, and scalar and vector products.

    Study tip: Know the geometric meaning of dot and cross products (projection and area). It makes many questions one-liners.

  13. Unit 13: Statistics and probability

    The syllabus lists: Measures of dispersion, mean, median, mode, standard deviation, variance and mean deviation, probability of events, the addition and multiplication theorems, Bayes' theorem and probability distributions of a random variable.

    Study tip: Draw a tree diagram for conditional probability. For statistics, practise a short table calculation until it is quick and free of slips.

  14. Unit 14: Trigonometry

    The syllabus lists: Trigonometric identities and functions, and inverse trigonometric functions with their properties.

    Study tip: Keep the identity list short and learn it by use. Know the principal value ranges of the inverse functions; examiners do test them.

How to use this list

Rate each unit as strong, fair or weak, and revisit the rating every two weeks. Practise with our mathematics practice set, and read the preparation guide.
