JEE Main 2027 syllabus

The syllabus for Paper 1 (B.E./B.Tech.) has three subjects, with 14 units of mathematics, 20 of physics and 20 of chemistry. Choose a subject for the unit-by-unit guide.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin, JEE (Main) 2027, read on 13 November 2026. Rules and dates can change, so check jeemain.nta.nic.in before you act. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any coaching institute.

  • Mathematics14 units
  • Physics20 units
  • Chemistry20 units

What the syllabus covers

The syllabus draws on the class XI and class XII topics of the three subjects. Mathematics ranges from sets and algebra to calculus, coordinate geometry, vectors and probability. Physics runs from units and mechanics through heat, waves, electricity and magnetism to optics, modern physics, electronic devices and experimental skills. Chemistry covers physical chemistry (mole concept, atomic structure, thermodynamics, solutions, equilibrium, kinetics), inorganic chemistry (periodic trends, blocks, coordination compounds) and organic chemistry (principles, hydrocarbons, functional groups, biomolecules, practical chemistry).

The syllabus for the architecture and planning papers (2A and 2B) includes mathematics (the same units as above), an aptitude test and either a drawing test or planning-based questions. See Paper 2A and 2B.

A suggested order

StageMathematicsPhysicsChemistry
Start withSets, quadratic equations, sequences, trigonometryUnits, kinematics, laws of motion, work and energyBasic concepts, atomic structure, periodic trends
ThenMatrices, permutations, binomial theorem, coordinate geometryRotation, gravitation, solids and liquids, thermodynamicsBonding, thermodynamics, solutions, equilibrium
LaterCalculus, differential equations, vectors, 3D geometry, probabilityElectrostatics, circuits, magnetism, induction, optics, modern physicsRedox, kinetics, p-, d- and f-block, organic chemistry

This order is only a suggestion: it follows how later ideas build on earlier ones. If the order your school uses is different, follow that one and fill gaps as you find them.

Using the syllabus well

  • Study only what is listed. Textbooks contain more than the syllabus asks.
  • Work through the units in an order that builds: algebra before calculus, mechanics before electromagnetism, physical chemistry before organic reactions.
  • Tick a unit only after solving mixed problems from it.
