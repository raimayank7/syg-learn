JEE Main twelve-week study plan

This is a twelve-week plan that you can adjust. It assumes that you have already read each syllabus unit once and that you can give about three to five hours on a school day and more on a holiday. It does not promise a score. Change it to fit your schedule, your board examinations and your own strengths.

Weeks 1 to 4: close the gaps

  • Use the syllabus pages to rate each unit: strong, fair or weak.
  • Spend about two-thirds of your time on weak units and one-third on fair ones.
  • After each unit, do about twenty mixed questions and write wrong answers in your mistake notebook.
  • Once a week, take a half-length timed set (about 35 questions, 90 minutes).

Weeks 5 to 8: build speed and accuracy

  • Take a full three-hour paper every week, in the same shift hours as the real exam.
  • Spend a day on analysis: which questions took too long? which mistakes repeated?
  • Do daily 30-minute drills in your two weakest units.
  • Use the time planner to set per-subject minutes and keep to it.

Weeks 9 to 11: sharpen

  • Two full papers a week, with a full review the next day.
  • Revise formulas and reactions in short daily sessions from your own notebook.
  • Practise the numerical section in particular (see this guide).
  • Fix your rule for guessing (see negative marking).

Week 12: calm and ready

  • One light paper in the first half of the week; no new topics in the last three days.
  • Check the admit card, ID, travel and the exam-centre route.
  • Sleep on schedule. Eat normally. Keep the last evening free.

How to use the plan

Counting backwards from an exam day is easy: use the dates to place week 12 just before the paper you are aiming for. If you have fewer than twelve weeks, shorten the first block, not the last. If your board examinations are in the middle, pause the plan for them and restart; never drop board preparation for a mock test.

Tracking

Keep a simple table of week, paper score, accuracy, and the two most common mistakes. After four weeks you will see a trend. If it is flat, change one thing (the order of the subjects, the speed of the first hour, the use of the mistake notebook) and see what happens.
