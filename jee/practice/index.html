JEE Main practice questions

Original questions with +4 and −1 marking. Four sets: mathematics, physics, chemistry and mixed.

  • MathematicsTen questions, +4 and −1 marking
  • PhysicsTen questions, +4 and −1 marking
  • ChemistryTen questions, +4 and −1 marking
  • Mixed: all three subjectsTen questions, +4 and −1 marking

What the sets are for

Practice builds three habits that the JEE Main scheme rewards: working carefully, writing units and checking the sign, and deciding when to skip. Each round has ten questions: a mix of multiple-choice questions with four options and numerical questions where you type a number, as in the real paper.

Our questions are original and easier than the real thing. They are made by a small program, so there is no question bank to memorise, and each answer is checked by a second program when the site is built. Use them for warming up and for drilling a weak unit, and use full past papers from the NTA site for a true measure.

How to use them

  1. Pick a set. Start with the subject you like least.
  2. Do a round of ten without help. Skip when you are unsure.
  3. Read the working for every wrong answer and write the reason in a notebook.
  4. Repeat the same set the next day. Aim for fewer wrong answers, not more attempted.

What a good practice habit looks like

Ten minutes a day with a set, done honestly, is worth more than one long session a week. Keep the same time each day and the same place, and write your score and your two worst slips in a notebook. After two weeks, look back: you will see which slips repeat. Those are the ones to fix first.

Do not chase a perfect score on these sets. They are easier than the real paper by design, so a perfect score only means that the basics are in place, which is the point. The real test is how you behave when the question is harder, and for that you need full-length papers under exam conditions.

Why the marking is +4 and −1

The sets use the same marking as the paper, from the 2027 bulletin, so that skipping feels like a real choice. Try playing a round once while answering everything, and once while skipping anything you are unsure of; compare the scores. Many people are surprised by the result.
