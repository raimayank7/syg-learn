How this set works

Ten questions a round drawn from all three subjects, as in a mixed paper. Questions are made new each time. A right answer scores +4, a wrong one −1 and a skipped one 0, as in the NTA bulletin. Multiple-choice questions show four options; numerical questions need a number typed in. After each answer you see the working.

A mixed round is closer to the real paper, where subjects follow one another. Notice how switching between subjects affects your speed and care, and which subject you drift towards when you are in a hurry.

For separate sets see mathematics, physics and chemistry in the practice hub.

How to review a round

A mixed round shows where you lose marks across subjects. After the round, sort the wrong answers by subject and by kind of slip. If one subject accounts for most of the lost marks, spend the next session on it with the separate set. If the slips are of the same kind across all subjects (signs, units, reading the question), the fix is a habit, not more content: write units on every line, and underline what is asked before you start.

Repeat the mixed set after a week and compare the number of wrong answers, not the score. Fewer wrong answers is the sign of progress that matters.
