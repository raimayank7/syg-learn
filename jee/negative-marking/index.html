JEE Main negative marking: when to guess

JEE (Main) gives +4 for a correct answer and −1 for an incorrect one, in both multiple-choice and numerical questions. An unanswered question scores 0. This simple rule produces an important question: when should you guess?

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin, JEE (Main) 2027, read on 13 November 2026. Rules and dates can change, so check jeemain.nta.nic.in before you act. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any coaching institute.

The arithmetic of a guess

Suppose you attempt a multiple-choice question with four options and you are completely sure only that one is wrong, so you guess among three. The chance of being right is one in three. In the long run, your expected marks from that guess are (1/3 × 4) + (2/3 × −1) = 4/3 − 2/3 = about +0.67. A pure guess among all four options gives (1/4 × 4) + (3/4 × −1) = +0.25. These are small positives. They are averages: in a single paper the wrong guesses can cost you.

What matters more

  • Accuracy: each wrong answer costs 5 marks compared with a right one (the 4 you could have got, and the 1 you lost).
  • Elimination: the more options you can rule out for a real reason, the better the odds.
  • Time: a guess takes little time, but the time saved is only useful if you use it on a question you can solve.

Use the tools

The score planner turns a target score and your accuracy into the number of questions you need to attempt. The marks calculator totals your counts across the three subjects.

A sample rule of thumb

Many students set a rule such as: "attempt a question only if I can rule out at least two options, or I can solve it." Whatever rule you pick, practise it in mock papers so that it is automatic. Record in your mistake notebook which guesses worked and which did not. After ten papers, you will know whether your guesses are better than chance.

For numerical questions

There are no options to eliminate, so a guess is nearly always wrong. Skip unless you have a method. See the numerical section guide.
