Common JEE Main mistakes and how to avoid them

Teachers who have prepared many students for entrance tests often list the same mistakes again and again. This page collects the most common ones in our own words, with a fix for each. None of this is from NTA; it is practical advice.

Studying mistakes

  • Reading instead of solving. Fix: after each topic, close the book and solve questions.
  • Jumping between many books. Fix: pick one main source per subject and use others only for practice.
  • Skipping the syllabus. Fix: check every topic against the official syllabus and ignore what is not there.
  • Leaving revision for the end. Fix: schedule short weekly revisions from week one.
  • Ignoring the mistake notebook. Fix: reread it every week.

Exam-hall mistakes

  • Starting with the hardest subject. Fix: begin with the subject in which you are the most fluent and build confidence.
  • Spending ten minutes on one question. Fix: set a limit of about three minutes and move on.
  • Not reading the units. Fix: write them down in your working.
  • Guessing on numerical questions. Fix: skip unless you have a method (see numerical guide).
  • Marking the wrong option. Fix: re-read the option you chose before moving on.

Mindset mistakes

  • Comparing yourself to others. Fix: compare your own papers over time.
  • Giving up after a bad paper. Fix: treat it as information about what to fix next.
  • Studying until you are exhausted. Fix: plan breaks, sleep and exercise as part of the schedule.

Practical mistakes

  • Applying after the date has passed. Fix: put the dates from the dates page on your calendar.
  • Wrong photo or signature on the form. Fix: read the instructions in the bulletin carefully.
  • Forgetting the admit card or ID. Fix: pack the night before.
  • Missing the correction window. Fix: check your form as soon as it is submitted, and again before the correction dates.

If one of these sounds familiar, try the twelve-week plan or look at the preparation guide.
