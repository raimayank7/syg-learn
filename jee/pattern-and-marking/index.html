JEE Main 2027 pattern and marking scheme

JEE (Main) is the national entrance test that NTA, the National Testing Agency, conducts for admission to B.E. and B.Tech. courses and, through separate papers, to B.Arch. and B.Planning. This page sets out the pattern of the main paper, Paper 1 for B.E. and B.Tech., exactly as the 2027 information bulletin gives it.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin, JEE (Main) 2027, read on 13 November 2026. Rules and dates can change, so check jeemain.nta.nic.in before you act. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any coaching institute.

Paper 1 (B.E. / B.Tech.) at a glance

SubjectSection A (multiple choice)Section B (numerical value)Marks
Mathematics205100
Physics205100

Total6015300

That is 75 questions in all, 25 in each subject, taken on a computer (a Computer Based Test). In Section B the answer is a number that you enter with the mouse and an on-screen number pad, and it is rounded to the nearest integer.

Marking

What you doMarks
Correct answer (multiple choice or numerical)+4
Incorrect answer (multiple choice or numerical)−1
Question not answered, or marked for review without an answer0

The bulletin says there is negative marking in both Section A and Section B. The numerical section is also marked down for a wrong answer, so a blind guess there has a cost.

Time and shifts

Paper 1 lasts 3 hours. Candidates with a disability who are eligible for a scribe get 4 hours. Each day has two shifts: 9:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon and 3:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m. (Indian Standard Time).

How merit is worked out

Your raw scores in Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry, and the total, are converted into NTA scores. The overall merit list is made by merging the NTA scores of all shifts of both sessions. The conversion exists because different shifts have different question papers; the bulletin describes the method in an appendix on normalisation by percentile score.

If two candidates have the same score

The bulletin gives an order of tie-breaks for Paper 1:

  1. Higher NTA score in Mathematics.
  2. Then higher NTA score in Physics.
  3. Then higher NTA score in Chemistry.
  4. Then the lower proportion of incorrect to correct answers across all subjects.
  5. Then the same proportion for Mathematics, then Physics, then Chemistry.
  6. If a tie is still there, the candidates get the same rank.

What this means for how you practise

The pattern rewards careful accuracy more than raw speed: a wrong answer costs 5 marks compared with a right one, and unanswered questions cost nothing. Our marks calculator shows how your counts turn into a total, and our page on negative marking explains how to decide when to attempt a question.
