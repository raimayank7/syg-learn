JEE Main 2027 fees and eligibility

This page gives the fee table and the eligibility rules of the JEE (Main) 2027 bulletin. Fees are charged for each session. Processing charges and GST, where they apply, are paid by the candidate in addition to these amounts.

Source: NTA, Information Bulletin, JEE (Main) 2027, read on 13 November 2026. Rules and dates can change, so check jeemain.nta.nic.in before you act. This site is independent and is not connected with NTA, the Ministry of Education or any coaching institute.

If you take more than one paper

For candidates taking Paper 1 with Paper 2A, or Paper 1 with Paper 2B, or Papers 2A and 2B together, or all three, the bulletin lists a higher fee: General, Gen-EWS and OBC (NCL) males pay ₹2,000 and females ₹1,600 in India; SC, ST, PwD and PwBD candidates and third gender candidates pay ₹1,000. Outside India these are ₹10,000, ₹8,000 and ₹5,000.

Late fee

A candidate who applies in the late-fee window pays an extra ₹500, the same for every category. The bulletin says the late fee is uniform whatever the candidate's category fee is.

Who can appear: age and year

  • There is no age limit for appearing in JEE (Main) 2027. The institute you join later may have its own age rules.
  • Candidates who passed Class XII or an equivalent examination in 2025 or 2026, or who are appearing in it in 2027, are eligible.
  • Candidates who passed Class XII in 2024 or earlier are not eligible for JEE (Main) 2027.
  • "Year of passing" means the year your result was first declared as pass. An improvement attempt does not change it.

Qualifying examinations

The bulletin recognises, among others, the Class XII examination of a recognised Central or State board, intermediate or pre-university examinations, the National Institute of Open Schooling senior secondary with at least five subjects, the two-year course of the Joint Services Wing of the National Defence Academy, a vocational higher secondary certificate, an AICTE or State board diploma of at least three years, GCE A-levels, the Cambridge high school certificate or the International Baccalaureate diploma, and the Sikkim open schooling board. Candidates from other boards or foreign boards need a certificate of equivalence from the Association of Indian Universities. The full list is in the bulletin, and the bulletin says NTA does not take up complaints about eligibility for a particular institute.

State of eligibility

The bulletin explains that the "state of eligibility" is the state from which you passed Class XII (or equivalent), not your native place or home address. If you passed from one state and later appeared for improvement from another, the state of first passing counts. For example, a candidate who studied in Delhi but lives in Noida would choose Delhi.
