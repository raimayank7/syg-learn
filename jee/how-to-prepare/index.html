How to prepare for JEE Main

A good JEE Main preparation is a plan for three things: knowing the syllabus, practising the methods until they are quick, and practising under exam conditions. This guide is our own writing and does not promise a score. It describes habits that most teachers agree on.

1. Start with the official syllabus

Everything you study should come from the syllabus in the NTA document. Our syllabus pages list the units for each subject in plain words with a study tip for each. Tick each unit off only when you can solve mixed problems from it, not when you have merely read it.

2. Class XI and XII together

The syllabus covers both years. Many students find it helpful to treat class XI topics as the base that must be strong before XII chapters build on them: mechanics before electromagnetism, physical chemistry before organic reaction rates, algebra before calculus.

3. Build a notebook of your own mistakes

Every time you get a question wrong, write the question, the correct method, and the reason you went wrong in a sentence: "forgot sign of acceleration", "used molarity for molality". Reread this notebook weekly. It is the most personal textbook you will have, and the cheapest.

4. Practise in the style of the paper

The paper has 20 multiple-choice and 5 numerical questions in each subject, with +4 for right and −1 for wrong. Practise both kinds. On this site, our practice sets use the same marking so you get used to the cost of a wrong answer. They are original and easier than real papers, so use them for habit, not for measuring yourself.

5. Learn to read a question before you solve it

Underline what is given and what is asked. Write the units. Many errors come from solving the wrong question well.

6. Time

You have three hours for 75 questions, which is about 2.4 minutes a question on average. Practise skipping a question that is eating time and coming back to it. Use the time planner to set per-subject targets.

7. Keep revising, in small pieces

A short daily review of formulas and reactions is more effective than one big revision at the end. Use short sessions with a break. Sleep matters more than one extra hour of study at night.

8. Look after yourself

A schedule that leaves no time for food, sleep, exercise or friends will not last. If you feel overwhelmed, talk to a parent, teacher or counsellor. Entrance exams are important, but they are not the only way to a good life.
