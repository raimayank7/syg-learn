How to use NCERT textbooks for exams

NCERT textbooks are the base for many school and entrance examinations in India. This page says what the official documents we read say about that, and then gives our own advice for using the books.

What the official documents say

  • The CBSE curriculum 2026-27 lists NCERT textbooks as the prescribed books for its subjects, for example four NCERT books for Class X Social Science, and says the core competencies assessed in Board papers come from the prescribed syllabus and textbooks.
  • The CTET bulletin says the questions for Paper I and Paper II are based on the NCERT syllabus for classes I to V and VI to VIII, with difficulty up to the secondary or senior secondary stage.
  • The CUET (UG) bulletin says the syllabus of the domain-specific subjects is the NCERT syllabus.

A reading routine (our own advice)

  1. Skim the chapter: headings, boxes and summary, to see the shape.
  2. Read it fully once without stopping for every word.
  3. Read it again with a pencil: underline terms, circle formulas and dates, copy diagrams.
  4. Solve every exercise at the end of the chapter before looking at any other book.
  5. Write a summary: five lines and a list of formulas or dates.
  6. Revise from the summaries, and re-read the chapter only for the parts you cannot recall.

What a textbook cannot do

A textbook does not give you practice at the speed of an exam. After finishing a chapter, solve questions from sample papers or other practice and time yourself. Use our other sites for the exam patterns.

Using this site

The chapter tracker keeps your ticks in your own browser. The pace planner turns the number of chapters left and the days you have into chapters a day.

Common mistakes

  • Reading only the summary and skipping the body.
  • Skipping the exercises.
  • Buying or downloading a book of the wrong edition: check the class, the subject and the title on the official page.
  • Leaving the last chapters for the last week.
