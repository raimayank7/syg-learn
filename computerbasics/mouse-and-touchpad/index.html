Mouse and touchpad basics for beginners

The mouse moves a small arrow on the screen, called the pointer. You move the mouse on a flat surface and the pointer follows. A laptop has a touchpad instead: you slide one finger on it to move the pointer. The ideas are the same.

Holding the mouse

Rest your hand on the mouse without gripping it. Your first finger sits on the left button and your middle finger on the right button. Move your whole hand slowly, not just your fingers. If you run out of space, lift the mouse, put it back in the middle and carry on; the pointer stays where it is.

The five things a mouse does

ActionHowWhat it usually does
ClickPress the left button onceChooses a button, link or item
Double-clickPress the left button twice, quickly, without movingOpens a file, folder or program
Right-clickPress the right button onceShows a small menu of choices for what is under the pointer
ScrollTurn the wheel, or slide two fingers on a touchpadMoves the page up and down
DragHold the left button down, move, then let goMoves an item, or selects text

Using a touchpad

  • Tap once with one finger to click. Tap twice quickly to double-click.
  • On many laptops, tapping with two fingers is a right-click and sliding two fingers scrolls. Settings differ between laptops, so if it does not work, look in the laptop's settings for the touchpad.
  • The buttons under the touchpad also work as left and right buttons.

If the pointer is hard to control

Go slowly. If it feels too fast or too slow, the computer's settings let you change the pointer speed, and the size of the pointer can be made larger. A mouse pad or a plain table works better than a rough or shiny surface.

Practise here

The mouse practice game asks you to click circles, then smaller circles, then to double-click them. Five minutes a day for a week makes a clear difference.
