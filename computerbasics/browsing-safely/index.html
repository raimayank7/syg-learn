Browsing the internet safely: simple habits to follow

A browser is the program you use to visit websites. Most trouble on the internet can be avoided with a few habits. None of them needs technical knowledge.

Know the address bar

The long box at the top of the browser shows the address of the page you are on. Read it. A fake page often has an address that is almost right, such as an extra letter or word. For a bank or a shop you use, it is safest to save its real address as a bookmark and always use that.

The padlock sign

A small padlock beside the address means that the connection between you and the page is private on the way. It does not mean that the owner of the page is honest. Fake sites can have it too, so do not trust a page only for that.

Pop-ups that say you have a virus

A page that suddenly shows a loud warning, a siren, or a phone number to call is a trick. A website cannot really know that your computer is sick. Do not call the number and do not press its buttons. Close the browser window or the tab. If it will not close, switch the computer off and on again.

Downloads and programs

  • Get programs only from the official website of the maker or from your device's official store.
  • Be careful with "free" versions of paid programs, films and games. They are a common way to spread harmful software.
  • Do not install something because a stranger on the phone or a pop-up told you to.

Keep things up to date

Let the computer, the phone and the browser install their updates. Updates repair weak spots that people with bad intentions try to use.

On a shared computer

In a cyber café or on a family computer, log out of every account when you finish, and do not let the browser save your password. A private or incognito window forgets your history on that computer when you close it, but it does not make you invisible on the internet.
