Safe habits for UPI and phone payment apps

Phone payment apps are quick and convenient, and most people use them without trouble. These are general habits, not bank rules. Your own bank and app explain their current limits and rules on their official pages, and those are the ones to follow.

Keep your PIN and codes to yourself

Your PIN is for you alone. Do not tell it to a family member, a shopkeeper, a caller or an "official". No genuine helper needs your PIN or the code that arrives by message.

Receiving and paying are different

As a habit, if a screen asks you to enter your PIN, ask yourself: am I sending money? If someone tells you that they are paying you, but your phone is asking for your PIN, stop. Stop and check with your bank before going on. Read each screen before you press anything.

Check before you pay

  • Read the name of the person or shop shown before you confirm. If it is not who you expect, do not pay.
  • Read the amount. A wrong digit or an extra zero is easy to make.
  • Do not rush because someone behind you is waiting.

QR codes

Scanning a code is one way to pay, so be careful what you scan. Do not scan a code that a stranger sends you "to receive money". Check that a code on a shop counter has not been covered by another sticker.

Fake helpers

Be careful with a phone number from a search result for "customer care"; scammers put fake numbers there. Do not install any "support" program or share your screen at a stranger's request. Use the help section inside the app or the number printed on your bank card.

Look after the phone

  • Use a screen lock, and keep it private.
  • Install updates, and install apps only from the official store.
  • Look at the messages from your bank to see if every payment is yours.
  • If the phone is lost, use your bank's official way to block the apps and the card, and tell your phone company.

Read how to spot a scam too: most losses begin with a trick, not with a fault in the app.
