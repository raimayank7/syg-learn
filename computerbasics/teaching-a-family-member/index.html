Teaching a parent or grandparent to use a computer

If you help an older relative, the way you teach matters as much as what you teach. This is our own advice, from the habits that tend to work.

Keep sessions short

Twenty or thirty minutes is enough. Teach one thing, such as making a video call, and stop. A little each day beats one long afternoon.

Let them hold the mouse

Watching someone else do it teaches very little. Sit beside, not in front, and let your learner do each step, even if it is slower. If you take the mouse, they have learned only that you can do it.

Write the steps on paper

Write the steps in big letters, in order, and in their own words: "1. Press the round green button. 2. Touch the name." Keep the sheet next to the computer. Add a small picture if it helps.

Use the practice tools

Start with the mouse game and the typing drill. They cannot break anything and they show progress.

Teach the safe habits first

  • Never tell anyone a PIN or code.
  • A stranger who phones to help with the computer is not to be believed.
  • It is always fine to stop and ask the family.

These are in the lessons on scams and phone payments.

When they make a mistake

Say "that happens to everyone" and show how to undo it with Ctrl + Z or by closing the window. Fear of breaking something is the biggest barrier. Tell them plainly that pressing the wrong button almost never breaks a computer, and that the worst of it can usually be undone.

Celebrate small steps

The first time they send a photo or a message by themselves is a real achievement. Say so.
