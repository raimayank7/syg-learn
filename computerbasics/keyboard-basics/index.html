Keyboard basics: the keys you need and useful shortcuts

A keyboard has many keys, but you use a few of them all the time. Learn these first.

The keys that matter most

KeyWhat it does
EnterStarts a new line, or presses the highlighted button
Space barPuts a space between words
BackspaceDeletes the letter to the left of the blinking line
DeleteDeletes the letter to the right of the blinking line
ShiftHold it while pressing a letter to get a capital letter, or the upper sign on a key
Caps LockSwitches capital letters on and off. If everything comes out in capitals, press it once
TabMoves to the next box in a form
EscCloses a menu or cancels something
Arrow keysMove the blinking line or the selection

Shortcuts worth knowing

A shortcut means holding one key and pressing another. On a Windows computer the first key is Ctrl. On an Apple computer it is usually the Command key instead.

PressIt does this
Ctrl + CCopies what you have selected
Ctrl + VPastes what you copied
Ctrl + XCuts: copies it and removes it from where it was
Ctrl + ZUndoes your last action
Ctrl + ASelects everything
Ctrl + SSaves your work
Ctrl + FFinds a word on the page in most programs and browsers

Good habits

  • Sit with your wrists straight and your shoulders loose. Rest your fingers lightly on the keys.
  • Look at the screen, not the keys, as soon as you can. The small bumps on the F and J keys help your fingers find the middle.
  • Go slowly and be accurate. Speed comes by itself with practice.
  • Take a short break every half hour.

Practise

The typing drill starts with the middle row of letters and moves to words, sentences and numbers.
