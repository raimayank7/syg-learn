CTET languages: 27 languages and their codes

For CTET you pick two languages while applying. Language I is the medium of instruction you have in mind, and Language II must be a different language. The bulletin lists 27 languages, each with a code. The main question paper is bilingual (Hindi and English), and each of the two language papers tests the language you chose.

What each language paper tests

  • Language I (30 questions): two unseen passages, one prose or drama and one poem, with questions on comprehension, inference, grammar and verbal ability, plus 15 questions on the pedagogy of language development.
  • Language II (30 questions): two unseen prose passages with questions on comprehension, grammar and verbal ability, plus the same 15 questions on pedagogy.

How to choose

The bulletin says you specify your two languages on the confirmation page, so decide before you apply and check the choice on your confirmation page. Language II is meant to check comprehension and the elements of language, so many candidates choose a language they read well. Language I follows the proficiency related to the medium of instruction. Think about the schools you plan to apply to, and about which two languages you can read quickly and accurately under time pressure.

A correction window for languages is announced from time to time: for the 22nd edition it ran from 7 to 10 September 2026, and it has closed.
