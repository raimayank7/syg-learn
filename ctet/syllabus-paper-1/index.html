CTET Paper I syllabus: classes I to V

The CTET bulletin lists the content of Paper I in its Appendix I. The list below follows that appendix. For classes I to V, the bulletin points to the NCERT syllabus and textbooks for the detailed content.

I. Child Development and Pedagogy (30 questions)

Child development, primary school child (15 questions)

  • Concept of development and its relationship with learning
  • Principles of the development of children
  • Influence of heredity and environment
  • Socialization processes: the social world and children (teacher, parents, peers)
  • Piaget, Kohlberg and Vygotsky: constructs and critical perspectives
  • Concepts of child-centred and progressive education
  • Critical perspective of the construct of intelligence; multi-dimensional intelligence
  • Language and thought
  • Gender as a social construct; gender roles, gender bias and educational practice
  • Individual differences among learners, and differences based on diversity of language, caste, gender, community, religion and so on
  • Assessment for learning and assessment of learning; school-based assessment, continuous and comprehensive evaluation
  • Formulating appropriate questions to assess readiness levels, to enhance learning and critical thinking, and to assess achievement

Inclusive education and children with special needs (5 questions)

  • Learners from diverse backgrounds, including the disadvantaged and deprived
  • Children with learning difficulties and impairments
  • Talented, creative and specially abled learners

Learning and pedagogy (10 questions)

  • How children think and learn; how and why children fail to achieve success in school performance
  • Basic processes of teaching and learning; children's strategies of learning; learning as a social activity and its social context
  • The child as a problem solver and a "scientific investigator"
  • Children's errors as significant steps in the learning process
  • Cognition and emotions; motivation and learning
  • Factors contributing to learning: personal and environmental

II. Mathematics (30 questions)

Content (15 questions)

Geometry, shapes and spatial understanding, solids around us, numbers, addition and subtraction, multiplication, division, measurement, weight, time, volume, data handling, patterns and money.

Pedagogical issues (15 questions)

Nature of mathematics and logical thinking; understanding children's thinking and reasoning patterns and their strategies of making meaning; place of mathematics in the curriculum; language of mathematics; community mathematics; evaluation through formal and informal methods; problems of teaching; error analysis; diagnostic and remedial teaching.

III. Environmental Studies (30 questions)

Content (15 questions)

Family and friends (relationships, work and play, animals, plants), food, shelter, water, travel, and things we make and do.

Pedagogical issues (15 questions)

Concept and scope of EVS; significance of integrated EVS; environmental studies and environmental education; learning principles; scope and relation to science and social science; approaches to presenting concepts; activities; experimentation and practical work; discussion; continuous and comprehensive evaluation; teaching material and aids; problems.

IV. Language I (30 questions) and V. Language II (30 questions)

  • Language I, comprehension (15): two unseen passages, one prose or drama and one poem, with questions on comprehension, inference, grammar and verbal ability.
  • Language II, comprehension (15): two unseen prose passages (discursive, literary, narrative or scientific) with questions on comprehension, grammar and verbal ability.
  • Pedagogy of language development (15 in each): learning and acquisition; principles of language teaching; the role of listening and speaking; the critical role of grammar; challenges in a diverse classroom, errors and disorders; language skills; evaluating comprehension and proficiency; teaching and learning materials; remedial teaching.

How to read this list

Half of every subject is pedagogy: how children learn and how the subject is taught, and how to find and fix errors. Many candidates prepare only the content half and lose marks on the other half. Study both.
