CTET time planner

Set the minutes for a final check and for reading the passages. The tool shows the seconds a question gets in the 150-minute paper.

The numbers behind it

Each paper has 150 questions in 150 minutes. The planner takes your final-check and passage-reading minutes off 150 and divides the rest by 150.

How the minutes divide

Each CTET paper has 150 questions in 150 minutes. The table shows the time per question for different amounts kept for a final check and for reading the four language passages.

With nothing kept back you have 60 seconds a question. The language parts have unseen passages that take longer to read, so most candidates spend less than a minute on a short pedagogy question to give more to a passage.

Three sample plans

  • Section by section: 30 minutes for each 30-question section, 15 minutes at the end to check.
  • Passages first: read the four language passages at the start while your mind is fresh, then the rest.
  • Easy first: answer every question you can do at once, then return to the longer ones with the time left.

Whichever you choose, mark an answer for every question before the end because wrong answers do not lose marks.

Questions people ask

Is the paper really 150 minutes?

Yes, the bulletin gives two and a half hours (150 minutes) for each paper. Candidates with certain benchmark disabilities may get extra time as the bulletin describes.

How long should I keep for a final check?

Find out by practice. Try a few values in timed papers.
