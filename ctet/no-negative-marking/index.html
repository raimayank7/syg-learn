CTET has no negative marking: what it means

The bulletin says every question carries one mark and there is no negative marking. That shapes how you should take the paper.

The arithmetic

With four options, a pure guess is right one time in four, so on average a guess gains a quarter of a mark and loses nothing. Leaving the question blank gains nothing. Since a wrong answer is not penalised, guessing is never worse than skipping in CTET.

An example

ApproachRightWrongBlankMarks
Skip what you are unsure of100050100
Answer everything; guess the 50 rest at random (on average 12.5 right)112.537.50112.5

The table is an average illustration, not a promise: guesses vary. The point is that the second approach cannot do worse than the first on marks.

Better guesses

  • Cross out any option you know is wrong. Two left means a one in two chance.
  • Answer the questions you know first, then return and fill the rest.
  • Keep the last five minutes for blanks: mark every unanswered question.
  • In pedagogy questions, an option that is extreme or blames the child is usually weaker than one that supports learning. This is a habit of thought, not a rule: read the question.

Do not rush the ones you know

No negative marking does not mean carelessness is free. A mistake on a question you knew still costs you a mark. Read each question fully before choosing.

Three habits for the exam hall

  1. First pass: answer every question you can do at once and mark the rest.
  2. Second pass: for each marked question, cross out what is wrong and choose from what is left.
  3. Last five minutes: look for any question with no answer and fill it, even with a guess.

The bulletin says the main paper is bilingual (Hindi and English), so if a wording is unclear in one language, look at the other before you guess.
