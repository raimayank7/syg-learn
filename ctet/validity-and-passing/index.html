CTET pass mark, validity and certificate

How the pass mark works

The bulletin quotes NCTE: a person who scores 60% or more in the TET exam is considered to have passed. Out of 150 marks that is 90. School managements may give concessions to candidates of reserved categories according to their own reservation policy, and the bulletin says they should give weightage to CTET scores in recruitment, but that qualifying does not give a right to a job.

How long the certificate lasts

The bulletin says the validity of the CTET qualifying certificate for appointment is for life for all categories. This follows a CBSE public notice of 21 June 2021, which says NCTE replaced the earlier seven-year limit with "unless otherwise notified by the appropriate Government, would remain valid for life". CBSE said it would not issue revised mark sheets for earlier editions.

Attempts and improving your score

There is no restriction on the number of attempts. A person who has qualified may also appear again to improve the score.

Certificates

CBSE issues mark sheets to all candidates and eligibility certificates to those who qualify in digital form in their DigiLocker account. They are digitally signed and carry an encrypted QR code that can be scanned and checked with the DigiLocker mobile app.

Answer key and rechecking

  • The OMR answer sheet and the answer keys are displayed on the CTET site after a public notice, for two to three days.
  • A candidate who does not agree with an answer can challenge it by paying a non-refundable Rs 1,000 per question as processing charge.
  • The bulletin says there is no re-evaluation or rechecking of the result.
  • Records, including OMR sheets, are kept for only two months after the result.

Where CTET applies

The bulletin says CTET applies to schools of the Central Government (such as Kendriya Vidyalayas, Navodaya Vidyalayas and Central Tibetan Schools) and to schools under the administrative control of the Union territories it names (Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep and NCT of Delhi). Unaided private schools may choose to consider it. State government and aided schools consider the TET of their own State, but a State can also consider CTET. From 6 March 2012 teachers of classes I to VIII in CBSE-affiliated schools have to pass CTET or a State TET.

Source: CBSE, Information Bulletin CTET-September 2026 and CBSE public notices, from ctet.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. Notices after the bulletin changed some dates (see the dates page).
