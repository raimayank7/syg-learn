How to prepare for CTET

This is our own advice, not CBSE's. It is built on the structure the bulletin gives: 150 questions, 150 minutes, one mark each, no negative marking, half of every subject on pedagogy.

1. Learn the structure first

Read the syllabus pages for your paper and write down, for each part, how many questions come from content and how many from pedagogy. The bulletin splits them: for example Mathematics in Paper I has 15 content and 15 pedagogy questions. Your study time should follow that split.

2. Child Development and Pedagogy is worth a fifth of the paper

Thirty of 150 marks come from Child Development and Pedagogy, and the same ideas appear in the pedagogy half of every other subject. The bulletin names the topics: development and learning, heredity and environment, socialization, Piaget, Kohlberg and Vygotsky, intelligence, language and thought, gender, individual differences, assessment, inclusive education, motivation and learning. Understand the ideas and the names behind them, and practise telling the ideas apart in short classroom situations.

3. For content, use the NCERT textbooks

The bulletin says the content is based on the NCERT syllabus for the classes concerned. Read those textbooks the way a teacher would: ask what a child would find hard, which mistake is common, and how you would explain it.

4. Passages are practice, not luck

Each language part has unseen passages. Read a passage every day in each of your two languages, answer questions about the meaning and the grammar, and time yourself.

5. No negative marking

A wrong answer costs nothing, so there is no reason to leave a question blank. Mark an answer for every question before time is up. See our page on no negative marking.

6. Practise the clock

150 questions in 150 minutes is one minute a question, but the language passages take longer. Use the time planner, then do full timed papers.

7. Keep a mistake notebook

After every practice session write the question type, why you got it wrong and what you will do next time. Review it every week.

Source: CBSE, Information Bulletin CTET-September 2026 and CBSE public notices, from ctet.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. Notices after the bulletin changed some dates (see the dates page).
