What is on this site

The Central Teacher Eligibility Test is the national teacher eligibility test held by CBSE for people who want to teach classes I to VIII. Passing it is one of the eligibility criteria, not a job guarantee. This site gathers the pattern and marking, the syllabus for both papers, the language list, the dates, fees and pass rule, and a guide to choosing your paper.

Alongside the facts are mathematics practice sets for both papers, and tools that turn your counts into marks and 150 minutes into seconds per question.

Where the facts come from

Every figure about the exam comes from CBSE's Information Bulletin for CTET-September 2026 and CBSE's public notices on ctet.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. The exam date in the bulletin (6 September 2026) was replaced by a later notice: the 22nd edition is on 12 and 13 December 2026. Rules and dates change by public notice, so check the official site before you act. Our study advice is our own and makes no promise about results.

Common questions

What is the CTET pattern?

There are two papers. Paper I (classes I to V) and Paper II (classes VI to VIII) each have 150 multiple-choice questions of one mark in 150 minutes, with four options and no negative marking, per the CBSE bulletin read on 14 November 2026.

What is the CTET pass mark?

A score of 60% or more is a pass: 90 of 150 marks. The certificate is valid for life, and you can take CTET as many times as you want.

When is CTET 22nd edition?

CBSE's public notice of 14 September 2026 says the 22nd edition will be conducted on 12 and 13 December 2026. Your city and date appear on the CTET portal 15 days before and the admit card two days before.

What are the fees?

Rs 1,000 for one paper and Rs 1,200 for both for General and OBC (NCL); Rs 500 and Rs 600 for SC, ST and differently abled candidates. Bank GST is extra.

Which paper should I take?

Paper I if you want to teach classes I to V, Paper II for classes VI to VIII, and both if you want both levels. See the page on choosing between the papers.

Are you connected with CBSE?

No. This is an independent site, not connected with CBSE, NCTE or the Ministry of Education.
