CTET twelve-week study plan

Here is a twelve-week plan of our own. It is an example to adjust, not an official schedule. It assumes about two hours on weekdays and four on weekends. The 22nd edition is on 12 and 13 December 2026, so count back from your allotted date.

WeeksFocusWhat to do
1 to 2Read the bulletin and syllabusKnow your paper, your two languages and the part split. Collect the NCERT textbooks. Take one untimed practice paper to see where you stand.
3 to 5Child Development and PedagogyCover each syllabus bullet. Make a one-line note for each name and idea. Do short scenario questions.
6 to 8Content subjectsRead the NCERT books for your subject parts. After each chapter write three questions a teacher might ask.
9 to 10LanguagesDaily passage practice in both languages. Revise pedagogy of language development.
11Full papersTwo timed papers of 150 questions in 150 minutes. Review every mistake.
12RevisionGo through your mistake notebook. Sleep well. Check the admit card and the route to the centre.

If you have fewer weeks

Keep the order but shorten each block. Child Development and Pedagogy and the pedagogy halves of each subject are worth the most per hour, because they repeat across parts.

If you take both papers

Study the shared parts once: Child Development and Pedagogy and language pedagogy are common. Then split your time between the content of classes I to V and VI to VIII.

Each week

  • One timed section test
  • One review of the mistake notebook
  • One short passage practice a day in each language
  • One rest day

Source: CBSE, Information Bulletin CTET-September 2026 and CBSE public notices, from ctet.nic.in, read on 14 November 2026. Notices after the bulletin changed some dates (see the dates page). Rules can change by public notice: check ctet.nic.in. This site is independent and is not connected with CBSE, NCTE or the Ministry of Education.

How to spend a typical weekday

  1. Thirty minutes of passage reading in one of your languages, then five questions on it.
  2. Sixty minutes on the topic of the week from the plan above, with notes written in your own words.
  3. Thirty minutes of questions on that topic, followed by a look at every wrong answer.

What to do when you fall behind

Do not try to catch up by doubling the next day. Drop the least valuable task of the week and keep the rest. Child Development and Pedagogy and the pedagogy half of each subject repeat across parts, so protect them first. Content you have read once can be revised in the last two weeks.

The last three days

Stop learning new topics. Read your mistake notebook, one short passage a day and the syllabus list once more. Check your admit card, the centre address and the route, and pack what the admit card tells you to bring. Sleep well: a clear head helps more than one more chapter.
