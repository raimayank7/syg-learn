CTET marks calculator

Choose your paper and enter the right answers in each section. Each is worth one mark and nothing is taken off for wrong answers.

How it works

The tool adds your right answers. A paper has 150 questions, one mark each, so it is out of 150, and the bulletin (read on 14 November 2026) says 60% or more is a pass, which is 90 marks. It then tells you whether you reached 90 or how many marks you are short.

Using it well

  • Use it on mock papers, not to predict your result.
  • Look at which section is weakest and spend your next week there.
  • Remember that a recruiting body may ask for more than the pass mark, and that CTET only makes you eligible.

Every version has 150 questions, one mark each. Choose your version in the tool and enter the right answers section by section. The tool never lets a section go above its number of questions.

A way to use the calculator

After a mock paper, enter your right answers section by section. Look first at the total against 90, then at the section where you lost most. If one section is far behind the others, give it the next week. If all sections are close, work on speed: more timed papers.

What the result means

The tool cannot tell you your real score, because mock papers differ from the real paper. Use it to compare yourself with yourself over several papers, and treat a rise over three papers as a better sign than one good paper.

Questions people ask

Is there a negative mark?

No. The bulletin says there is no negative marking.

What is the pass mark?

60% or more is a pass, which is 90 of 150 marks.

Does this give a rank?

No. CTET gives marks and a pass or fail result; this tool only adds your counts.
