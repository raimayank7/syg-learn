CTET Paper II syllabus: classes VI to VIII

Paper II is for classes VI to VIII. The bulletin lists the content in Appendix I and, for the detailed content of classes VI to VIII, points to the NCERT syllabus and textbooks.

I. Child Development and Pedagogy (30 questions)

The same headings as Paper I, but about the elementary school child (age group 11 to 14 years): 15 questions on child development, 5 on inclusive education and children with special needs, 10 on learning and pedagogy. See the full list under Paper I.

II. Mathematics and Science (60 questions, for a mathematics and science teacher)

Mathematics (30 questions: content 20, pedagogy 10)

  • Number system: knowing our numbers, playing with numbers, whole numbers, negative numbers and integers, fractions
  • Algebra: introduction to algebra, ratio and proportion
  • Geometry: basic geometrical ideas (2-D), understanding elementary shapes (2-D and 3-D), symmetry (reflection), construction (using a straight edge, scale, protractor and compasses), mensuration
  • Data handling
  • Pedagogical issues: nature of mathematics and logical thinking, place of mathematics in the curriculum, language of mathematics, community mathematics, evaluation, remedial teaching, problems of teaching

Science (30 questions: content 20, pedagogy 10)

  • Food: sources of food, components of food, cleaning food
  • Materials: materials of daily use
  • The world of the living; moving things, people and ideas
  • How things work: electric current and circuits, magnets
  • Natural phenomena; natural resources
  • Pedagogical issues: nature and structure of sciences, aims and objectives, understanding and appreciating science, approaches and the integrated approach, observation, experiment and discovery, innovation, text material and aids, evaluation (cognitive, psycho-motor, affective), problems, remedial teaching

III. Social Studies / Social Science (60 questions, for a social studies teacher)

Content (40 questions)

  • History: from the earliest societies and the first farmers to the first cities, early states, new ideas, the first empire, contacts with distant lands, political developments, culture and science, new kings and kingdoms, the Sultans of Delhi, architecture, the creation of an empire, social change and regional cultures, then the establishment of Company power, rural life and society, colonialism and tribal societies, the Revolt of 1857-58, women and reform, challenging the caste system, the nationalist movement, and India after independence
  • Geography: geography as a social study and as a science, planet Earth in the solar system, the globe, the natural and human environment, air, water, human environment (settlement, transport and communication), resources (natural and human) and agriculture
  • Social and political life: diversity, government, local government, making a living, democracy, state government, understanding media, unpacking gender, the Constitution, parliamentary government, the judiciary, social justice and the marginalised

Pedagogical issues (20 questions)

Concept and nature of social science, classroom processes, activities and discourse, developing critical thinking, enquiry and empirical evidence, problems of teaching, primary and secondary sources, project work and evaluation.

IV. Language I and V. Language II (30 questions each)

The same as in Paper I: comprehension from unseen passages (15) and pedagogy of language development (15) in each language. See Paper I.

Which third part to take

If you teach mathematics or science you take the Mathematics and Science part. If you teach social studies or social science you take that part. For any other teacher the bulletin says either one will do, so pick the one you know better.
