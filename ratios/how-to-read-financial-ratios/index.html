How to read financial ratios without being misled

This is our own advice. A ratio is a summary, and a summary can hide as much as it shows.

Compare, do not judge alone

A single ratio has little meaning. Compare the same business over three or more years, and compare it with similar businesses in the same trade.

Use several together

QuestionLook at
Can it pay its bills soon?Current, quick and cash ratio, and receivable and payable days
Is it earning enough?Margins, return on assets, return on equity
Is it too dependent on borrowing?Debt to equity, debt ratio, interest cover
Is it using its money well?Inventory days, asset turnover

Check the definition

Some books use average balances, 360 days or different pieces of debt. When you compare two sets of ratios, make sure the same definitions were used.

What ratios cannot show

  • Whether the figures are honest or complete.
  • One-off items, seasonal swings and changes in accounting policy.
  • The future.

For exams

Use the formula your textbook gives, write the working, and say in a sentence what the number means.

These are common textbook definitions, using the closing figures you type and 365 days a year. Books and companies differ (some use average balances), so use the definition your course or lender uses. We give no target values and no advice on any business.
