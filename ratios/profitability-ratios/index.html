Profitability ratios: margins, return on assets and equity

Profitability ratios ask how much profit a business makes from its sales, from its assets and from its owners' money.

RatioHow it is worked outSample result
Gross profit margin(revenue − cost of goods sold) / revenue × 10035.00 %
Operating profit marginoperating profit / revenue × 10012.50 %
Net profit marginnet profit / revenue × 1007.50 %
Return on assetsnet profit / total assets × 10010.00 %
Return on equitynet profit / shareholders' equity × 10023.68 %

Gross profit margin

The share of each unit of sales left after the direct cost of the goods sold. It shows how much is left to pay all other costs and make a profit.

Operating profit margin

The share of sales left after the day-to-day costs of running the business, before interest.

Net profit margin

The share of sales left as profit after all costs, interest and tax in the figure you type as net profit.

Return on assets

How much profit the business earned for each 100 units of assets it holds.

Return on equity

How much profit the business earned for each 100 units of the owners' own money in it.

The sample figures (in any one currency) are: revenue 1,200,000, cost of goods sold 780,000, operating profit 150,000, interest 30,000, net profit 90,000, cash 60,000, receivables 150,000, inventory 140,000, current assets 380,000, total assets 900,000, payables 110,000, current liabilities 250,000, total liabilities 520,000, equity 380,000, borrowings 300,000. They are made up.

Using them

  • Margins compare costs with sales; returns compare profit with what was invested.
  • Return on equity can look high when a business has borrowed a lot, so read it with the debt ratios.

These are common textbook definitions, using the closing figures you type and 365 days a year. Books and companies differ (some use average balances), so use the definition your course or lender uses. We give no target values and no advice on any business.
