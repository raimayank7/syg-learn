Simple final accounts from a trial balance

Final accounts are the summary of a period: a statement of profit (or loss) and a statement of what the business has and owes at the end. They are built directly from the trial balance by sorting each account into its kind. The tool above does the sorting for you once you choose a type for each account.

The steps

  1. Make sure the trial balance agrees first.
  2. Sort each account: income and costs go to the profit statement; assets, liabilities, capital and drawings go to the position statement.
  3. Gross profit = sales less direct costs such as purchases. Net profit = gross profit plus other income less other expenses.
  4. Closing capital = opening capital + net profit − drawings.
  5. Check that assets equal capital plus liabilities. This always works when the trial balance agrees, so it is not a check on your sorting. The real check on sorting is to look at each balance: an asset or a cost should have a debit balance, and a liability, capital or income a credit balance.

Worked example

The trial balance of the ten example entries was sorted by type and worked out by a program.

Profit statement

ItemAmount
Sales or service income350,000.00
Less: direct costs(150,000.00)
Gross profit200,000.00
Add: other income0.00
Less: other expenses(45,000.00)
Net profit155,000.00

Position statement

ItemAmount
Capital at the start (as in the trial balance)500,000.00
Add: net profit155,000.00
Less: drawings(20,000.00)
Closing capital635,000.00
Liabilities0.00
Capital + liabilities635,000.00
Assets635,000.00

The net profit is 155,000.00 and the closing capital is 635,000.00. Assets of 635,000.00 equal capital plus liabilities, so the position statement balances.

What this simple version leaves out

  • Closing stock of goods unsold: without it, gross profit is wrong for any business that keeps stock.
  • Accrued and prepaid expenses, depreciation and bad debts: put these in as journal entries first, then use the adjusted trial balance.
  • The exact headings and order your board uses. Learn its layout for exams; the arithmetic here is the same.
