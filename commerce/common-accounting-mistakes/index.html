Accounting mistakes a trial balance cannot catch

When the trial balance agrees, it is tempting to stop. But the totals can agree while the books are wrong. This page takes the ten example transactions, introduces one mistake at a time, and records what the trial balance does. The last column was worked out by a program, not guessed.

Errors tried on the example books

Kind of errorWhat was doneDoes the trial balance show it?
Error of omissionEntry 7, paying Meera Traders, was never written (both sides missing).No: totals agree
Error of commissionThe rent was debited to the Wages account instead of Rent (a wrong account of the same kind).No: totals agree
Error of principleThe furniture was debited to the Rent account, so an asset was treated as an expense.No: totals agree
Error in the original entryThe wages were recorded as 3,000 on both sides instead of 30,000.No: totals agree
Compensating errorsSales were credited 1,000 too much, and wages were debited 1,000 too much.No: totals agree
One side left outThe wages were debited but the credit to Cash was forgotten.Yes: totals differ by 30,000.00
Different amounts on each sideThe rent was debited 15,000 but credited 5,100.Yes: totals differ by 9,900.00
Both sides posted the wrong way roundThe rent entry was reversed: Cash debited and Rent credited. (Totals still agree.)No: totals agree
Wrong amount on one side onlyThe sales credit of entry 4 was posted as 520,000 instead of 250,000 (digits mixed up).Yes: totals differ by 270,000.00

What the pattern tells you

  • Errors that affect both sides equally (a missing entry, a wrong account of the same side, a wrong amount on both sides) leave the totals equal. Only checking source documents, or a second person reviewing, finds them.
  • Errors that affect one side only make the totals differ, and the difference points at where to look.
  • Two errors can cancel each other out. The more entries you check, the less likely this slips through.

Habits that catch the rest

  1. Read each narration back: does the accounts debited and credited match what happened?
  2. Tick each journal line when posted to the ledger.
  3. Compare ledger balances with outside evidence: the cash in the till, the bank statement (see bank reconciliation), a supplier's statement.
