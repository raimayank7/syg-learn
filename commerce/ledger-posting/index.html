How to post to the ledger, step by step

The ledger has one account for each thing you want to know the total of: Cash, Sales, Rent, each customer, each supplier. Posting means copying each line of the journal into the account it names, on the same side (debit or credit) that the journal used.

The steps

  1. Take the next journal line. Find or open the account it names.
  2. Write the amount on the debit side if the journal line was a debit, on the credit side if it was a credit.
  3. Note which entry it came from, and which other accounts were in the same entry. This makes it easy to trace later.
  4. After all lines are posted, add each side of each account and find the balance: the difference between the two sides.

Reading a balance

If the debit side is bigger the account has a debit balance (Dr); if the credit side is bigger it has a credit balance (Cr). Assets and expenses normally end with a debit balance; liabilities, capital and income with a credit balance. An account with the "wrong" balance, such as a bank account with a credit balance, is worth a second look: it may be an overdraft or it may be a mistake.

Why bother with the ledger?

The journal tells the story in time order. The ledger gathers the story by account, so you can see at a glance how much cash is left, how much a customer owes or how much was spent on rent. The next step, the trial balance, lists every account balance in one place.
