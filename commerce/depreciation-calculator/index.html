Depreciation calculator: straight line and reducing balance

Depreciation spreads the cost of a long-lasting asset over the years it is used, instead of treating the whole cost as an expense in the year it was bought. This calculator builds the year-by-year schedule for the two methods most courses teach. Enter the cost, the scrap value you expect at the end, and the number of whole years.

The two methods

  • Straight line: the same amount every year: (cost − scrap value) ÷ years.
  • Reducing balance (written down value): a fixed percentage of the value at the start of each year, so the charge is highest in the first year and falls every year.

Worked example 1: straight line

Cost 500,000.00, scrap value 50,000.00, 5 years. Each year is (500,000.00 − 50,000.00) ÷ 5 = 90,000.00.

When the division is not exact

Dividing a cost of 100,000.00 by 3 years gives 33,333.33, 33,333.33 and 33,333.34. The calculator rounds to whole paise or cents and puts the odd paisa in the last year, so the total always equals the cost less the scrap value.

The journal entry

Each year the charge is recorded as: Depreciation account Dr, to the asset account (or to an accumulated depreciation account, if your course uses one) Cr. Depreciation is a non-cash expense: no money leaves the bank. Whole years only: if an asset is bought part-way through a year, follow the rule your course gives.
