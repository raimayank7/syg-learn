Journal entry practice problems with answers

Try each problem on paper before you open the answer. For every one, decide which accounts are touched, what kind of account each is, and whether it grew or fell; then write the debit and credit lines. If you are unsure of the sides, read the debit and credit rules first.

All eight problems are about one made-up trader whose books use a bank account instead of cash. The answers were generated by a program from the list of lines, and the program checks that every entry has equal debits and credits and that the trial balance of all eight entries agrees (427,500.00 on each side).

Problem 1

A trader starts a business by putting 300,000.00 into a new bank account.

Show the answer

    To Capital A/c300,000.00
(Being the owner started the business with a bank deposit)

After the eight entries

The bank account ends with a debit balance of 278,300.00. To see this and the other account balances, type all eight entries into the journal tool, or open it with the practice entries below the tool's instructions. Writing the ledger accounts yourself first is the best practice for a test.

Make more problems

Change the amounts in any problem, or turn a cash transaction into a credit one: a purchase on credit credits the supplier instead of the bank. Each change gives a new problem with the same method.

All names, businesses and figures on this site are made up. This site teaches only the universal double-entry method: every transaction has equal debits and credits, and assets equal liabilities plus capital. Boards, universities and accountancy bodies set their own formats, terms and rules, so always follow the format your own course or examiner asks for. We state no tax rule, rate or limit.
