Journal entries explained with a worked example

The journal is the first book of record. Each transaction is written once, in date order, as a journal entry: the accounts to debit, the accounts to credit, the amounts, and a short explanation called the narration. Only after that are the amounts copied into the ledger.

The parts of an entry

  • Debit lines come first. The account name is written with "Dr".
  • Credit lines follow, indented, with "To" before the name.
  • The amounts go in two columns, debit and credit. The column totals of one entry are always equal.
  • The narration says what happened, often starting with "Being".

Simple and compound entries

A simple entry has one debit and one credit. A compound entry has three or more lines because one transaction touches several accounts. Entry 3 below is compound: goods were bought, part was paid in cash and the rest was left owing. It debits Purchases 150,000.00 and credits Cash 100,000.00 and Meera Traders 50,000.00: the credits add up to the debit.

Common slips

  • Debiting the wrong account because the words in the question are misleading: "paid wages" debits Wages, it credits Cash.
  • Forgetting the second half of a credit transaction: goods sold on credit debit the customer, not Cash.
  • Writing a narration but no amounts on one side. An entry that does not balance on paper will not balance in the tool either.
