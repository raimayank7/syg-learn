Trial balance: how to prepare it and check it

A trial balance is a list of every ledger account with its balance, split into two columns: debit balances and credit balances. Because every entry had equal debits and credits, the two columns should add up to the same total. This is the first check on the books.

How to prepare one

  1. Find the balance of every ledger account (see ledger posting).
  2. Write each account name once. Put a debit balance in the debit column and a credit balance in the credit column.
  3. Add both columns. The totals should be equal.

The trial balance of the example

If the totals do not agree

  1. Add the columns again; a slip in adding is the commonest cause.
  2. Divide the difference by 2. If the result is an amount in your books, an entry may have been posted to the wrong side.
  3. If the difference is exactly divisible by 9, two digits may have been swapped (for example 540 written as 450).
  4. Check that every ledger balance was copied correctly, and every account was included.
  5. Check that every journal entry has equal debits and credits. The journal tool does this for you in its first section.

What agreement does and does not prove

Equal totals show the double entry was done consistently. They do not show that the right accounts were used, that nothing was left out, or that the amounts are true. Some mistakes leave the totals equal.
