NISM Series VIII: Equity Derivatives examination

Futures and options on shares and indices: how they are priced, traded, settled and used. A calculation-heavy paper with negative marking.

Who takes it

Series VIII is the examination for people who work in the equity derivatives segment of a trading member: dealers, sales staff and those who support them. Employers in broking name it as a requirement for such roles. It is also taken by traders and students who want a structured understanding of futures and options. If you are taking it for a job, confirm with the employer's compliance team that this, and not the Common Derivatives examination (Series XIII), is the one required.

How to prepare for this paper

Two things set this paper apart from the mutual fund papers: a pass mark of 60%, and a deduction of a quarter mark for every wrong answer. Sixty correct answers with forty wrong ones is a score of 50 and a fail.

  • Master payoffs. Every option question at expiry is the same small calculation. Work through option payoffs until you can do one in twenty seconds.
  • Master futures arithmetic: profit and loss, contract value, cost of carry, basis, hedging with beta. See futures pricing.
  • Learn strategies by drawing them. For each spread, straddle and strangle, sketch the payoff and mark the maximum profit, maximum loss and break-even points. A drawn strategy is remembered; a memorised table is not.
  • Read trading, clearing, margins and regulation from the current workbook. Contract specifications, margin rules and position limits are revised by the exchanges and the regulator; do not trust figures from old notes or from any website, including this one.
  • Practise with the penalty on. Our timed test deducts 0.25 for wrong answers in this module. Learn to leave pure guesses: see negative marking.

After passing

The certificate validity shown on NISM's page is three years; read the module's FAQ for how to renew. A certificate shows knowledge of the product; it is not a recommendation to trade. Derivatives are leveraged, and losses can exceed the money put up as margin.
