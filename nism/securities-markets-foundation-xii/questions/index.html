Securities Markets Foundation (Series XII): 30 practice questions with answers

Our own questions on the subjects of this examination, under 8 topic headings. Try each before opening its answer.

Take these as a test About the examination

How to use this page

Read a question, choose your answer, and only then open "Show the answer". Each answer has a line of explanation; if it surprises you, find the topic in NISM's workbook and read that part again. The questions are written by us in the four kinds a multiple choice paper uses: definitions, roles, rules of principle and calculations. They are not NISM's questions and the real paper will word things differently, so learn the idea, not the sentence. We have left out figures that the regulator or the exchanges revise, such as tax rates, limits, timings and margins: take those from the current workbook. The real paper has 100 questions in 120 minutes with a pass mark of 60%; there is no negative marking.

The questions

Securities markets

1. The market in which a company issues new securities to investors for the first time is the

  1. derivatives market
  2. money market
  3. primary market
  4. secondary market
Show the answer to question 1

C. primary market

New issues are made in the primary market. Trading among investors after listing takes place in the secondary market.

2. In the secondary market, an investor who sells shares receives money from

  1. SEBI
  2. the buyer, through the exchange settlement system
  3. the registrar
  4. the company
Show the answer to question 2

B. the buyer, through the exchange settlement system

Secondary market trades are between investors. The company whose shares are traded receives nothing.

3. An equity share represents

  1. part ownership of the company
  2. a loan to the company
  3. a guarantee of dividend
  4. a deposit with the company
Show the answer to question 3

A. part ownership of the company

Shareholders are owners and share in profits and losses. Lenders hold debt securities.

4. Which security pays a fixed rate of interest and returns the principal at maturity?

  1. Index future
  2. Bond or debenture
  3. Call option
  4. Equity share
Show the answer to question 4

B. Bond or debenture

Bonds and debentures are debt securities with a coupon and a maturity date.

5. The regulator of the securities markets in India is

  1. the Ministry of Commerce
  2. the Securities and Exchange Board of India
  3. the stock exchanges
  4. the Reserve Bank of India
Show the answer to question 5

B. the Securities and Exchange Board of India

SEBI regulates securities markets, intermediaries and issuers, and protects investors.

Primary market

6. An Initial Public Offer is

  1. a bonus issue
  2. a buy-back of shares
  3. the first sale of a company's shares to the public
  4. a rights issue
Show the answer to question 6

C. the first sale of a company's shares to the public

In an IPO a company offers shares to the public for the first time and gets them listed.

7. An issue of new shares offered to existing shareholders in proportion to their holding is a

  1. bonus issue
  2. rights issue
  3. private placement
  4. offer for sale
Show the answer to question 7

B. rights issue

A rights issue gives existing shareholders the right to buy new shares, usually at a discount to the market price.

8. The offer document issued by a company for a public issue is the

  1. balance sheet
  2. prospectus
  3. contract note
  4. account statement
Show the answer to question 8

B. prospectus

The prospectus gives details of the company, the issue, its risks and its financials.

Secondary market

9. A document given by a stock broker to a client confirming the trades done on a day is the

  1. contract note
  2. delivery instruction slip
  3. prospectus
  4. allotment advice
Show the answer to question 9

A. contract note

The contract note is the legal record of the trade, showing price, quantity, brokerage and charges.

10. An order to buy or sell at the best price currently available is a

  1. limit order
  2. good-till-cancelled order
  3. market order
  4. stop-loss order
Show the answer to question 10

C. market order

A market order executes at once at prevailing prices. A limit order executes only at the stated price or better.

11. Holding securities in electronic form is known as

  1. dematerialisation
  2. pledging
  3. rematerialisation
  4. transmission
Show the answer to question 11

A. dematerialisation

Dematerialisation converts physical certificates into electronic holdings in a demat account with a depository participant.

12. A depository participant is

  1. an agent of the depository through whom investors hold demat accounts
  2. a stock exchange
  3. a mutual fund
  4. a credit rating agency
Show the answer to question 12

A. an agent of the depository through whom investors hold demat accounts

Investors open demat accounts with depository participants, who act as agents of the depository.

13. Market capitalisation of a company is

  1. its total assets
  2. its paid-up capital
  3. its annual profit
  4. the market price per share multiplied by the number of shares
Show the answer to question 13

D. the market price per share multiplied by the number of shares

Market cap = price × number of shares outstanding. It is the market's valuation of the company's equity.

Corporate actions

14. When a company issues bonus shares in the ratio 1:1, a shareholder with 100 shares will hold

  1. 100 shares
  2. 150 shares
  3. 200 shares
  4. 50 shares
Show the answer to question 14

C. 200 shares

A 1:1 bonus gives one new share for each share held, so 100 becomes 200. The share price adjusts downward so that total value is broadly unchanged.

15. A share of face value ₹10 is split into shares of face value ₹2. A holder of 1 old share gets

  1. 5 shares
  2. 10 shares
  3. 2 shares
  4. 20 shares
Show the answer to question 15

A. 5 shares

10 ÷ 2 = 5. A split increases the number of shares and reduces the price per share proportionately.

16. A dividend is

  1. a fee paid to the broker
  2. interest on debentures
  3. a tax on shares
  4. a part of profit distributed to shareholders
Show the answer to question 16

D. a part of profit distributed to shareholders

Dividend is a distribution of profit. It is not guaranteed and depends on the company's decision.

Debt securities

17. A bond with a face value of ₹1,000 pays an annual coupon of ₹80. If its market price is ₹800, the current yield is

  1. 10%
  2. 8%
  3. 12.5%
  4. 6.4%
Show the answer to question 17

A. 10%

Current yield = annual coupon ÷ market price = 80 ÷ 800 = 10%.

18. When market interest rates fall, the price of an existing fixed-rate bond will generally

  1. not change
  2. fall
  3. become equal to face value
  4. rise
Show the answer to question 18

D. rise

An existing bond paying a higher coupon becomes more attractive, so its price rises. Price and yield move inversely.

19. A credit rating indicates

  1. an opinion on the issuer's ability to repay its debt on time
  2. the expected return on a share
  3. the dividend policy
  4. the liquidity of a share
Show the answer to question 19

A. an opinion on the issuer's ability to repay its debt on time

Rating agencies assess the likelihood of timely payment of interest and principal on a debt instrument.

20. Treasury bills are

  1. short-term borrowing instruments of the central government
  2. long-term corporate bonds
  3. mutual fund units
  4. equity shares of banks
Show the answer to question 20

A. short-term borrowing instruments of the central government

T-bills are short-term government securities issued at a discount and redeemed at face value.

Mutual funds

21. The price at which units of an open-ended mutual fund scheme are bought and redeemed is based on the

  1. issue price
  2. market capitalisation
  3. face value
  4. net asset value
Show the answer to question 21

D. net asset value

Transactions take place at NAV-related prices. NAV is the value of the scheme's net assets per unit.

22. An Exchange Traded Fund is

  1. a fund whose units are listed and traded on a stock exchange
  2. a derivative contract
  3. a fixed deposit
  4. an insurance plan
Show the answer to question 22

A. a fund whose units are listed and traded on a stock exchange

ETFs usually track an index and can be bought and sold on the exchange like shares.

Derivatives

23. A futures contract is an agreement to

  1. receive dividends
  2. insure a portfolio
  3. buy or sell an asset at a future date at a price agreed today
  4. lend money at a fixed rate
Show the answer to question 23

C. buy or sell an asset at a future date at a price agreed today

A futures contract fixes today the price for a transaction that will be settled on a future date.

24. The seller of an option is also called the

  1. holder
  2. writer
  3. hedger
  4. custodian
Show the answer to question 24

B. writer

The writer receives the premium and has the obligation if the buyer exercises.

Financial planning

25. The first step in financial planning is to

  1. buy shares
  2. sell old investments
  3. take a loan
  4. identify goals and assess the present financial position
Show the answer to question 25

D. identify goals and assess the present financial position

Planning begins with goals, time horizons and the current position of income, expenses, assets and liabilities.

26. An emergency fund should be kept in

  1. real estate
  2. long-term locked-in products
  3. liquid, low-risk instruments
  4. small cap shares
Show the answer to question 26

C. liquid, low-risk instruments

Money for emergencies must be safe and quickly accessible.

27. Diversification reduces

  1. all risk
  2. inflation
  3. taxes
  4. the risk specific to individual securities
Show the answer to question 27

D. the risk specific to individual securities

Spreading investments reduces company-specific (unsystematic) risk. Market-wide (systematic) risk remains.

28. The power of compounding means that

  1. returns are guaranteed
  2. risk disappears with time
  3. earnings themselves earn returns over time
  4. taxes are avoided
Show the answer to question 28

C. earnings themselves earn returns over time

With compounding, returns are earned on both the principal and accumulated returns, so growth accelerates over long periods.

29. ₹1,00,000 invested at 10% a year compounded annually becomes, after two years,

  1. ₹1,20,000
  2. ₹1,21,000
  3. ₹1,10,000
  4. ₹1,25,000
Show the answer to question 29

B. ₹1,21,000

1,00,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = ₹1,21,000.

30. An investor with a long time horizon and high risk tolerance would generally have a higher allocation to

  1. equity
  2. cash
  3. savings account
  4. treasury bills
Show the answer to question 30

A. equity

Equity has higher expected long-term returns with higher short-term volatility, which suits long horizons and higher risk tolerance.
