Securities markets glossary

75 terms from the mutual fund, derivatives and research papers, each in one plain sentence.

Absolute return
The total percentage gain or loss over a period, without regard to its length.
American option
An option that can be exercised on any day up to expiry.
Arbitrage
Buying in one market and selling in another at the same time to gain from a price difference, without price risk.
Asset allocation
Dividing money among kinds of asset, such as equity, debt and gold, to suit goals and risk.
Asset management company (AMC)
The company that manages the investments of a mutual fund's schemes.
At the money
An option whose strike price equals the current price of the underlying.
Basis
The spot price minus the futures price.
Benchmark
An index against which the performance of a scheme or portfolio is compared.
Beta
A measure of how much a share or portfolio moves when the market moves.
Bond
A debt security: the issuer borrows and promises interest and repayment.
Bonus issue
Additional shares given free to existing shareholders in a fixed ratio.
Book value
The value of a company's equity as shown in its balance sheet.
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate: the yearly compounded rate that turns the beginning value into the end value.
Call option
A right, without obligation, to buy the underlying at the strike price.
Clearing corporation
The body that guarantees settlement of trades on an exchange and collects margins.
Closed-ended scheme
A scheme open for subscription only during its offer; units are then traded on an exchange.
Contango
A market in which the futures price is above the spot price.
Corporate action
An event started by a company that affects its securities, such as a dividend, bonus, split or rights issue.
Cost of carry
The cost of holding an asset until a future date: mainly interest, less any income from the asset.
Coupon
The interest rate a bond pays on its face value.
Custodian
The entity that holds the securities of a mutual fund or other institution in safe keeping.
Delta
The change in an option's premium for a one rupee change in the underlying.
Dematerialisation
Converting paper certificates into electronic holdings in a depository account.
Depository
An institution that holds securities in electronic form.
Derivative
A contract whose value comes from the price of something else, called the underlying.
Diversification
Spreading money over many securities so that no single one decides the result.
Dividend yield
Dividend per share divided by the market price.
Earnings per share (EPS)
Profit available to equity shareholders divided by the number of shares.
Entry and exit load
A charge on buying or redeeming units. An exit load is deducted from the NAV at redemption.
European option
An option that can be exercised only at expiry.
Expense ratio
The yearly expenses of a scheme as a percentage of its assets.
Face value
The nominal value of a share or bond, as stated by the issuer.
Forward contract
A private agreement to buy or sell at a fixed price on a future date.
Fundamental analysis
Judging a security by studying the economy, the industry and the company's finances.
Futures contract
A standardised forward traded on an exchange and settled through a clearing corporation.
Hedging
Taking a position that offsets the risk of another.
In the money
An option that has intrinsic value: a call with spot above strike, or a put with spot below strike.
Index
A number that tracks the prices of a chosen group of securities.
Initial margin
Money collected upfront when a derivatives position is opened.
Initial public offer (IPO)
The first sale of a company's shares to the public.
Intrinsic value
What an option would be worth if exercised now; never less than zero.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be turned into cash without moving its price.
Lot size
The fixed quantity of the underlying in one derivatives contract.
Mark to market
Valuing a position at the current price and settling the gain or loss daily.
Market capitalisation
Share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding.
Net asset value (NAV)
The value of one unit of a scheme: assets less liabilities, divided by units.
Open interest
The number of derivatives contracts outstanding at a point in time.
Open-ended scheme
A scheme that sells and redeems units on every business day at NAV-based prices.
Out of the money
An option with no intrinsic value.
Premium
The price paid by the buyer of an option to the seller.
Price to earnings ratio (P/E)
Share price divided by earnings per share.
Primary market
The market in which securities are first issued.
Put option
A right, without obligation, to sell the underlying at the strike price.
Registrar and transfer agent
The entity that keeps investor records and processes transactions for a fund or company.
Rights issue
An offer of new shares to existing shareholders in proportion to their holding.
Risk profile
An assessment of how much risk an investor is able and willing to take.
Secondary market
The market in which already issued securities are traded among investors.
Settlement
The exchange of securities and money that completes a trade.
Sharpe ratio
Return above the risk-free rate per unit of total risk.
SIP
Systematic investment plan: investing a fixed amount at regular intervals.
Sponsor
The entity that sets up a mutual fund.
Spot price
The price for immediate delivery.
Standard deviation
A measure of how widely returns vary around their average.
Stock split
Dividing each share into several of smaller face value.
STP
Systematic transfer plan: moving a fixed amount regularly from one scheme to another.
Straddle
Buying (or selling) a call and a put with the same strike and expiry.
Strike price
The price at which an option can be exercised.
SWP
Systematic withdrawal plan: redeeming a fixed amount at regular intervals.
Technical analysis
Judging a security from its past prices and volumes.
Theta
The fall in an option's premium as a day passes, other things equal.
Time value
The part of an option's premium above its intrinsic value.
Trustee
The body that holds a mutual fund's property for the benefit of unit holders and oversees the AMC.
Underlying
The asset or index on which a derivative is based.
Volatility
The degree to which a price moves up and down.
Yield to maturity
The yearly return on a bond bought at its current price and held until it is repaid.

Why definitions matter in these papers

A large share of any multiple choice paper on finance is vocabulary. The question gives a description and asks for the term, or gives the term and asks which statement about it is true. The wrong options are usually neighbouring terms: basis and spread, intrinsic value and time value, sponsor and trustee, primary and secondary market. So it is not enough to recognise a word; you must be able to tell it from the words beside it.

Pairs that are confused

  • Sponsor, trustee, AMC: the sponsor sets the fund up; the trustee holds its property for unit holders and supervises; the AMC manages the money.
  • Custodian and registrar: the custodian keeps the securities; the registrar keeps the investors' records.
  • Forward and future: both fix a price for a later date; a future is standardised, exchange traded and guaranteed by a clearing corporation.
  • Intrinsic value and time value: together they make the premium. Intrinsic value is what exercise would give now; the rest is time value.
  • Volume and open interest: volume counts trades in a period; open interest counts contracts still outstanding.
  • Absolute return and CAGR: the first ignores time; the second is the compounded yearly rate.
  • Bonus and split: both increase the number of shares and lower the price in proportion; a bonus capitalises reserves, a split divides the face value.

Using the glossary

These are our own short definitions, written to be understood, not to be quoted. The official workbook defines each term more exactly and in the regulatory sense where one exists; when the two differ, learn the workbook's. A good exercise is to cover the right-hand side, define each term aloud in your own words, and check. Then do the reverse.
