Option payoffs: calls, puts, buyers and sellers

Every option question at expiry comes down to one comparison, between the strike price and the price of the underlying, and one subtraction, of the premium.

The four positions

Buy a call (long call)Right to buy at the strikePrice will riseUnlimitedPremium paid
Sell a call (short call)Obligation to sell at the strike if exercisedPrice will not risePremium receivedUnlimited
Buy a put (long put)Right to sell at the strikePrice will fallStrike − premium (if the price falls to zero)Premium paid

Formulas at expiry

  • Call payoff = the greater of (Spot − Strike) and 0.
  • Put payoff = the greater of (Strike − Spot) and 0.
  • Buyer's profit = Payoff − Premium.
  • Seller's profit = Premium − Payoff.
  • Break-even, call = Strike + Premium. Break-even, put = Strike − Premium.

The buyer's gain is always the seller's loss, rupee for rupee.

Intrinsic value and time value

Before expiry, an option's premium has two parts.

  • Intrinsic value is what the option would be worth if exercised now: for a call, Spot − Strike if positive, otherwise zero; for a put, Strike − Spot if positive, otherwise zero.
  • Time value = Premium − Intrinsic value. It reflects the chance that the option gains value before expiry.

Example. A call with strike ₹90 trades at ₹14 when the share is at ₹100. Intrinsic value = ₹10. Time value = 14 − 10 = ₹4. An out-of-the-money option has no intrinsic value; its whole premium is time value. At expiry, time value is zero.

Lot size

Exchange-traded options are traded in lots. Multiply the per-share figure by the lot size to get the rupee profit or loss. With a lot of 500 shares, the call buyer above who makes ₹7 a share at an expiry price of ₹112 earns 7 × 500 = ₹3,500. Lot sizes and contract specifications are set by the exchanges and are revised; a question will state the lot size to use.

Steps for any question

  1. Call or put? Buyer or seller?
  2. Compute the payoff from strike and expiry price.
  3. Subtract the premium for the buyer; subtract the payoff from the premium for the seller.
  4. Multiply by lot size and number of lots if a rupee total is asked.
  5. Check the sign: profit or loss.

Common slips

  • Forgetting the premium and giving the payoff as the profit.
  • Treating a put like a call.
  • Giving the buyer's answer when the seller's is asked.
  • Saying the seller of a call has limited loss. The loss is unlimited, because the price can rise without limit.
  • Thinking an out-of-the-money option at expiry has some value. It has none.

Questions candidates ask

How do I calculate the profit on a call option?

At expiry, payoff = spot price minus strike price, if positive, otherwise zero. The buyer's profit is the payoff minus the premium paid.

What is the break-even of a put option?

Strike price minus the premium.

What is the maximum loss for an option buyer?

The premium paid. An option seller's loss can be much larger, and for a call seller it is unlimited.
