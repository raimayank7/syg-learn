NISM Series V-A: Mutual Fund Distributors examination

The examination most people mean when they say "the NISM exam". NISM describes it as the common minimum knowledge benchmark for everyone involved in selling and distributing mutual funds.

Who takes it

NISM's page says the examination seeks to create a common minimum knowledge benchmark for all persons involved in selling and distributing mutual funds, including individual distributors, employees of organisations engaged in sales and distribution of mutual funds, and employees of asset management companies, especially those in sales and distribution. In practice the candidates are bank and distribution staff, insurance agents adding a second line, commerce graduates looking for a first job in financial services, and people who want to start a distribution business of their own.

How to prepare for this paper

The paper has no negative marking and a pass mark of 50%, which makes it the most approachable of NISM's examinations, and also the one most often failed through under-preparation. The workbook is long, and candidates who read only the first few chapters meet a paper spread across all twelve.

  • Do not skip the regulatory chapters. Legal structure, the regulatory framework, scheme documents and investor services are less interesting than returns, but they are asked directly and reward plain reading.
  • Make the calculations certain marks. NAV, units allotted, exit load, absolute return and CAGR are worked on our NAV and returns page.
  • Learn the scheme categories as a table: what each kind of scheme invests in, its risk, and the investor it suits. Scheme selection questions describe an investor and ask for the suitable scheme.
  • Read figures from the current workbook only. Limits, timings, tax rates and expense limits are revised often; this site deliberately does not state them.
  • Attempt every question. With no negative marking, a blank earns nothing and a reasoned guess may.

After passing

NISM's FAQ says the certificate is valid for three years from the date of the examination, and is renewed by attending NISM's CPE for Mutual Fund Distributors or by passing the examination again before expiry. Passing does not by itself permit anyone to sell mutual funds: registration with the industry body and empanelment with fund houses come after, under rules that are theirs and not NISM's. See registration, fees and validity.
