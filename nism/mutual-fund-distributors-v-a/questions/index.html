Mutual Fund Distributors (Series V-A): 36 practice questions with answers

Our own questions on the subjects of this examination, under 12 topic headings. Try each before opening its answer.

Take these as a test About the examination

How to use this page

Read a question, choose your answer, and only then open "Show the answer". Each answer has a line of explanation; if it surprises you, find the topic in NISM's workbook and read that part again. The questions are written by us in the four kinds a multiple choice paper uses: definitions, roles, rules of principle and calculations. They are not NISM's questions and the real paper will word things differently, so learn the idea, not the sentence. We have left out figures that the regulator or the exchanges revise, such as tax rates, limits, timings and margins: take those from the current workbook. The real paper has 100 questions in 120 minutes with a pass mark of 50%; there is no negative marking.

The questions

Investment landscape

1. Which of these is a financial asset, as distinct from a physical asset?

  1. A plot of land
  2. A house
  3. A bank fixed deposit
  4. Gold jewellery
Show the answer to question 1

C. A bank fixed deposit

A fixed deposit is a claim on a bank, so it is a financial asset. Land, gold and a house are physical assets.

2. Inflation risk for an investor means that

  1. the issuer may not repay
  2. prices of all shares fall together
  3. the purchasing power of the money returned is lower
  4. the investment cannot be sold quickly
Show the answer to question 2

C. the purchasing power of the money returned is lower

Inflation reduces what money can buy. If the return is below inflation, the investor is poorer in real terms even though the amount has grown.

3. An investor keeps all savings in a savings bank account for a goal twenty years away. The main risk is

  1. high volatility
  2. credit risk
  3. liquidity risk
  4. inflation eroding the real value
Show the answer to question 3

D. inflation eroding the real value

A savings account is safe and liquid but its return may not keep pace with inflation over long periods.

4. Asset allocation refers to

  1. dividing investments among asset classes such as equity, debt and gold
  2. timing the market
  3. buying only government securities
  4. choosing the best single share
Show the answer to question 4

A. dividing investments among asset classes such as equity, debt and gold

Asset allocation is the division of a portfolio among asset classes according to goals, time horizon and risk profile.

5. An investor who sells winning investments too early and holds losing ones too long is showing

  1. herd behaviour
  2. loss aversion
  3. overconfidence
  4. anchoring
Show the answer to question 5

B. loss aversion

Loss aversion makes the pain of a loss feel larger than the pleasure of an equal gain, so investors avoid booking losses.

Concept and role of a mutual fund

6. A mutual fund is best described as

  1. an insurance policy
  2. a company that guarantees returns
  3. a vehicle that pools money from investors and invests it according to a stated objective
  4. a bank deposit scheme
Show the answer to question 6

C. a vehicle that pools money from investors and invests it according to a stated objective

A mutual fund pools investors' money and invests it in securities in line with the scheme's objective. Returns are not guaranteed.

7. An open-ended scheme is one in which

  1. investors can buy and redeem units with the fund on an ongoing basis
  2. units are listed and cannot be redeemed with the fund
  3. the scheme has a fixed maturity date
  4. the fund invests only in government securities
Show the answer to question 7

A. investors can buy and redeem units with the fund on an ongoing basis

Open-ended schemes accept purchases and redemptions continuously at NAV-related prices. Close-ended schemes have a fixed tenure.

8. Which type of scheme aims to mirror the composition of a market index?

  1. Liquid fund
  2. Sector fund
  3. Balanced fund
  4. Index fund
Show the answer to question 8

D. Index fund

An index fund is passively managed and holds the securities of an index in the same proportion.

9. The difference between the return of an index fund and the return of its index is called

  1. beta
  2. alpha
  3. tracking error or tracking difference
  4. exit load
Show the answer to question 9

C. tracking error or tracking difference

An index fund will not match its index exactly because of expenses and cash holdings. The gap is measured as tracking difference, and its variability as tracking error.

Legal structure

10. In the structure of mutual funds in India, the investments of a scheme are held on behalf of unit holders by the

  1. distributor
  2. sponsor
  3. registrar
  4. trust, through its trustees
Show the answer to question 10

D. trust, through its trustees

A mutual fund in India is constituted as a trust. The trustees hold the property of the fund for the benefit of unit holders.

11. Day to day investment management of a mutual fund scheme is carried out by the

  1. asset management company
  2. sponsor
  3. custodian
  4. trustees
Show the answer to question 11

A. asset management company

The asset management company (AMC) manages the schemes, under the supervision of the trustees.

12. Safekeeping of the securities owned by a scheme is the function of the

  1. distributor
  2. custodian
  3. auditor
  4. registrar and transfer agent
Show the answer to question 12

B. custodian

The custodian holds the scheme's securities. The registrar and transfer agent keeps investor records and processes transactions.

Legal and regulatory framework

13. Mutual funds in India are regulated primarily by

  1. the Securities and Exchange Board of India
  2. the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
  3. the stock exchanges
  4. the Reserve Bank of India
Show the answer to question 13

A. the Securities and Exchange Board of India

SEBI regulates mutual funds. Other regulators come in where a fund's activity touches their domain.

14. AMFI is

  1. a credit rating agency
  2. the industry association of mutual funds in India
  3. the regulator of mutual funds
  4. a stock exchange
Show the answer to question 14

B. the industry association of mutual funds in India

The Association of Mutual Funds in India is the industry body. It is not a regulator, but it sets a code of conduct for intermediaries.

Scheme related information

15. The document that gives the investment objective, asset allocation, risk factors and fees of a particular scheme is the

  1. Scheme Information Document
  2. annual report of the sponsor
  3. Statement of Additional Information
  4. account statement
Show the answer to question 15

A. Scheme Information Document

The Scheme Information Document (SID) has scheme-specific details. The Statement of Additional Information has details of the fund house common to all schemes.

16. A short summary of the key features of a scheme, given with the application form, is the

  1. trust deed
  2. fact sheet
  3. Key Information Memorandum
  4. offer letter
Show the answer to question 16

C. Key Information Memorandum

The Key Information Memorandum (KIM) is a summary of the SID and accompanies the application form.

Distribution

17. A mutual fund distributor earns income mainly in the form of

  1. salary from SEBI
  2. a share of the scheme's profit
  3. interest from the fund
  4. commission paid by the AMC
Show the answer to question 17

D. commission paid by the AMC

Distributors are paid commission by the AMC for selling and servicing. An investment adviser, in contrast, is paid a fee by the client.

18. Under a direct plan of a scheme

  1. returns are guaranteed
  2. the investor invests without a distributor and no distribution commission is charged to the plan
  3. units are bought on the stock exchange only
  4. only companies can invest
Show the answer to question 18

B. the investor invests without a distributor and no distribution commission is charged to the plan

A direct plan has no distributor commission, so its expense ratio is lower than that of the regular plan of the same scheme.

NAV, expenses and pricing

19. A scheme has assets of ₹105 crore, liabilities of ₹5 crore and 5 crore units outstanding. Its NAV per unit is

  1. ₹21
  2. ₹20
  3. ₹19
  4. ₹22
Show the answer to question 19

B. ₹20

NAV = (assets − liabilities) ÷ units = (105 − 5) ÷ 5 = ₹20.

20. An investor puts ₹10,000 into a scheme at an applicable NAV of ₹25, with no entry charge. Units allotted are

  1. 400
  2. 250
  3. 4,000
  4. 2,500
Show the answer to question 20

A. 400

Units = amount ÷ NAV = 10,000 ÷ 25 = 400.

21. A scheme's NAV is ₹50 and the exit load applicable is 1%. An investor redeeming 200 units receives

  1. ₹10,000
  2. ₹9,900
  3. ₹10,100
  4. ₹9,800
Show the answer to question 21

B. ₹9,900

Redemption price = 50 − 1% of 50 = ₹49.50. Amount = 200 × 49.50 = ₹9,900.

22. The total expense ratio of a scheme is

  1. the profit of the AMC
  2. the tax paid by the scheme
  3. the commission paid by the investor on redemption
  4. the annual expenses of the scheme as a percentage of its average net assets
Show the answer to question 22

D. the annual expenses of the scheme as a percentage of its average net assets

TER expresses the scheme's recurring expenses as a percentage of average net assets. It is deducted before the NAV is declared.

Taxation

23. Under the growth option of a scheme

  1. income is paid out every month
  2. income is reinvested at par value
  3. the investor receives bonus units every year
  4. no payouts are made and the gains are reflected in a rising NAV
Show the answer to question 23

D. no payouts are made and the gains are reflected in a rising NAV

In the growth option, profits stay in the scheme and show in the NAV. The investor realises gains on redemption.

24. The gain made when units are redeemed for more than their purchase cost is called

  1. interest
  2. capital gain
  3. dividend
  4. exit load
Show the answer to question 24

B. capital gain

The difference between redemption value and cost is a capital gain. Its tax treatment depends on the type of scheme and the holding period under the tax law in force.

Investor services

25. In a Systematic Investment Plan the investor

  1. invests a fixed amount at regular intervals
  2. invests only at the time of a new fund offer
  3. switches between schemes daily
  4. withdraws a fixed amount at regular intervals
Show the answer to question 25

A. invests a fixed amount at regular intervals

An SIP invests a fixed amount periodically. A Systematic Withdrawal Plan does the opposite.

26. An investor does an SIP of ₹1,000 in two months when the NAV is ₹10 and ₹20. The average cost per unit is

  1. ₹16.67
  2. ₹12.50
  3. ₹13.33
  4. ₹15.00
Show the answer to question 26

C. ₹13.33

Units bought = 100 + 50 = 150 for ₹2,000. Average cost = 2,000 ÷ 150 = ₹13.33, lower than the average NAV of ₹15. This is rupee cost averaging.

27. Moving money from one scheme to another scheme of the same fund house at regular intervals is called a

  1. Systematic Transfer Plan
  2. Systematic Withdrawal Plan
  3. dividend reinvestment
  4. lien
Show the answer to question 27

A. Systematic Transfer Plan

An STP transfers a fixed amount periodically from one scheme to another within the same fund house.

28. KYC stands for

  1. Key Yield Calculation
  2. Keep Your Capital
  3. Know Your Commission
  4. Know Your Customer
Show the answer to question 28

D. Know Your Customer

Know Your Customer is the process of verifying the identity and address of an investor before investment.

Risk, return and performance

29. An investment grows from ₹10,000 to ₹14,400 in two years. The compound annual growth rate is

  1. 44%
  2. 22%
  3. 18%
  4. 20%
Show the answer to question 29

D. 20%

10,000 × (1 + r)² = 14,400, so (1 + r)² = 1.44 and 1 + r = 1.2. CAGR = 20%.

30. The NAV of a scheme rises from ₹20 to ₹23 in six months. The absolute return is

  1. 15%
  2. 3%
  3. 30%
  4. 13%
Show the answer to question 30

A. 15%

Absolute return = (23 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100 = 15%. It is not annualised.

31. Which measure indicates the sensitivity of a scheme's returns to movements in the market?

  1. Beta
  2. Standard deviation
  3. Expense ratio
  4. Modified duration
Show the answer to question 31

A. Beta

Beta measures market (systematic) risk. Standard deviation measures the total variability of returns.

32. In debt funds, the risk that the issuer of a bond fails to pay interest or principal is

  1. reinvestment risk
  2. interest rate risk
  3. liquidity risk
  4. credit risk
Show the answer to question 32

D. credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of default or downgrade. Interest rate risk is the fall in bond prices when rates rise.

33. When interest rates in the economy rise, the prices of existing bonds generally

  1. rise
  2. fall
  3. stay unchanged
  4. become zero
Show the answer to question 33

B. fall

Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions. Longer maturity bonds are affected more.

Scheme performance

34. A scheme earned 12% in a year when the risk-free rate was 6%. The standard deviation of its returns was 10%. Its Sharpe ratio is

  1. 2.0
  2. 0.6
  3. 1.2
  4. 0.5
Show the answer to question 34

B. 0.6

Sharpe ratio = (return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation = (12 − 6) ÷ 10 = 0.6.

35. The standard against which the performance of a scheme is compared is its

  1. NAV
  2. portfolio turnover
  3. expense ratio
  4. benchmark
Show the answer to question 35

D. benchmark

Each scheme states a benchmark index. Its returns are compared with the benchmark's returns over the same periods.

Scheme selection

36. For an investor who needs the money in three months and cannot accept loss of capital, the most suitable category is likely to be

  1. a long duration gilt fund
  2. a liquid or money market fund
  3. a sector fund
  4. a small cap equity fund
Show the answer to question 36

B. a liquid or money market fund

Short horizons and low risk tolerance call for schemes with low volatility. Equity and long duration funds can fall sharply over short periods.
