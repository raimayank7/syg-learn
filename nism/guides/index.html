NISM examination guides

8 guides: five on how the examinations work and how to prepare, three that work through the calculations.

  • NISM negative marking: how it works and how to handle it In some NISM papers a wrong answer costs a quarter of a mark; in others it costs nothing. Knowing which kind you are sitting changes how you should answer.
  • How to prepare for a NISM examination NISM examinations are set from the official workbook for each module. The surest preparation is that workbook, read properly, and then tested.
  • NISM examination day: pacing and method By the day of the examination the reading is done. What remains is to convert what you know into marks without losing any to haste.
  • NISM registration, fees and certificate validity Enrolment for every NISM examination is on NISM's own portal. Fees and validity are printed at the top of each module's page; here they are in one place, with the date we read them.
  • Which NISM examination should you take? NISM runs dozens of certification examinations. The right one is fixed by the work you want to do, and for regulated roles, by what the regulator requires for it.
  • NAV, units and returns: the calculations Six small calculations account for most of the numerical marks in a mutual fund paper. Each takes under a minute once the formula is in your hand.
  • Option payoffs: calls, puts, buyers and sellers Every option question at expiry comes down to one comparison, between the strike price and the price of the underlying, and one subtraction, of the premium.
  • Futures: profit and loss, pricing, basis and margins A futures position gains or loses rupee for rupee with the price. The rest of the arithmetic follows from that and from the cost of holding the asset until expiry.

Two kinds of guide

The first five are about the examination itself. Negative marking lists which examinations deduct marks and shows, with a calculator, what that does to a score. How to prepare is a four-week plan built on NISM's own workbook and test objectives. Exam day gives a method for pacing and for reading multiple choice questions. Registration, fees and validity gathers what NISM's pages say about enrolling, paying, PAN and renewal. Which exam? groups NISM's examinations by subject.

The other three are about the arithmetic that earns the surest marks: NAV and returns for the mutual fund papers, and option payoffs and futures pricing for the derivatives papers. Every example in them is worked in full, and the figures were checked by calculation.

How these guides treat facts

Where a guide states a fee, a pass mark, a rule on negative marking or a validity period, the figure was read on nism.ac.in on 12 November 2026 and the guide says so. Where we did not read NISM's own words on a point, such as what to carry to the test centre, the guide sends you to NISM's candidate instructions instead of guessing. The guides do not state tax rates, expense limits, margins or other regulatory numbers, because those change and belong in the current workbook.

Where to start

If you have not yet chosen an examination, begin with which exam and the table of all exams. If you have enrolled, read how to prepare today and exam day in the last week. If your paper has negative marking, read that guide before your first practice test, so that you practise the habit you will need.
