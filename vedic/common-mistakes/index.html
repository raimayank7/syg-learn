Common mental maths mistakes and how to avoid them

Mistakes with the shortcuts

  • Forgetting a carry. In multiplying by 11 and in the near-100 method a part can reach ten or a hundred. Carry it to the left.
  • Dropping a leading zero. When the units product is a single digit, as in 51 × 59, write it as 09, not 9.
  • Using a shortcut on numbers that do not fit. Check the pattern first.
  • Mixing up the two shortfalls. Subtract one shortfall from the other number, not from its own.

Mistakes in practice

  • Skipping the steps. Go slowly at first; speed comes later.
  • Learning eight at once. One a day is enough.
  • Never checking. Estimate every answer.

A mistake log

Keep a small table in a notebook: the problem, your wrong answer, the right one and which step went wrong (a carry, a zero, the wrong shortfall, a slip in adding). After ten entries the pattern is clear, and it is usually one or two kinds of slip, not many. Practise those two on purpose for a few days.

Speed and accuracy

Many learners try to go fast too early. Accuracy first: aim for ten right of ten at your own pace, then start timing yourself. A shortcut done fast and wrong is worse than the usual method done slowly and right.

How to fix a mistake

When the trainer shows you a wrong answer, read its steps and redo the problem. Write the kind of mistake in a notebook. After a week, you will see which one you repeat.
