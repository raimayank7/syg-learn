Multiplying two numbers just below 100

When both numbers are a little below 100, their shortfalls do the work.

Loading...

The shortcut in three steps

  1. Find how far each is below 100. For 97 × 94 the shortfalls are 3 and 6.
  2. Subtract either shortfall from the other number: 97 − 6 = 91 (or 94 − 3 = 91). This is the hundreds part.
  3. Multiply the shortfalls: 3 × 6 = 18. Add it after the hundreds: 9100 + 18 = 9118. So 97 × 94 = 9118.

Why it works

(100 − x)(100 − y) = 10000 − 100x − 100y + xy = 100(100 − x − y) + xy, and 100 − x − y is the number minus the other shortfall.

Things to watch

Our trainer uses numbers from 85 to 99. If the product of the shortfalls has three digits, as in the last example, carry the hundreds into the left part.

Check your answer

Round both numbers to 100 and subtract: 97 and 94 are about 100 each, so the product is a little under 10000, and 9118 is. The answer always has four digits for numbers from 85 to 99.

How to learn it

  1. Read the steps once with the example.
  2. Try the trainer for ten problems. Work each by the shortcut, then check the steps it shows.
  3. Repeat next day until you can do five in a row without looking.
