Subtracting a number from 1000

To take a number from 1000, no borrowing is needed.

Loading...

The shortcut in three steps

  1. Write the digits of the number. For 346: 3, 4, 6.
  2. Take each digit except the last from 9: 9 − 3 = 6, 9 − 4 = 5.
  3. Take the last digit from 10: 10 − 6 = 4. Read the digits: 654. So 1000 − 346 = 654.

More worked examples

ProblemWorking
5279−5 = 4, 9−2 = 7, 10−7 = 3: 473
8139−8 = 1, 9−1 = 8, 10−3 = 7: 187
9990, 0, 1: 001, which is 1

Why it works

1000 − n = 999 − n + 1. Taking each digit of n from 9 gives 999 − n with no borrowing; the +1 turns the last 9 into a 10.

Things to watch

It works when the last digit of the number is not 0. Our trainer avoids numbers ending in 0 and uses three-digit numbers.

Check your answer

Add the answer to the number: 346 and 654 make 1000. The digits of the number and of the answer add up in pairs to 9, with the last pair adding to 10.

How to learn it

  1. Read the steps once with the example.
  2. Try the trainer for ten problems. Work each by the shortcut, then check the steps it shows.
  3. Repeat next day until you can do five in a row without looking.
