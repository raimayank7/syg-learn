How to read a syllabus file

A CBSE subject file can run from eight to more than thirty pages. Most students look only at the chapter list. The rest of the file tells you how you will be examined.

The parts of a subject file

  1. Rationale and objectives. Why the subject is taught and what a student should be able to do at the end. Worth one reading; the examination is built on these aims.
  2. Course structure. A table of units with their marks. This is the single most useful page: it tells you where the marks are.
  3. Course content. Each unit set out chapter by chapter, often with the exact topics inside each chapter. If a topic is not written here, it is not in the syllabus, even if it is in the textbook.
  4. Learning outcomes or competencies. What you should be able to do with each topic: define, explain, derive, solve, compare. These verbs show the depth expected.
  5. Practicals, projects or internal assessment. How the marks outside the written paper are earned.
  6. Question paper design. How the paper is divided by kind of question and by the thinking it demands.
  7. Prescribed books. The textbooks the syllabus is based on.

Words used in the files

  • Curriculum: the whole plan of studies for a stage. Syllabus: the content of one subject. People use the two words loosely for each other.
  • Unit: a group of related chapters. Marks are usually given by unit.
  • Weightage: the marks given to a unit or to a kind of question.
  • Theory: the written paper. Practical: the laboratory examination in science subjects.
  • Internal assessment: marks given by the school for work during the year.
  • Periodic assessment: the school's pen and paper tests. Portfolio: a collection of the student's classwork and assignments.
  • Subject enrichment: practical work, projects or speaking and listening activities, depending on the subject.
  • Competency-based questions: questions that ask you to apply what you know to a new situation, such as case studies.

Marks for groups of units

Some tables bracket two or three units and give one figure for the bracket. Class 12 Physics does this: Electrostatics and Current Electricity together are 16 marks. You cannot tell from the table how those 16 will be divided, so both units must be prepared. Our pages keep such groups exactly as the Board prints them and do not split the marks by guesswork.

Notes that change what is examined

Read every note and footnote. In the 2026-27 files we met notes saying that a topic is included but will be assessed only in school, that a chapter is to be evaluated for map work only, or that part of a chapter belongs to a project. Such a line can remove a whole chapter from your Board paper, or from your plans if you miss it.

Syllabus and textbook are not the same

The textbook may contain more than the syllabus. A chapter or section can be in the book and outside the syllabus for a given year. When the two differ, the syllabus decides what can be asked. See syllabus, textbook and sample paper.

A ten-minute routine

  1. Open the course structure and copy the unit table into your notebook.
  2. Mark the two or three largest units.
  3. Read the notes for conditions on chapters.
  4. Read how the internal or practical marks are split, and note what you must keep or submit.
  5. Look at the question paper design to see how many marks go to short, long and case-based questions.

Always check the official file. This page is a summary of the CBSE curriculum for 2026-27, made from the subject file on cbseacademic.nic.in, which we read on 12 November 2026. CBSE can revise a syllabus by circular during the year. Before an examination, download the current file from the official site. This is an independent site, not connected with CBSE.
