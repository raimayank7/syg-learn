About this syllabus

The English paper is divided by skill, not by chapter: reading, writing with grammar, and literature. Half of the paper, 40 marks, is on the literature textbooks, and the other half tests skills on material you have not seen before. Grammar is tested in context through gap filling, editing and transformation exercises. The file lists the prescribed lessons and poems of the textbooks; take the list for your year from the official file, because prescribed lessons are revised from time to time.

Units and chapters

  1. Section A: Reading Skills
    • Reading comprehension through unseen passages (two passages, 600 to 700 words in all)
  2. Section B: Writing Skills with Grammar
    • Grammar: determiners, tenses, modals, subject-verb concord, reported speech (10 marks)
    • Writing skills (10 marks)
  3. Section C: Language through Literature
    • Reference to the context (5 + 5 = 10 marks)
    • Short and very long answer questions (30 marks)

Where the marks are

The theory paper is 80 marks in 3 hours. The largest share goes to Section C: Language through Literature with 40 marks, which is 50% of the paper. The smallest is Section A: Reading Skills with 20 marks. The two largest entries in the table together carry 60 marks, 75% of the paper.

Using this to plan

  • Reading and writing are 40 marks that cannot be memorised. They reward regular practice with unseen passages and timed writing.
  • The grammar topics are few and are named in the file. Ten marks are available for five topics.
  • In literature, reference-to-context questions need you to know the lines; long answers need you to know the themes and characters.
