About this syllabus

The paper is in two equal halves of 40 marks: Part A, Introductory Macroeconomics, with five units, and Part B, Indian Economic Development, with three. Macroeconomics is numerical and built on definitions and formulas; Indian Economic Development is descriptive. A project carries the remaining 20 marks.

Where the marks are

The theory paper is 80 marks in 3 hours. The largest share goes to Part B: Current Challenges facing Indian Economy with 20 marks, which is 25% of the paper. The smallest is Part A: Money and Banking with 6 marks. The two largest entries in the table together carry 32 marks, 40% of the paper.

Using this to plan

  • Current Challenges facing Indian Economy is the largest single unit at 20 marks, a quarter of the paper.
  • In Part A, National Income and Determination of Income and Employment together are 22 of the 40 marks, and both need numerical practice.
  • The two parts are worth the same. Students who prefer numbers should not neglect Part B, nor the other way round.
