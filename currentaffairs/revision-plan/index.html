Current affairs revision plan: daily, weekly, monthly

Current affairs are forgotten quickly if they are only read once. This plan is our own and is meant to be adjusted.

WhenHow longWhat to do
Daily10 minutesReread yesterday's notes. Write today's three notes.
Weekly1 hourReread the week's notes by topic. Cover them and test yourself. Mark the ones you missed.
MonthlyHalf a dayReread the month's notes in one sitting. Take a test on the month. Cross out what you know.
Before the examLast two weeksReread only the marked notes and the last three months in full.

If you are short of time

Keep the daily ten minutes and the weekly hour. Skip the monthly half day, but still do the monthly test. Testing yourself helps more than rereading.

How far back to go

Check the exam's notice: some ask about the last year, some about a longer period. We do not know how far back any exam goes, so follow its own words.

Using the quiz

Our quiz tests only the UN list of days. Use it as a break, not as a substitute for revising the news.

An example week

DayWhat to do
Monday to FridayTen minutes each morning: reread the notes of yesterday and write three new ones.
SaturdayOne hour: reread the notes of the week by topic, cover them and test yourself.
SundayRest, or a light reread of the notes you marked as missed.

Common mistakes

  • Rereading without testing yourself.
  • Writing notes too long to revise.
  • Skipping the weekly hour because the daily ten minutes felt enough.
  • Starting the monthly revision only in the last month before the exam.

Measuring progress

After a month, take the notes of the first week and try to recall them with the page covered. If you recall most of them, the plan is working. If not, shorten the notes and test yourself more often.
