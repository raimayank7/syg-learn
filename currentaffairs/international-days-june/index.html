Reading this month's list

  • June has 33 entries, all of them single days.
  • The busiest date is 5 June, with 2 entries.
  • The date shown for a week is the first day of the week.

Who sets the days in June

The first entry of the month is on 1 June and the last on 30 June. 9 fall in the first ten days, 11 in the second ten and 13 in the last eleven or so, which tells you where the month is busy. The longest gap between two entries is 2 days, from 27 June to 29 June. The authorities named most often on the UN page for this month are WHO (1), FAO (1), IMO (1), STCW (1). An authority named beside a day is the UN body that coordinates it, such as a specialised agency, and it is a good place to look for that year’s theme. Many of these days have a new theme every year, so if a question is about a day, learn the date and the topic, and check the current theme from the official site just before the exam.

How to learn them (our own advice)

Do not try to memorise the whole list at once. Read this month's table once, say each date aloud with its name, then cover the date column and test yourself. Our quiz asks the same two questions: which date is a day on, and which day is on a date. Come back to the month you got most wrong.

What this list is not

It is a list of observances, not of news. Questions on current affairs usually ask about what happened this year around a day (a theme, a host country, a report), which a list cannot give. See how to follow current affairs.

Source: United Nations, "List of International Days and Weeks" (un.org/en/observances/list-days-weeks), read on 14 November 2026. The page lists observances with a fixed date; it does not list the days whose date moves from year to year, and it names the authority (a General Assembly resolution, or a UN agency such as WHO or FAO) behind each.
