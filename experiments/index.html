Safe science experiments to do at home

Household things, clear steps and a reason for what happens. An adult nearby for every one.

Adult supervision needed. An adult must be nearby for every experiment on this site, from start to finish. Children should never do the steps marked Adult alone. If anything feels unsafe, stop.

Choose an experiment

  • Baking Soda and Vinegar FizzWhat happens when baking soda meets vinegar?
  • Dancing RaisinsCan bubbles lift a raisin up a cup of fizzy water?
  • Floating Egg in Salt WaterCan salt make an egg float?
  • Walking WaterHow can water climb up a paper towel and cross to another cup?
  • Static Balloon and PaperCan a rubbed balloon pick up bits of paper without touching them?
  • Magnet HuntWhich things around the house does a magnet pick up?
  • Shadows Long and ShortHow does the height of a light change the length of a shadow?
  • Flat Paper and Paper BallWhich falls faster, a flat sheet of paper or the same sheet crumpled?
  • Ice Melting RaceDoes salt change how fast ice melts?
  • Sugar in Warm and Cold WaterDoes sugar dissolve faster in warm water than in cold water?
  • Rubber Band GuitarHow can a rubber band make high and low sounds?
  • Seed in a BagCan a seed grow without soil?
  • Mixing Paint ColoursWhich new colours can you make by mixing three paints?
  • Paper SpinnerWhy does a paper spinner twirl slowly to the ground?

How each page works

  1. What you need: a tick-box list, to find everything first.
  2. Predict: guess what will happen before you start.
  3. Steps: short numbered steps. A red Adult mark shows a step that an adult does.
  4. Stop if: the signs that mean you should stop.
  5. Why it works: a plain explanation.
  6. Try next: a question to explore.

Safety comes first

We use only household things that are safe when used as the steps say. Steps that need an adult are marked. We never mix household chemicals, use a flame, or use mains electricity. Read adult supervision and safety before you start, and how to run an experiment to get more from each one.

These experiments are our own writing, using only facts from school textbooks. They are not medical or safety certification, and an adult is responsible for judging whether an experiment is safe for a particular child, home and set of materials, including allergies.
