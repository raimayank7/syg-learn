Seed in a Bag: a safe home experiment

Can a seed grow without soil?

Adult supervision needed. An adult must be nearby for every experiment on this site, from start to finish. Children should never do the steps marked Adult alone. If anything feels unsafe, stop.
At a glance
TimeAbout 10 minutes
Adult neededNo step needs an adult alone, but an adult should be nearby for the whole experiment.
MessTidy
Look fora seed sprouting

What you need

Tick each thing as you find it. You can print this page.

  • dried bean or other seed
  • paper towel
  • clear zip bag
  • water
  • tape
  • pencil and paper

Predict first

Before you start, write down what you think will happen. Then you can see whether you were right. Scientists do this too.

Steps

  1. Fold a paper towel to fit inside the bag and wet it until it is damp but not dripping.
  2. Put the bean on the paper towel in the bag, and seal the bag.
  3. Tape the bag to a window, where it gets daylight but is not left in strong sun all day.
  4. Look at it every day for two weeks. Draw what you see, and write the date.
  5. When there is a root and a shoot, ask an adult to help plant it in a pot of soil.
Safety. Do not eat the seeds. Wash your hands after touching them. If you see fuzzy mould in the bag, throw the bag away without opening it and wash your hands.

Stop if…

  • Stop and throw the bag away if it smells bad or grows mould.
  • Stop if the bag leaks and the window sill becomes wet. Wipe it up.

Why it works

A seed has a small plant inside it and a store of food. When it gets water and the right warmth, it swells and begins to grow. First a root comes out, then a shoot. Because the seed has its own food, it can start without soil, but it needs soil or water and light later on.

Try next

Set up two bags, one in a dark cupboard and one at the window. Is there a difference after a week?
