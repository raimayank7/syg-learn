Sugar in Warm and Cold Water: a safe home experiment

Does sugar dissolve faster in warm water than in cold water?

Adult supervision needed. An adult must be nearby for every experiment on this site, from start to finish. Children should never do the steps marked Adult alone. If anything feels unsafe, stop.
At a glance
TimeAbout 20 minutes
Adult neededYes. Steps marked Adult need an adult to do them. An adult should still be nearby for the whole experiment.
MessWet: use a tray or work near a sink
Look forsugar vanishing

What you need

Tick each thing as you find it. You can print this page.

  • two clear plastic cups
  • sugar
  • spoon
  • cold tap water
  • warm water from the tap
  • clock or timer

Predict first

Before you start, write down what you think will happen. Then you can see whether you were right. Scientists do this too.

Steps

  1. AdultAn adult runs hot water from the tap until it is hot, mixes it with cold so it is comfortably warm to touch, and fills one cup. Never use a kettle or a pan.
  2. Fill the other cup with the same amount of cold tap water.
  3. Put one spoon of sugar into each cup and stir each one ten times at the same speed.
  4. Look at both cups. In which one has the sugar gone? Time how long it takes.
  5. Keep adding one spoon at a time to each cup, stirring ten times, until sugar stays at the bottom. Count the spoons for each cup.
Safety. Hot water can scald. Only an adult handles water that is hot. Do not drink anything that has been used in an experiment.

Stop if…

  • Stop at once if anyone is splashed by hot water. Run cool tap water over the place and tell an adult.
  • Stop if the cups are on a wobbly surface.

Why it works

Sugar dissolves when its tiny parts spread out among the water. In warm water the tiny parts of water move faster, so they pull the sugar apart more quickly. Usually more sugar dissolves in warm water than in cold water as well.

Try next

Does stirring faster help? Does a sugar cube dissolve more slowly than loose sugar? Why might that be?
