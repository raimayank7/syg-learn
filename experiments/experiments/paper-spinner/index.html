Paper Spinner: a safe home experiment

Why does a paper spinner twirl slowly to the ground?

Adult supervision needed. An adult must be nearby for every experiment on this site, from start to finish. Children should never do the steps marked Adult alone. If anything feels unsafe, stop.
At a glance
TimeAbout 25 minutes
Adult neededYes. Steps marked Adult need an adult to do them. An adult should still be nearby for the whole experiment.
MessTidy
Look fora spinning paper

What you need

Tick each thing as you find it. You can print this page.

  • sheet of paper
  • paper clip
  • scissors (for the adult)
  • ruler
  • pencil

Predict first

Before you start, write down what you think will happen. Then you can see whether you were right. Scientists do this too.

Steps

  1. AdultAn adult cuts a strip of paper about 5 cm wide and 20 cm long with scissors. Children should not use scissors without an adult.
  2. AdultWith the ruler, draw a line across the strip, about one-third from the top. An adult cuts along the line from the top down to it, to make two flaps.
  3. Fold the two flaps in opposite directions, one to the left and one to the right, to make wings.
  4. Fold the bottom of the strip up a little and slip the paper clip over it as a weight.
  5. Hold the spinner high at standing height and let it go. Watch it twirl. Time the fall and try again with a heavier or a less heavy clip.
Safety. Scissors are for adults only. Drop from standing height and never from stairs, windows or chairs. Keep paper clips away from small children.

Stop if…

  • Stop if anyone climbs up to drop the spinner from higher.
  • Stop if the cut edges are rough or the paper tears.

Why it works

Air pushes up against the folded wings as the spinner falls. Because the wings are tilted in opposite ways, the push makes the spinner turn round and round. The air also slows it down, so it falls more slowly than it would without wings.

Try next

Make the wings longer or shorter. Does a longer wing make it fall more slowly? Time each try.
