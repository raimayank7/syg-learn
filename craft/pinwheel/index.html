How to make a paper pinwheel

The pinwheel shows how moving air can turn a wheel, the same idea as a windmill. It needs an adult for the sharp parts, but the folding and decorating are a child's job.

  • Ages 6 to 12
  • About 25 min
  • Adult needed
  • 5 steps
An adult is needed. An adult must use the scissors and the pin. Please read all the steps before the child begins.

What you need

You needHow many
Square paper sheet1
Scissors1
Ruler1
Pencil with a rubber end1
Pin with a round head1

Steps

  1. Colour or decorate both sides of the square. Lay it flat and draw a line from each corner to the opposite corner with the ruler. The lines cross in the middle.
  2. An adult cuts along each line from the corner towards the middle and stops about two finger-widths before the centre.
  3. Take every second corner and bend it gently to the middle, without creasing it. You now have a windmill shape with four bent points.
  4. An adult pushes the round-headed pin through the four bent points and the centre, and then into the rubber end of the pencil, leaving a small gap so the pinwheel can spin.
  5. Hold the pencil and blow on the pinwheel, or hold it up in a light breeze, and watch it turn.

Tips

Put the pin in only at the end, and keep the pin away from small children. When you have finished, push the pin fully into the rubber so the point is covered. Do not run with the pinwheel near other children. Try different sizes of square and see which turns best.
