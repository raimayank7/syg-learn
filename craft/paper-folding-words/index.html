Paper folding words: crease, valley fold and mountain fold

Instructions for folding paper use a few short words. Once you know them, any set of steps is easier to follow. These are plain explanations in our own words.

WordWhat it means
CreaseThe sharp line you make when you fold paper and press it flat with a thumbnail. A good crease makes every later step easier.
Valley foldA fold where the paper bends toward you, so the crease makes a V like a valley.
Mountain foldA fold where the paper bends away from you, so the crease points up like a mountain.
DiagonalA line from one corner of a square to the opposite corner.
Centre lineA crease down the middle of the paper. Folding edges to the centre line is the first step of many crafts, such as the paper plane.
Concertina or accordion foldFolding back and forth in equal strips, like a fan or the bellows of an accordion.
Unfold or open outReturn the paper to flat, keeping the crease, so that you can see the guide lines.
FlattenPress a shape down so that it lies flat on the table, often to turn a pocket into a new shape.
Square paperA sheet with four equal sides. It is the usual start for folding games like the fortune teller.

Tips for folding well

Work on a flat, hard surface such as a table, not on your lap. Line up the edges or corners before you press, and crease from the middle outwards with a thumbnail or the side of a ruler. If a step does not work, undo it and check that the earlier creases are sharp. Most diagrams show the fold as a dotted line for a valley fold and a line of dashes and dots for a mountain fold, and an arrow for the direction in which the paper moves, so it helps to know the words above before you read them.

Which paper to use

Ordinary printer paper is fine for a first try. Thin square origami paper makes sharper creases, and very thick paper is hard to fold more than a few times. If you start with a rectangle, you can make a square by folding one corner to the opposite edge and cutting off the leftover strip.

Habits of a good folder

  • Fold on a hard, flat table.
  • Line up the edges before you press.
  • Press from the middle of the fold outward.
  • Read the whole step first, then fold.

Try it out

The paper fan uses the concertina fold, and the paper plane uses the centre line.
