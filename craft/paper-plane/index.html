How to make a paper plane

The dart is the plane most people learn first. It is made only by folding, so it needs nothing but a sheet of paper, and it teaches the most important habit in paper craft: make every crease sharp and every fold even.

  • Ages 5 to 12
  • About 10 min
  • No adult needed
  • 7 steps
A child can do this alone, but sit nearby for the first one.

What you need

You needHow many
A4 paper sheet1

Steps

  1. Lay the sheet on a table with a long side towards you. Fold it in half lengthways, press the crease hard with your thumbnail, then open it out again so you can see the centre line.
  2. Fold the two top corners in so that their edges lie along the centre line. The top of the paper now looks like the roof of a house.
  3. Fold the two slanted edges in again to meet the centre line. You now have a long, narrow point.
  4. Fold the whole plane in half along the centre line, with the folds on the outside.
  5. Fold one wing down so that its top edge lines up with the bottom edge of the body. Turn the plane over and fold the other wing the same way.
  6. Open the wings flat so that, seen from the front, they make a shallow V.
  7. Hold the plane under the body near the front and give it a gentle push forward. Never throw it at a person's face.

Tips

If the plane dives to the ground, bend the back edges of the wings up a very little. If it climbs and drops, bend them down a little. Fly it in a room with space or outdoors, away from windows and people. Try different paper, such as a page from a notebook and a page from a magazine, and see which flies further. Measure each flight with a tape or by counting steps.
