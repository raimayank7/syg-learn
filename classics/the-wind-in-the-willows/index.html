The Wind in the Willows: study guide

A shy Mole leaves his spring-cleaning to wander out into the sun and meets Water Rat, who lives by the river. Over 12 chapters the two become close friends and are drawn into the doings of the rich, boastful Mr. Toad, the stern and kindly Mr. Badger, and the dangerous Wild Wood. Some chapters are quiet scenes of friendship, home and the changing seasons; others are comic adventures with motor-cars, prisons and disguises. It is a book to be read slowly, one chapter at a time.

Main characters

  • Mole: The shy, house-proud animal who leaves his burrow and learns about the river.
  • Rat: Water Rat, who knows everything about the river and loves boats and poetry.
  • Toad: Mr. Toad of Toad Hall: rich, vain, boastful and reckless.
  • Badger: Mr. Badger, the gruff, solitary and wise animal of the Wild Wood.
  • Otter: A friendly river-bank animal whose small son goes missing.
  • Portly: Otter's small son, who strays from home.

Main themes

Friendship

Mole and Rat help each other through everything, and the book shows what friends owe each other, from a picnic to a rescue. Even Toad, who is vain and foolish, is not given up on.

Home

Mole's old burrow, Rat's riverside house and Badger's deep, warm home are all places the animals love. The book treats a home as something to cherish, and the return to it as the best of all adventures.

Freedom and settling down

Toad and the sea-going Rat both love the open road. Mole and Badger love home. The book lets both be good, and shows that each needs the other.

A line from the book

"simply messing about in boats" (chapter 1).
