The Time Machine: chapter-by-chapter summary

The book has 17 parts: sixteen numbered chapters and a short epilogue. The headings are as printed in the text we checked. Most of the story is told by the Time Traveller to his guests, and the narrator of the dinner party frames it.

Chapter 1: Introduction

The narrator describes a dinner at the Time Traveller's house where the host argues that time is a fourth dimension, one in which a person should be able to move as freely as in space. His guests, including Filby, find the argument fanciful. The host promises to show them something.

Chapter 2: The Machine

The Time Traveller shows his guests a small model of a time machine and, to their astonishment, makes it disappear by pressing a lever. He says that he is building a full-size one, and invites them to dinner the following week.

Chapter 3: The Time Traveller Returns

At the next dinner the host is not there. The guests wait, and the Time Traveller at last comes in dirty, tired and limping. After washing and eating, he begins to tell what has happened to him.

Chapter 4: Time Travelling

The Time Traveller describes finishing the machine and setting off on his first trip: nights and days flickering, the sun streaking across the sky, the house gone from around him. He stops suddenly, thrown from the saddle, in a storm of hail.

Chapter 5: In the Golden Age

He sees a white sphinx on a pedestal and a world of gardens and beautiful buildings. A group of small, delicate people come to meet him. They are friendly but childlike, and have no interest in his questions.

Chapter 6: The Sunset of Mankind

He tries to understand the world he has come to and invents a first theory: that human beings have become so comfortable that they no longer need to work or think. He sees that his theory may be wrong.

Chapter 7: A Sudden Shock

At night, he finds that his time machine has been taken from where he left it. He searches in vain and fears he may be left in this world for ever.

Chapter 8: Explanation

He wanders over the country, trying to understand the world, and saves a small woman named Weena from drowning in a river. She becomes his companion. He forms a second theory about the world.

Chapter 9: The Morlocks

He finds that the wells scattered about the land are ventilation shafts, and discovers the pale underground creatures, the Morlocks, who live below. He climbs down and learns that they are not as harmless as the Eloi. Weena is afraid of the dark.

Chapter 10: When Night Came

He realises that the Morlocks have his machine and that the Eloi fear them at night. He leaves for a distant building he calls the Palace of Green Porcelain, thinking he may find weapons.

Chapter 11: The Palace of Green Porcelain

The palace turns out to be a ruined museum. In its galleries he takes a bar of iron to use as a weapon, and finds some camphor and a box of matches that are still good. He sets out with Weena in the evening to return.

Chapter 12: In the Darkness

Night falls in the forest and the Morlocks close in. The fire spreads and Weena is lost. He escapes at dawn with only a few matches left.

Chapter 13: The Trap of the White Sphinx

He returns to the white sphinx and finds the bronze valves of its pedestal standing open. Inside, in a small room, is his Time Machine, and he still has the small levers in his pocket. It is a trap: the Morlocks close in, but he fits the levers and escapes.

Chapter 14: The Further Vision

He rides the machine forward in time and sees the world grow old. In the far future, the sun is large and red, and the land is silent, with huge crab-like creatures crawling on the shore. He turns back before the cold takes him.

Chapter 15: The Time Traveller's Return

He travels back to his own time and arrives in his own laboratory. He washes, comes in to his guests and finishes telling his story.

Chapter 16: After the Story

The guests do not know whether to believe him. The next day the narrator visits and sees the Time Traveller preparing for another journey. He promises to return in half an hour. He does not come back.

Chapter 17: Epilogue

The narrator is left wondering what became of the Time Traveller. He holds two strange white flowers that the Time Traveller brought back from the future.
