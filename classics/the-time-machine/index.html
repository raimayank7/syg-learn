The Time Machine: study guide

At a dinner party an inventor, whom the narrator calls the Time Traveller, explains that time is a fourth direction in which one can travel, and shows a small model of his machine. A week later he staggers in, filthy and exhausted, and tells his guests where he has been: to a far future, where he meets two very different kinds of descendants of humanity. The book has 17 parts, counting the short epilogue, and most of the story is told by the Time Traveller himself. It is among the first stories ever written about time travel, and it is short and quick to read.

Main characters

  • Time Traveller: The inventor who builds the machine and goes to the future.
  • Filby: An argumentative guest at the dinner, with red hair.
  • Medical Man: A guest who is the Time Traveller's doctor friend.
  • Weena: A small, gentle Eloi whom the Time Traveller saves from drowning and who then follows him.
  • Eloi: The small, graceful, childlike people who live above ground in the far future.
  • Morlocks: The pale creatures who live underground and keep the machinery going.

Main themes

Class and the future

The two peoples of the future grew apart from a divide between those who own and those who work. Wells uses the far future to say something about the society of his own day.

Ease and decline

The Eloi have no need to work or think, and they have become small, trusting and unable to look after themselves. The book suggests that a people who are never tested lose their strength.

Science and wonder

The story takes a scientific idea seriously, treating the time machine as a real machine and its traveller as a real observer. The result is part adventure and part thought experiment.

A line from the book

"The Time Traveller (for so it will be convenient to speak of him)" (chapter 1).
