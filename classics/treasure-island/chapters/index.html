Treasure Island: chapter-by-chapter summary

The book has 34 chapters, grouped by the author into six parts. The text we checked prints one heading (chapter 17) with a wrong Roman numeral, XXVII; we treat it as chapter 17 because it comes between chapters 16 and 18. Headings below are as printed.

Chapter 1: The Old Sea-dog at the Admiral Benbow

Jim Hawkins looks back on the day an old seaman came to his father's inn, the Admiral Benbow. The man says he is a captain, pays well, and asks Jim to watch for a seafaring man with one leg. He frightens the guests with songs and stories, and Jim's sick father is hurt by his noise.

Chapter 2: Black Dog Appears and Disappears

A stranger named Black Dog comes looking for the captain. The two fight with cutlasses in the inn and Black Dog runs away wounded. Afterwards the old seaman collapses, and the doctor warns him that he must stop drinking.

Chapter 3: The Black Spot

Jim's father dies. A blind beggar arrives and presses the Black Spot into the captain's hand. The captain dies of a fit, and Jim and his mother take the key to his sea-chest from his body.

Chapter 4: The Sea-chest

Jim and his mother open the chest and count out only what they are owed. Hearing the pirates come, they flee into the dark and hide by the bridge. Jim creeps back and takes an oilskin packet from the chest.

Chapter 5: The Last of the Blind Man

The pirates break into the inn and search in vain. Horsemen arrive from the revenue service, led by Mr. Dance, and the pirates scatter. Pew is trampled by the horses and killed, and Jim gives the packet to Dr. Livesey.

Chapter 6: The Captain's Papers

Dr. Livesey and Squire Trelawney open the packet and find a map of an island with the treasure of the pirate Flint marked on it. The squire decides to hire a ship and sail at once, with Jim as cabin boy.

Chapter 7: I Go to Bristol

The squire fits out the ship at Bristol and hires a cook, Long John Silver, who has one leg and a parrot. Jim goes to Bristol to join them, and the squire writes to the doctor about the preparations.

Chapter 8: At the Sign of the Spy-glass

Jim goes to Silver's inn to give him a message and finds it full of seamen. He sees a man he knows as Black Dog and gives chase; Silver pretends to know nothing of him. The doctor and squire hear the story and are pleased with the cook.

Chapter 9: Powder and Arms

On the ship, Captain Smollett complains that the crew know where they are going and that the squire has talked too much. He asks for the powder and arms to be moved near the cabin, and the men agree.

Chapter 10: The Voyage

The ship sails. The mate, Mr. Arrow, is found to be drunk and later lost overboard. Jim talks with Silver, who is friendly and shows him his parrot, and with the coxswain Israel Hands.

Chapter 11: What I Heard in the Apple-Barrel

Near the island, Jim goes to the apple barrel for an apple and falls asleep inside it. He wakes to hear Silver planning with some of the crew to take the ship and kill the honest men.

Chapter 12: Council of War

Jim tells the squire, the doctor and the captain what he overheard. They decide to keep quiet and wait, since the loyal men are outnumbered. They count those they can trust.

Chapter 13: How I Began My Shore Adventure

The island comes into sight. Silver seems pleased to be sent ashore. Jim steals into one of the boats and goes with the pirates to the beach, where he runs from them into the island.

Chapter 14: The First Blow

Hidden near the shore, Jim sees Silver argue with an honest sailor named Tom, who refuses to join the mutiny. Silver kills Tom, and Jim hears another honest sailor killed out of sight. Terrified, Jim runs deeper into the island.

Chapter 15: The Man of the Island

Running from the pirates, Jim meets a wild-looking man who turns out to be Ben Gunn, left on the island three years before by a pirate crew. Ben tells Jim he knows where the treasure is and will help the honest men.

Chapter 16: Narrative Continued by the Doctor: How the Ship Was Abandoned

Dr. Livesey takes over the story. Two boatloads of the crew have gone ashore from the Hispaniola, leaving six mutineers on board, and there is no wind to sail away. The loyal men decide to load a small boat with powder and food and slip away to the old stockade on the island. A sailor named Gray joins them, and they push off.

Chapter 17: Narrative Continued by the Doctor: The Jolly-boat's Last Trip

The fifth trip in the little boat is the worst: she is grossly overloaded, and the ship's big gun fires at them as they cross. They reach the shallows and wade ashore, leaving the boat and about half of the powder and food behind them.

Chapter 18: Narrative Continued by the Doctor: End of the First Day's Fighting

The loyal party hurries through the trees to the stockade with the pirates' voices close behind. They get inside and fight off the first rush, and the doctor tells of the dead and wounded on both sides. At the end of the chapter, Jim Hawkins comes climbing over the wall, safe and sound.

Chapter 19: Narrative Resumed by Jim Hawkins: The Garrison in the Stockade

Jim takes up the story again. He tells how, in the company of Ben Gunn, he approached the stockade and saw the British flag flying. He is welcomed by his friends, sleeps in the log house, and wakes in the morning to the news that Silver is outside with a flag of truce.

Chapter 20: Silver's Embassy

Silver comes to the stockade under a white flag and asks to speak to the captain. He offers terms for the map, and the captain refuses to bargain with mutineers. Silver leaves in a furious temper, and the loyal men get ready for an attack.

Chapter 21: The Attack

The captain scolds his men for leaving their posts, and the pirates attack from several sides at once. There is a fierce fight in and around the log house, and the attack is beaten back. The loyal party count their own loss and the pirates' too.

Chapter 22: How I Began My Sea Adventure

After the quiet that follows the fight, Jim decides on a plan of his own. He slips out of the stockade without a word to his friends and goes to find the little coracle that Ben Gunn has made, meaning to cut the ship loose from where she lies.

Chapter 23: The Ebb-tide Runs

Jim sets out in the coracle, which is hard to steer. The ebb-tide takes hold of him and carries him out toward the open sea, and he can do little but hold on. Worn out and frightened, he falls asleep dreaming of the old inn.

Chapter 24: The Cruise of the Coracle

Jim wakes to find himself off the south-west end of the island, below the great hill called the Spy-glass. He sees the Hispaniola drifting about with no one at the helm and, as she bears down, scrambles aboard just as she strikes and wrecks his little boat.

Chapter 25: I Strike the Jolly Roger

Jim finds the schooner almost empty: one dead pirate and one badly wounded one, Israel Hands, lie on the deck. Jim hauls down the pirate flag and throws it into the sea. Hands offers to help him sail the ship into a safe bay, and Jim, who has no other choice, agrees, while keeping a watch on the man.

Chapter 26: Israel Hands

Jim and Hands steer the ship toward the north of the island. When the moment is right, Hands tries to kill Jim, who climbs the mast to escape him. Hands is shot and falls into the water.

Chapter 27: "Pieces of Eight"

After the ship is aground, Jim sets off for the stockade in the dark. He enters expecting his friends, finds the pirates inside, and is caught. Silver's parrot screams at him, and Silver calls for a torch.

Chapter 28: In the Enemy's Camp

By torchlight Jim sees that the pirates hold the stockade and that there is no sign of his friends, and he fears the worst. Silver, who seems to be hedging his bets, quietly takes Jim's part against the angrier men.

Chapter 29: The Black Spot Again

In their council, the pirates give Silver the Black Spot to show they have lost faith in him. Silver talks his way back into command, and Jim learns how he has been playing both sides.

Chapter 30: On Parole

Dr. Livesey arrives in the morning to tend the pirates' wounded, and speaks briefly to Jim through the fence. He offers Jim the chance to leave with him, but Jim has given his word to stay, and keeps it.

Chapter 31: The Treasure-hunt--Flint's Pointer

The party sets out to find the treasure with the map and Jim tied to Silver by a rope. They come across the skeleton of a man, laid out so that his arms point in one direction, and the pirates take it as a sign from Flint.

Chapter 32: The Treasure-hunt--The Voice Among the Trees

As they go on, a voice from among the trees sings out, and the pirates freeze in fear. They go on and reach the place on the map, where they find only an empty hole with the broken boards of a chest. The treasure has been taken.

Chapter 33: The Fall of a Chieftain

The pirates turn on Silver in anger. Dr. Livesey, Ben Gunn and Gray arrive and fire on them, and the mutineers scatter. Silver changes sides at once, and goes back with the loyal party.

Chapter 34: And Last

The loyal party carry the treasure from Ben Gunn's cave to the ship. They sail for home, taking Silver as a prisoner. He slips away with some of the coin at the first port they reach. Everyone who is left gets a share.
