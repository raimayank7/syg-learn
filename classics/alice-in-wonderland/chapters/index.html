Alice's Adventures in Wonderland: chapter-by-chapter summary

The book has 12 chapters. Each one is a separate scene in Wonderland, so you can read them in order or dip into one. The headings below are exactly as they appear in the text we checked.

Chapter 1: Down the Rabbit-Hole

Alice is bored on the river bank when a White Rabbit hurries past, talking to himself about being late. She follows him down a rabbit-hole and falls for a long time. She lands in a hall of locked doors, finds a tiny key and a small door to a lovely garden, and drinks from a bottle marked DRINK ME so that she shrinks small enough to fit.

Chapter 2: The Pool of Tears

Alice eats a cake and grows so tall that she cannot reach the garden. She cries a pool of tears, then shrinks again and swims in it. There she meets a Mouse, whom she upsets by talking about her cat, and a crowd of other animals falls into the water too.

Chapter 3: A Caucus-Race and a Long Tale

The wet animals gather on the bank and need to dry off. The Dodo suggests a Caucus-race, in which everyone runs round and round and everyone wins. The Mouse tells his long tale, and the animals leave one by one when Alice again mentions Dinah.

Chapter 4: The Rabbit Sends in a Little Bill

The White Rabbit mistakes Alice for his housemaid and sends her to fetch his gloves. In his house she drinks from another bottle, grows until she fills the room, and is stuck. The animals outside throw pebbles that turn into little cakes; she eats one, shrinks, and runs out into the wood.

Chapter 5: Advice from a Caterpillar

A Caterpillar sits on a mushroom and asks Alice, again and again, who she is. He tells her that one side of the mushroom makes her taller and the other shorter. She nibbles too much, her neck grows long, and a pigeon takes her for a serpent. Finally she gets back to a size she likes.

Chapter 6: Pig and Pepper

Alice reaches the house of the Duchess, where the cook throws pans and the air is full of pepper. The baby the Duchess holds turns into a pig in Alice's arms, and Alice lets it go into the wood. The Cheshire Cat appears in a tree, tells her everyone there is mad, and fades away leaving only its grin.

Chapter 7: A Mad Tea-Party

Alice finds the March Hare, the Hatter and a sleepy Dormouse at a long table with too many cups. It is always six o'clock for them, so it is always tea-time. They ask riddles with no answers and talk rudely to Alice. She walks away and finds a door in a tree that leads back to the hall of doors.

Chapter 8: The Queen's Croquet-Ground

Alice goes through the little door into the garden. Gardeners are painting white roses red because they planted the wrong ones, and soon the Queen of Hearts arrives with the King. The Queen calls for heads to be cut off for the smallest thing. Alice joins a game of croquet played with flamingoes and hedgehogs, and the Cheshire Cat's head appears in the air.

Chapter 9: The Mock Turtle's Story

The Duchess, now friendly, talks in morals. The Queen sends her away, and a Gryphon takes Alice to meet the Mock Turtle, who weeps as he tells how he once went to school under the sea and learned Reeling and Writhing.

Chapter 10: The Lobster Quadrille

The Mock Turtle and Gryphon show Alice the Lobster Quadrille, a dance on the beach. Alice tries to recite a poem, but the words come out wrong. The Mock Turtle sings of Turtle Soup, and they are interrupted by a cry that a trial is about to begin.

Chapter 11: Who Stole the Tarts?

The King and Queen sit in court with a jury of animals. The Knave of Hearts stands accused of stealing the Queen's tarts. The White Rabbit reads the charge, the Hatter gives nervous evidence, and Alice finds that she has begun to grow larger again.

Chapter 12: Alice's Evidence

Alice is called as a witness and upsets the jury box by standing up too quickly. The King's rules and the Queen's cry of sentence first, verdict afterwards are too much for her; she says they are only a pack of cards, and the whole pack flies up at her. She wakes on the river bank with her sister, who tells her to go in to tea.
