The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde: chapter-by-chapter summary

The book has 10 short chapters. The first eight are told by an outside narrator who stays close to Mr. Utterson; the last two are written statements by two of the people in the story. The headings below are as printed in the text we checked.

Chapter 1: Story of the Door

Utterson and his relative Enfield pass a shabby door in a smart London street, and Enfield tells how he once saw a man named Hyde trample a girl and pay her family with a cheque from a respected name. The cheque led back to this door. Utterson, who knows the cheque's signer, says nothing but is troubled.

Chapter 2: Search for Mr. Hyde

Utterson takes out the will of his friend Dr. Jekyll, which leaves everything to Edward Hyde if Jekyll disappears. He asks Dr. Lanyon about Hyde and learns nothing, then begins to watch the door at night. He meets Hyde at last and finds him small, pale and hateful to look at.

Chapter 3: Dr. Jekyll Was Quite at Ease

At a dinner party, Utterson raises the matter of the will with Jekyll. Jekyll says he can be rid of Hyde whenever he likes and begs Utterson to leave it alone and to promise to see Hyde's rights respected if anything happens.

Chapter 4: The Carew Murder Case

Nearly a year later a maid sees, from her window, Mr. Hyde beat Sir Danvers Carew to death with a cane. Utterson recognises the cane as one he gave Jekyll. He leads a police officer to Hyde's rooms in Soho, which are empty and ransacked, and the murderer is not found.

Chapter 5: Incident of the Letter

Jekyll tells Utterson that he is done with Hyde and shows him a letter said to be from Hyde. Utterson's clerk Mr. Guest compares the handwriting with Jekyll's own and finds them alike. Utterson begins to fear that Jekyll has forged the letter to protect a murderer.

Chapter 6: Incident of Dr. Lanyon

Lanyon grows ill and dies after a shock he will not explain. Before he dies he leaves Utterson a sealed letter, to be opened only after Jekyll's death or disappearance. Utterson is left to wonder what has happened to both friends.

Chapter 7: Incident at the Window

Utterson and Enfield walk past Jekyll's house and see him at a window. Jekyll starts to talk, then his face changes with a look of terror, and the window is shut. The two men leave in horror.

Chapter 8: The Last Night

Poole, Jekyll's butler, comes to Utterson in fear, saying that the voice in the doctor's cabinet is not his master's. The two men break down the door. They find Hyde dead, in Jekyll's clothes, and a will and a letter for Utterson, but no sign of Jekyll.

Chapter 9: Dr. Lanyon's Narrative

Utterson reads Lanyon's letter. Lanyon tells how Jekyll sent him to fetch chemicals from his cabinet and then to wait for a messenger. When Hyde arrived, mixed the drug and drank it, Lanyon saw him change into Jekyll before his eyes. The shock killed him.

Chapter 10: Henry Jekyll's Full Statement of the Case

Jekyll's own account explains everything. He made a drug that separated his bad side from his good, and he enjoyed being Hyde. Over time Hyde grew stronger and began to take over without the drug. When he can no longer make the drug, Jekyll writes his statement, knowing the end has come.
