The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde: study guide

A London lawyer, Mr. Utterson, hears a disturbing story about a small, hateful man called Hyde who trampled a child and then paid for it with a cheque signed by a respected name. When he learns that his friend Dr. Jekyll has left everything to Hyde, he sets out to find the truth. The tale runs through 10 short chapters, told mostly from the lawyer's side, and the full answer only arrives in two letters at the end. It is short enough to read in an evening, and the idea at its centre is still used everywhere today.

Main characters

  • Utterson: The lawyer and friend of Jekyll, through whose eyes most of the story is seen.
  • Enfield: Utterson's distant relative, who tells the first story about Hyde.
  • Jekyll: A respected doctor and scientist, well liked, with a secret.
  • Hyde: A small, ugly man who gives everyone who sees him a feeling of dislike.
  • Lanyon: A doctor and old friend of Jekyll who has quarrelled with him over his science.
  • Poole: Jekyll's trusted butler.
  • Carew: Sir Danvers Carew, a gentle old member of Parliament who is killed in the street.
  • Guest: Utterson's clerk, who knows handwriting.

Main themes

Two sides of one person

The central idea is that one man may hold a good side and a bad side, and that separating them does not remove the bad one. The story's most quoted line says that man is not truly one, but truly two.

Reputation and secrecy

Jekyll, Utterson and their friends care a great deal about being respectable. The story shows what people will hide, and what that hiding costs.

Science without limits

Jekyll's experiments begin as a search for knowledge and become something he cannot stop. The story asks who is responsible when a discovery goes wrong.

A line from the book

"man is not truly one, but truly two" (chapter 10).
