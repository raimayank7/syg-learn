The Call of the Wild: study guide

Buck is a large, well-fed dog living in comfort in California when he is stolen and sold as a sled dog to the Yukon gold rush. In 7 short chapters he learns the rules of a harsh new life, wins the leadership of the sled team in a fight to the death, survives cruel owners, and finds one kind master. When that master is lost, the wild itself calls to him. It is a fast, direct story told almost entirely from the dog's side.

Main characters

  • Buck: The large dog who is the hero and whose thoughts we follow.
  • Manuel: The gardener's helper who steals Buck and sells him.
  • Curly: A friendly dog who is killed by the huskies soon after arriving in the north.
  • Spitz: The white lead dog who is Buck's rival.
  • Perrault: A French-Canadian courier who buys Buck for the mail teams.
  • Thornton: John Thornton, the kind man who saves Buck and whom Buck loves.
  • Hal: A careless young man who drives the team to ruin.
  • Mercedes: Hal's sister, who travels with the party.

Main themes

Civilization and the wild

Buck begins as a pampered house dog and gradually becomes a wild animal, as the old instincts in him wake. The book asks how much of any creature is learned and how much is inherited.

Survival and strength

In the north, only the dogs and men who are tough and clever stay alive. Buck succeeds because he watches, learns, and adapts.

Love and loyalty

Buck's bond with one kind man is the only thing that holds him back from the wild. When it breaks, nothing stops him.

A line from the book

"Old longings nomadic leap" (chapter 1).
