AND, OR, NOT, IN and BETWEEN

One condition is often not enough. You want the stationery that costs under 100 rupees, or the customers from Delhi or Mumbai. This lesson joins conditions together and then shows two shortcuts, IN and BETWEEN.

AND: both must be true

SELECT name, category, price
FROM products
WHERE category = 'Stationery' AND price < 100;
Try it

Result

namecategoryprice
Notebook 200 pagesStationery60
Ball Pen pack of 10Stationery90

2 rows

A row is returned only if it passes both tests. Each AND you add makes the result smaller or leaves it the same.

OR: at least one must be true

SELECT name, city
FROM customers
WHERE city = 'Delhi' OR city = 'Mumbai';
Try it

Result

namecity
Ravi KumarDelhi
Kabir KhanMumbai
Imran AliDelhi
Joy ThomasMumbai

4 rows

A row is returned if it passes either test. Each OR you add makes the result larger or leaves it the same.

Each side of an OR must be a complete condition. WHERE city = 'Delhi' OR 'Mumbai' is wrong, though it may not give an error. Write the column name again on each side.

NOT: the opposite

NOT turns a condition around.

SELECT name, category
FROM products
WHERE NOT category = 'Grocery';

Mixing AND and OR: use brackets

When a condition has both AND and OR, the database does the AND parts first, the same way multiplication comes before addition in arithmetic. That can give a result you did not intend.

Suppose you want products in Grocery or Home that cost more than 400 rupees.

SELECT name, category, price
FROM products
WHERE category = 'Grocery' OR category = 'Home' AND price > 400;
Try it

Result

Basmati Rice 5 kgGrocery650
Toor Dal 1 kgGrocery160

Look at the result. Cheap groceries are in it. The database read the condition as "Grocery, or (Home and over 400)". Brackets fix it.

SELECT name, category, price
FROM products
WHERE (category = 'Grocery' OR category = 'Home') AND price > 400;
Try it

Result

namecategoryprice
Basmati Rice 5 kgGrocery650

Bedsheet DoubleHome899

4 rows

Make it a rule: whenever AND and OR appear together, add brackets, even where they are not strictly needed. The reader, and you in a month, will not have to think about it.

IN: one of a list

A chain of OR tests on the same column is long to write. IN does the same with a list.

SELECT name, city
FROM customers
WHERE city IN ('Delhi', 'Mumbai', 'Pune');

BETWEEN: inside a range

BETWEEN tests whether a value lies in a range. Both ends are included.

SELECT name, price
FROM products
WHERE price BETWEEN 100 AND 300;
Try it

Result

nameprice
Toor Dal 1 kg160
Sunflower Oil 1 L145
Tea 500 g280
Geometry Box120
LED Bulb 9 W110

5 rows

This is the same as price >= 100 AND price <= 300. The smaller value must come first. BETWEEN 300 AND 100 returns nothing.

It works well with dates. These are the orders placed in the first three months of 2026.

SELECT id, order_date
FROM orders
WHERE order_date BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-03-31';
Try it

Result

12026-01-05
22026-01-12
32026-01-20
42026-02-02
52026-02-14
62026-02-28
72026-03-03
82026-03-15
92026-03-22

9 rows

A warning for later: if a column stores a time of day as well as a date, BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-03-31' misses almost all of the last day, because 31 March at 10 in the morning is later than 31 March at midnight. For such columns, write >= '2026-01-01' AND < '2026-04-01'. Our practice database stores plain dates, so BETWEEN is safe here.

NOT BETWEEN gives the rows outside the range.

SELECT name, salary
FROM employees
WHERE salary NOT BETWEEN 35000 AND 60000;

Result

Putting it together

Here is a realistic question: which delivered or shipped orders were placed from April to June 2026?

SELECT id, order_date, status
FROM orders
WHERE status IN ('delivered', 'shipped')
  AND order_date BETWEEN '2026-04-01' AND '2026-06-30';

When a condition gets long, put each part on its own line, starting with AND or OR. It reads like a checklist.
