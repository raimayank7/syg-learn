Tracing Sheets

Number tracing sheets: digits 0 to 9 and 1 to 20

Choose 0 to 9 or 1 to 20, the size, and print the sheet.

How to use it

  1. Choose 0 to 9, or 1 to 20.
  2. Choose the size and how many dotted copies are in a row.
  3. Press Print the sheet.
  4. Say each number aloud as the child traces it.

How the digits are formed

Children learn digits best when they always start in the same place and move in the same direction. A common way of teaching is: 1 is a single straight line down; 4, 7 and 5 are made of straight lines; 0, 6, 8 and 9 are curves that begin at the top and turn anticlockwise; 2 and 3 are curves that begin at the top and go clockwise before finishing with a line or a second curve. Schools differ, so follow the stroke order your child’s school uses. Sit beside the child and say the movement aloud, for example, “down, curve, across”, as the pencil moves.

Which version to choose

Choose 0 to 9 for a child who is learning digits for the first time. Choose 1 to 20 once the child can write the single digits and is ready to see how larger numbers are made of two. Make the size bigger for a younger child, whose hand is not yet steady, and smaller as control improves. A few dotted copies in a row is enough for a short session; the aim is a few well-formed numbers, not a full page.

Making it meaningful

  • Count real things as you trace: three spoons for 3.
  • Trace in the air with a finger first.
  • Keep the sheet in a plastic pocket and use a washable marker so that it can be used again.
